# L25. "Vegans treat vegetarians as enemies"

**Thesis:** hostility toward vegetarians and toward people who cut down exists in part of the movement, and it's a human and strategic mistake. But it isn't the vegan position. The vegan criticism of vegetarianism is aimed at milk and egg production, which kills male calves, male chicks and the cows and hens themselves once their output drops (B5), not at people. Vegetarians are, in general, allies on the way. And some vegans' bad manners don't change what happens to those animals.

**Family:** L. Attacks on veganism and vegans · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "To a vegan, a vegetarian is worse than a meat eater. Not even people who cut down escape the policing. You fight each other more than you fight the industry."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Vegetarians and people who reduce their consumption are already making a real effort for animals.
2. **P2.** Some vegans respond to that effort with reproach and contempt.
3. **P3.** A movement that is hostile to its closest allies is sectarian and counterproductive.
4. **C.** So veganism deserves neither support nor attention.

P1 is true, and P2 describes something that happens. P3 is a reasonable criticism of strategy. The problem lies in the conclusion: the behavior of some vegans says nothing about whether it's fair to use and kill animals.

The argument is **invalid**: it moves from a flaw in some of the defenders to rejecting the cause.

## 3. Answer for children

Imagine you've started tidying your room and someone complains because the bookshelf is still left. It makes you want to give up, and complaining like that doesn't help. But the bookshelf is still messy. The right way is to say thanks for what's been done and help with what's left. It's similar with animals: someone who stopped eating meat has already done something good, and there's still a way to help the cows and the hens.

## 4. Short answer

Some vegans do that, and they're wrong: someone who gave up meat or is cutting down deserves support, not policing. But it's worth separating the person from the product. The vegan criticism is aimed at milk and egg production, because in it male chicks are killed at hatching, male calves are discarded and cows and hens are slaughtered when they yield less. That stays true even when it's said rudely. Vegetarians aren't enemies: they're the people closest to understanding this point.

**Question to ask back:** "If a vegan explained this to you respectfully, would the information about milk and eggs stop being valid?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument mixes criticism of a practice with an attack on people:

| What some vegans do | What the vegan position says | Difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reproach and look down on vegetarians | Milk and eggs also cause suffering and death | The right target is production, not the consumer |
| Treat reduction as nothing | Reducing is better than not reducing, and a harm still remains | You can acknowledge the step and talk about what's left |
| Argue about purity among themselves | The focus is the animals | Infighting distracts from the subject |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. It's an attack on the messenger.** As in L1, the speaker's tone replaces an assessment of what is said. A rude vegan can still be right about milk.

**2. The criticism is of milk and eggs, and it has a basis.** In egg production, males of laying breeds are no use for meat and are killed at hatching: Faunalytics estimated 7.83 billion in 2024. Hens are slaughtered when laying drops. In dairy, the cow has to give birth to produce milk, male calves have little value and cows go to slaughter when they yield less. B5 covers this in detail.

**3. The hostility exists, but the research doesn't show the picture the phrase paints.** A 2014 study by the psychologist Hank Rothgerber, with 431 vegetarians and vegans, found tension within the group, but what weighed most was motive, not diet: ethical vegetarians rated health vegetarians worse than they rated vegans, and ethical vegans rated ethical vegetarians better than health vegetarians. The study didn't test the idea that vegans prefer meat eaters to vegetarians. The sample was made up of volunteers recruited online and doesn't represent all vegans.

**4. Being hostile to reducers misses the target.** In the Faunalytics survey of American adults, 84% of those who adopted a vegetarian or vegan diet gave it up; among those who gave up, 63% disliked standing out because of their diet and 84% didn't take part in any group. In a later study by the same organization, which followed 222 people for six months, believing that society views the diet negatively was associated with more abandonment, with a small effect. A welcome makes a difference, and policing pulls the other way.

**5. Those who try and give up don't become opponents.** In the same survey, 37% of former vegetarians and vegans said they were interested in going back, and they ate less meat than the population average. These are people who have already agreed with the essentials.

**6. Some vegans' failings are no one's excuse.** If a person already recognizes that animals matter, third parties' bad temper doesn't change what they themselves can do (K7).

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: a sectarian movement goes nowhere

The most serious version says: this isn't about hurt feelings. Movements that spend their energy policing the purity of those closest to them shrink. If even someone who gave up meat is met with a sermon, the practical message is "nothing you do is enough", and the rational response to that is to do nothing. A movement that wants to change the world should celebrate every step.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **As a criticism of strategy, it's good.** Many animal advocates agree, and so does this guide: welcoming reducers and vegetarians is fairer and works better.
2. **Celebrating the step doesn't require hiding what's left.** You can say "it's great that you gave up meat" and, respectfully, explain what happens to the chicks and the calves. Leaving that out would be treating the person as unable to hear it.
3. **"Nothing is enough" isn't the vegan position.** The position is that every animal spared counts, and that there are more animals to spare (J7).
4. **Bad strategy doesn't change the ethics.** A badly run movement can still be right. The consistent response is to do better, not to give up on the animals.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The quarrel between vegans and vegetarians is a matter among humans. The dairy cow and the laying hen gain nothing from it, nor from someone's decision to ignore the cause because of it. What matters is what happens to these animals: is it fair to make them suffer and die for milk and eggs when we don't need to? Those who have already given up meat for them are closer than anyone to answering.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in L25 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Ad hominem | Rejects the criticism of milk and eggs because of the tone of whoever makes it |
| Logical | Hasty generalization | Judges all vegans by the most hostile ones |
| Logical | Straw man | Attributes to veganism the thesis that vegetarians are worse than meat eaters |
| Epistemological | Confusing criticism of a practice with an attack on the person | Hears "milk causes harm" as "you are bad" |
| Moral | Changing the subject | Relations among humans take the place of what happens to cows and hens |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Criticizes the division and uses it.** Someone who says vegans should welcome allies uses the quarrel as a reason not to be one.
2. **Accepts criticism of practices on other subjects.** Hearing that a habit causes harm isn't usually taken as a personal insult.
3. **If reducing is good, reducing more is better.** The same value that justifies the step taken points to the next one.
4. **Judges the group by a few.** And wouldn't want vegetarians to be judged by the most inconsistent among them.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains L25 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do-gooder derogation | Those who feel judged by someone doing more tend to disparage them | [Minson and Monin, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550611415695) |
| Stereotype | A few hostile vegans become the portrait of all | Linked to L1 |
| Availability bias | One telling-off is remembered more than many calm conversations | General literature on heuristics |
| Moral licensing | The step already taken seems to excuse the next one | Linked to J7 |
| Psychological reactance | Being reproached creates the urge to do the opposite | Linked to L27 |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if part of a movement treats its allies badly, the movement's cause can be ignored".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Environmentalists who criticize people who only recycle | The environmental crisis could be ignored | No one concludes this |
| Political movements with internal quarrels | Their demands would lose their value | Every movement has internal quarrels |
| Someone who criticizes a smoker who cut down | Smoking the rest would be harmless | The remaining harm doesn't depend on the critic's tone |
| Vegans | Milk and eggs would stop causing harm | The same error |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's right to point out a real flaw. There are vegans who treat vegetarians and reducers with contempt, and that's unfair to people who have already changed their lives for animals. It's also bad strategy: it drives away people who are close and spends energy that should go to the animals. The criticism of milk and egg production can and should be made without humiliating anyone, and acknowledging each step is part of doing that well.

But it's right about the manner, not the content. Male chicks and calves go on dying whatever the tone of the person telling you. The best use of this criticism is to demand more respect from vegans, and to listen to what they have to say about milk and eggs.
