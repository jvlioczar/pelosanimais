# L19. "There are sexist, racist and transphobic vegans"

**Thesis:** there are, and that deserves criticism. Being vegan doesn't vaccinate anyone against prejudice, and the animal movement has documented cases of harassment and exclusion. But a person's failing in another cause doesn't change what happens to animals: it's an attack on the messenger, a relative of L4. The same reasoning would bring down any cause, because no movement is made of people who are consistent in everything. Consistency across causes is a good ideal, and those who demand it of vegans should apply it to themselves.

**Family:** L. Attacks on veganism and vegans · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "You defend animals and mistreat people. There are sexist, racist and transphobic vegans. Put your own house in order first, then come and talk about morals."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Whoever defends one cause of justice should be consistent with the other causes of justice.
2. **P2.** There are vegans who are sexist, racist or transphobic, and animal advocacy organizations with cases of harassment and exclusion.
3. **P3.** A movement that tolerates these inconsistencies has no moral authority.
4. **C.** So what vegans say about animals needn't be taken seriously.

P1 is a good ideal and P2 is true. The problem lies in the step from P3 to the conclusion: the moral authority of the speaker and the truth of what is said are different things.

The argument is **invalid**: nothing about animals follows from the inconsistency of some vegans.

## 2. How it can show up

| Context | Typical form |

## 3. Answer for children

Imagine a classmate who tells the teacher that someone is hurting a kitten, but who is sometimes rude to others. Being rude is wrong, and he needs to do better. But the kitten still needs help. A person can be right about one thing and wrong about another, and we need to look at each thing separately.

## 4. Short answer

There are, and I'll criticize them along with you: sexism, racism and transphobia are wrong in anyone, vegan or not. But notice that this says nothing about animals. If a prejudiced doctor says smoking is harmful, he's still right about cigarettes. And if the rule is "only those consistent in every cause may speak", no one speaks about anything. The best thing is to want both: respect for people and respect for animals.

**Question to ask back:** "If every vegan were exemplary in everything, what would change for the animal in the slaughterhouse?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

Three different questions are mixed up in the phrase:

| Question | About what | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Are there prejudiced vegans? | About people | Yes, and they should be criticized |
| Should the movement be consistent with other causes? | About the movement | Yes, it's a good ideal |
| Is it fair to make animals suffer and die without need? | About animals | The two answers above don't change this one |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. It's an attack on the messenger.** As in L4, the speaker's conduct is used to dismiss what is said. L4 deals with inconsistency in consumption; here the inconsistency is with other causes. The structure is the same: tu quoque.

**2. The true part should be admitted plainly.** In 2018, the head of the Humane Society of the United States, one of the country's largest animal protection organizations, resigned after accusations of sexual harassment. A 2022 study commissioned by the organization Encompass described the leadership of US farmed animal protection groups as mostly white and male, and gathered reports of exclusion from Black, Indigenous and other racialized advocates. And a 2013 experiment found that animal advocacy ads using sexualized images of women reduce support for the cause.

**3. On average, the data point the opposite way from the stereotype.** In a 2015 German survey of 1,381 people, meat eaters had higher average scores for authoritarianism, social dominance orientation and predisposition to prejudice than vegetarians and vegans. A 2014 Canadian study found that this same dominance orientation links speciesism to prejudice against ethnic groups. These are correlations in specific samples: they describe averages and say nothing about any particular vegan.

**4. The argument would bring down every cause.** There was racism in the movement for women's suffrage, and sexism in trade unions and civil rights movements. None of that made it false that women should vote or that workers have rights. If the inconsistency of some members cancelled a cause, no cause would be left.

**5. The demand for consistency applies to whoever makes it.** If those who defend one cause should respect the others, those who fight sexism, racism and transphobia should also ask what they owe animals. Consistency across causes runs both ways.

**6. There's no need to choose.** Not eating animals requires no prejudice and takes no time from any other struggle (J6). Some people defend both together, and the existence of prejudiced vegans stops no one from doing better than they do.

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: it's not a personal attack, it's criticism of a badly built cause

The most serious version says: the point isn't to discredit people. A movement that talks about liberation and reproduces sexism and racism shows that its analysis is shallow, drives away precisely those who already suffer oppression, and loses allies. A cause that wants to be universal has to be safe for everyone. And in practice people judge an idea partly by who carries it.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **It's a fair criticism of the movement, and it should be welcomed.** Animal advocacy organizations should be safe places, and campaigns shouldn't humiliate anyone. Fixing this is the movement's duty.
2. **It asks for a better veganism, not a return to the plate of meat.** The consistent conclusion is "defend animals without prejudice", not "so the animals can wait".
3. **Judging the idea by its bearer is a shortcut, not an argument.** Research on hypocrisy suggests we reject hypocrites because their condemnations signal a virtue they lack. That explains the dislike; it doesn't settle who is right about animals.
4. **The demand for purity is applied to one side only.** No one stops listening to medicine because there are prejudiced doctors.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Animals didn't choose who speaks for them. A pig on a truck is no better or worse off because one of its defenders has deplorable opinions about other people. Criticizing those opinions is right and necessary. Once they've been criticized, the question is still waiting: is it fair to make those who feel suffer and die when we don't need to?

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in L19 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Tu quoque | Uses the vegan's failing in another cause to reject what they say about animals |
| Logical | Ad hominem | Assesses the person in place of the argument |
| Logical | Hasty generalization | Judges all vegans by the worst ones |
| Logical | Guilt by association | The cause is tainted by some of its defenders |
| Moral | Changing the subject | The messenger's consistency takes the place of what happens to animals |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Doesn't apply the rule to their own causes.** Every cause has inconsistent defenders, and those who use the argument don't abandon theirs because of it.
2. **Demands consistency across causes and leaves animals out.** If justice is one, they belong in it too.
3. **The prejudice is of interest only as ammunition.** Often the criticism of the sexist vegan comes with no other action against sexism.
4. **Accepts truths told by flawed people.** In science, in politics and in the family.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if some defenders of a cause fail in other causes, the cause can be ignored".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Women's suffrage | Could be ignored, because there were racist suffragists | No one concludes this |
| The labor movement | Could be ignored, because there was sexism in unions | The right criticism was to change the unions |
| Environmentalism | Could be ignored, because there are prejudiced environmentalists | The climate doesn't depend on anyone's character |
| The defense of animals | Could be ignored, because there are prejudiced vegans | The same error |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It gets the facts and one value right. There are sexist, racist and transphobic vegans, and there are organizations that treated the people working in them badly. That causes real harm and deserves criticism, including from inside. It's also right that justice doesn't sit well with selectivity: whoever asks for consideration for all who feel should be the first to respect all people.

But that's where it stops being right. The failing of someone who defends animals is a reason to hold that person to account, not to take the debt out on the animals. And the ideal of consistency, taken seriously, asks for more consideration from every side, including from whoever raised the objection.
