# B6. "Veal makes use of the calves born in dairy farming"

**Thesis:** veal doesn't make use of a calf who would be born anyway: it is part of the system that causes these calves to be born. To give milk, a cow has to give birth every year, and the males are of no use for milk production. So they are killed at a few days or weeks old, or raised for a few months to become veal. In New Zealand, about 1.8 million of these calves were slaughtered in a single year, many at 4 days old. "Making use" of the calf just gives a destination to a victim that milk itself creates. And the calf isn't the only one: in most systems, above all industrial ones, the mother goes through pregnancy after pregnancy, loses her calf soon after birth and is slaughtered when production drops.

**Family:** B. Harm, death and welfare · **Part:** DIR

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "The male calf is going to be born anyway in dairy farming. At least veal makes use of him instead of wasting him."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Dairy production generates male calves who are no use for milk.
2. **P2.** If nothing is done with them, they'll be killed and wasted.
3. **P3.** Using what would be wasted is better than wasting it.
4. **C.** So eating veal is a responsible way to make use of these calves.

P1 is true. So is P2, within the current system. The problem is that the argument treats as fixed something that results from a choice: the calves only exist because people buy milk.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails because it treats as inevitable what depends on demand.

## 3. Answer for children

A cow only gives milk after having a baby, just like human mothers. To keep giving milk, she has to have a calf every year. The male calves won't give milk, so many are killed while still babies, some to become a meat called veal.

## 4. Short answer

Veal doesn't make use of a calf who would be born anyway: it's part of the system that causes that calf to be born. A cow only gives milk if she gives birth every year, and the males are no use for milk; so they are killed young, some as veal. In New Zealand, about 1.8 million calves were slaughtered in a single year, many at 4 days old. What decides how many are born is how much milk is bought; if you drink milk, that calf is part of your account. And the suffering isn't only the calf's: the mother is made pregnant again each year, separated from her calf and, on most farms, slaughtered young once she stops paying her way.

**Question to ask back:** "If the calf is only born because the cow needs to give milk, is he born 'anyway', or because of the milk people buy?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument starts in the middle of the story:

| Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Insemination | The cow is made pregnant so she starts giving milk |
| Birth | A calf is born; half are males |
| Separation | The calf is taken from his mother so the milk can be sold |
| The male's fate | Slaughter at a few days old, or a few months of rearing as veal |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. The calf isn't born "anyway".** He's born because dairy production requires births. Buying milk funds those births, and buying veal funds one of the ways of disposing of the males.

**2. The scale is large.** In New Zealand, where dairy is central, about 1.8 million such calves were slaughtered in the year ending September 2019, according to Ministry for Primary Industries data; slaughter is allowed from 4 days of age.

**3. Veal production has had very cruel versions.** Individual crates, in which calves were kept unable to turn around, were banned in the European Union for calves over 8 weeks by Directive 2008/119/EC. Improvements like this reduce suffering, but they don't change the animal's fate (B4).

**4. "Making use" isn't the same as not causing.** Giving a profitable destination to a victim of the system makes the system more profitable, not less.

**5. The whole cycle causes suffering, not only to the calf.** The argument looks at the male and forgets the mother. Separating the calf within the first hours or days of life is common practice on dairy farms, and in 2023 the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) recommended that the calf stay with its mother for at least one day. The cow is made pregnant again to keep producing, and EFSA's opinion on dairy cows dealt with problems such as lameness, mastitis and restriction of movement. When production drops, she too goes to slaughter: in a study of Dutch herds, the average age of culled cows was about 5.9 years, against a natural lifespan of around 20. Conditions vary a great deal between systems, and are worse in the most intensive ones, but pregnancy, separation and slaughter are part of almost all commercial dairy production (B5).

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: what if the veal is raised with good welfare?

The most serious version says there is veal from calves raised outdoors, with their mother or in groups, for a few months, and that this is better than killing them at a few days old.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **It's better for the calf, compared with slaughter at 4 days.** But it's still a young animal killed to be eaten.
2. **It doesn't change the cause.** The calf still exists because of milk, and veal still makes milk more profitable.
3. **The question goes back to milk.** The argument shows that milk and meat are part of the same system (B5).

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in B6 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | False inevitability | The calf's birth is treated as a natural fact, not as an effect of demand |
| Epistemological | Not knowing how milk is produced | Ignores that the cow must give birth to give milk |
| Moral | Use as justification | Giving a profitable destination to a victim is treated as a virtue |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Talks about not wasting.** And supports a system that generates millions of calves it has no use for.
2. **Considers milk harmless.** And knows the males must have some fate.
3. **Wouldn't accept the same with puppies.** Breeding dogs to sell their milk and killing the puppies would be cruelty.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains B6 | Evidence |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a system creates victims, making use of them is responsible".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Male chicks in the egg industry | Using them as feed would be responsible | They too are victims of an avoidable system |
| Any byproduct of a harm | Using it would justify the harm | See I8 |
| Reducing demand for milk | Fewer calves would be born to die | This is the actual solution |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that, within the current system, male calves will be born, and that raising them carefully for a few months may be better than killing them at a few days old. It's also true that wasting an animal's life is even worse than using it.

But the argument only works if the system is treated as inevitable. It isn't: the number of calves depends on how much milk is bought.
