# H5. "Imported vegan food pollutes in transport"

**Thesis:** transport is a small part of food emissions: for most products, less than 10%, and for beef, about 0.5%. What you eat weighs far more than how far it traveled. A kilo of beef emits about 60 kilos of CO₂ equivalent; a kilo of peas, about 1. Swapping meat for plants cuts emissions far more than eating local.

**Family:** H. Environment, ecology and agriculture · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Your tofu comes from far away. The neighbor's beef pollutes less than imported nuts."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Transporting food emits greenhouse gases.
2. **P2.** Many vegan products are imported or travel long distances.
3. **P3.** Local foods avoid those emissions.
4. **C.** So eating local meat can be more sustainable than eating imported vegan products.

P1 and P2 are true. So is P3, in part. The problem lies in the conclusion: it assumes transport is a large part of emissions, and it isn't.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails because transport is a small fraction of food emissions.

## 3. Answer for children

Bringing food from far away uses a bit of fuel. But what pollutes most is making the food, not carrying it from one place to another. Raising a cow pollutes much more than growing peas, even if the peas come from far away.

## 4. Short answer

Transport is a small part of food emissions: for most products, less than 10%, and for beef, about 0.5%. What you eat matters far more than where it comes from. A kilo of beef emits about 60 kilos of CO₂ equivalent; a kilo of peas, about 1. Even shipped from far away, plants emit much less than local meat, except for the few foods that travel by air.

**Question to ask back:** "If transport is a small fraction of emissions, what weighs more: the distance or what you eat?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument overestimates the weight of transport:

| Source of emissions | Relative weight |
| --- | --- |
| Land-use change and on-farm production | Most of the emissions, especially for meat |
| Transport | Less than 10% for most foods; about 0.5% for beef |
| Air freight | Very polluting, but about 0.16% of food transport |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Transport weighs little.** According to Our World in Data, transport accounts for less than 10% of the emissions of most foods. Across whole diets, about 5% to 6%.

**2. The type of food weighs much more.** A kilo of beef emits about 60 kilos of CO₂ equivalent; a kilo of peas, about 1. No transport distance makes up that difference.

**3. Most international food trade travels by ship.** Ships emit little per tonne carried. Air freight, the more polluting option, is a tiny fraction, and mainly affects fresh, perishable foods.

**4. Eating local doesn't solve the problem of meat.** Local meat still carries the emissions of production, which are most of the total.

**5. Many vegan foods are local.** Grains, beans, potatoes, corn and vegetables are grown in most regions of the world.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Our World in Data, food choice versus eating local](https://ourworldindata.org/food-choice-vs-eating-local) | Transport is less than 10% of the emissions of most foods and about 0.5% for beef; 1 kg of beef emits about 60 kg of CO₂ equivalent, and 1 kg of peas, about 1 | Based on Poore and Nemecek, 2018 |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: some foods come by air

The most serious version grants that shipping weighs little, but points out that fresh, perishable foods sometimes come by air, which greatly increases emissions.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Air freight is rare.** About 0.16% of food transport.
2. **Avoiding air-freighted foods is easy.** Just prefer seasonal or long-lasting products.
3. **Even by air, beef emits more.** The difference in production is still greater.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Transport is a detail in emissions, and even if it weighed more, it wouldn't answer the question about the animals. Local meat also comes from animals raised and killed. If it's possible to eat well on plants, local or not, what justifies that cost?

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in H5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Epistemological | Overestimating one factor | Treats transport as the main source of emissions |
| Logical | Unequal comparison | Compares distance, not production |
| Logical | Tu quoque | Accuses vegans instead of comparing impacts |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Eats imported products.** Coffee, chocolate, bananas and many others.
2. **Ignores the emissions from producing meat.** Which are much greater than those from transport.
3. **Also eats local vegan foods.** Grains, potatoes, vegetables.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "what's local is more sustainable".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tomatoes from local heated greenhouses | They'd be better than tomatoes imported from warm climates | They often emit more |
| Local beef | It would be better than imported peas | It emits dozens of times more |
| Any product | Local would always be better | Production weighs more than distance |

## 11. What the argument gets right

Buying local food can have benefits, such as supporting regional producers and reducing waste. Air-freighting perishables is indeed polluting, and worth avoiding.

But for the climate, what you eat weighs far more than where it comes from. The most effective change isn't shortening the distance; it's swapping meat for plants.
