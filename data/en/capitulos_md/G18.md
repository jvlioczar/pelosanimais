# G18. "Ultra-processed vegan foods are worse than meat"

**Thesis:** it's a false dichotomy. Veganism doesn't require ultra-processed food: rice, beans, lentils, vegetables, fruit and grains are vegan, and they're the basis of the *Dietary Guidelines for the Brazilian Population*, an international reference precisely because it recommends real food. And many processed meats, such as sausages, nuggets and other cured or reconstituted meats, are also ultra-processed, and processed meat is classified as carcinogenic by the WHO.

**Family:** G. Health and nutrition · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Vegan meat is full of chemicals. I prefer real food."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Ultra-processed foods are linked to worse health outcomes.
2. **P2.** Many vegan products, such as plant-based burgers and cheeses, are ultra-processed.
3. **P3.** A piece of meat is an unprocessed or minimally processed food.
4. **C.** So eating meat is healthier than eating vegan products.

P1 is true. So is P2. P3 is true for some cuts, but not for processed meat. The problem lies in the conclusion: it compares meat with the worst vegan products and ignores most of the vegan diet, which is made up of unprocessed foods.

The argument is **valid** for the comparison it makes. It fails because it's a false dichotomy.

## 3. Answer for children

Real food is food that comes from nature with few changes: rice, beans, fruit, vegetables. Almost all of it is vegan! Some store products, like sausages and nuggets, have lots of factory ingredients, and that goes for both the meat ones and the vegan ones.

## 4. Short answer

Veganism doesn't require ultra-processed food. Rice, beans, lentils, vegetables, fruit and grains are vegan, and they're exactly the basis that Brazil's dietary guidelines, often cited worldwide, recommend. Plant-based burgers are optional, useful for the transition or for variety. And many processed meats, such as sausages, nuggets and other reconstituted meats, are also ultra-processed, and processed meat is classified as carcinogenic. The fair comparison is real food against real food.

**Question to ask back:** "Are rice, beans and salad ultra-processed?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument compares different categories:

| Category | With meat | Vegan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unprocessed or minimally processed | Cuts of meat, eggs, milk | Rice, beans, lentils, vegetables, fruit, grains, nuts |
| Processed | Cheeses, cured meats | Tofu, breads, canned foods |
| Ultra-processed | Sausages, nuggets, bologna and other reconstituted meats | Industrial plant-based burgers and cheeses |

The fair comparison is within the same category.

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. It's a false dichotomy.** The choice isn't between meat and a plant-based burger. Most of a healthy vegan diet is made up of unprocessed foods.

**2. A leading dietary guideline itself favors a plant diet.** The 2014 *Dietary Guidelines for the Brazilian Population*, which popularized the "ultra-processed" category, recommends basing one's diet on unprocessed and minimally processed foods, in great variety and mainly of plant origin.

**3. Many meats are also ultra-processed.** Sausages, nuggets and other reconstituted meats are ultra-processed, and the WHO's cancer agency classifies processed meat, which also includes cured and smoked meats, as carcinogenic.

**4. Ultra-processed vegan products are optional.** They can help with the transition and with convenience, but no one needs them. It's like saying eating meat requires eating sausages.

**5. "Chemicals" isn't a criterion.** Everything is chemistry, including meat. What matters is nutritional composition and the degree of processing.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Brazil's Ministry of Health, *Dietary Guidelines for the Brazilian Population* (2014, in Portuguese; an English edition was published in 2015)](https://bvsms.saude.gov.br/bvs/publicacoes/guia_alimentar_populacao_brasileira_2ed.pdf) | Recommends basing one's diet on unprocessed or minimally processed foods, in great variety and mainly of plant origin; avoiding ultra-processed foods | An international reference |
| [IARC, press release no. 240 (2015)](https://www.iarc.who.int/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/pr240_E.pdf) | Processed meat is carcinogenic | Shows that meat is also among the problematic ultra-processed foods |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: in practice, vegans eat a lot of ultra-processed food

The most serious version grants that a vegan diet can be made of real food, but says that in practice many vegans depend on industrial products.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Meat eaters also eat a lot of ultra-processed food.** Consumption of ultra-processed food is high on every diet.
2. **The problem is ultra-processing, not veganism.** The recommendation is the same for everyone: eat real food.
3. **Many traditional plates are already vegan at their base.** Rice and beans, dal, lentil soups, salads and vegetables.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Concern about ultra-processed food applies to every diet, and doesn't justify eating animals. If it's possible to eat real food without meat, the moral question returns: why choose the option that requires someone's death?

| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | False dichotomy | Either meat or ultra-processed vegan food |
| Logical | Unequal comparison | Compares the best of meat with the worst of vegan food |
| Epistemological | Appeal to nature | "Chemicals" treated as synonymous with harmful |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Eats sausages, ham or nuggets.** Which are also ultra-processed.
2. **Eats rice and beans.** Which are vegan and real food.
3. **Uses the same criticism only against vegans.** Ultra-processed food with meat doesn't bother them.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains G18 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirmation bias | Sees ultra-processed food only on the vegan plate | General literature on confirmation bias |
| Naturalness bias | Meat seems "natural" even when processed | Linked to C1 |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if there are ultra-processed versions of a diet, the diet is worse".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diets with meat | They'd be worse, because sausages and nuggets exist | No one concludes this |
| Diets with bread | They'd be worse, because ultra-processed bread exists | Homemade bread exists |
| Any diet | It would be worse | The criterion is processing, not the diet |

## 11. What the argument gets right

Concern about ultra-processed food is legitimate, and many industrial vegan products aren't healthy. Real food is better than factory products, and veganism shouldn't be presented as a synonym for plant-based burgers.

But that concern applies to everyone. The best answer is to eat grains, beans, vegetables and fruit, a diet that in many cuisines is already traditional and almost vegan.
