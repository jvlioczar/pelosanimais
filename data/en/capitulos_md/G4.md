# G4. "You'll lack iron, zinc and other micronutrients"

**Thesis:** iron, zinc and other micronutrients can be obtained from plants with a varied diet and some planning. Iron deficiency is the most common nutritional deficiency in the world, and eating meat doesn't prevent it: about 30% of women aged 15 to 49 have anemia, in a population where most people eat meat. Vegetarians and vegans tend to have lower iron stores, which calls for attention and blood tests, not a return to meat.

**Family:** G. Health and nutrition · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Vegans are always anemic. Iron from plants isn't absorbed."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Iron from plants (non-heme) is absorbed at a lower rate than iron from meat (heme).
2. **P2.** Zinc from plants is also less bioavailable, because of phytates.
3. **P3.** So vegans are at greater risk of deficiency in these minerals.
4. **C.** So a meat-free diet is risky for health.

P1 and P2 are true. P3 is partly true: studies show vegetarians have, on average, lower iron stores. The problem lies in the conclusion: a risk that can be managed with planning doesn't make the diet inadequate.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at the conclusion: "greater risk" becomes "too risky", when the risk can be managed.

## 3. Answer for children

Iron helps the blood carry oxygen around the body. It's in beans, lentils, dark green leaves and lots of other foods. A trick: eating a fruit with vitamin C, like an orange, with your beans helps your body make better use of the iron.

## 4. Short answer

Iron from plants is absorbed at a lower rate, but there's plenty of iron in beans, lentils, chickpeas, tofu and dark leafy greens, and vitamin C in the meal increases absorption. Cooking, soaking and sprouting reduce the phytates that get in the way of zinc. And anemia is common on every diet: it affects about 30% of women aged 15 to 49 worldwide, and eating meat doesn't prevent it. Lower iron stores call for attention and blood tests, not meat.

**Question to ask back:** "If eating meat doesn't prevent anemia, is the solution meat or a well-planned diet?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument turns a point of attention into an obstacle:

| Nutrient | Plant sources | What improves absorption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Iron | Beans, lentils, chickpeas, tofu, dark leafy greens, seeds | Vitamin C in the same meal; avoiding coffee and tea alongside |
| Zinc | Legumes, nuts, seeds, whole grains | Soaking, sprouting, fermenting |
| Iodine | Iodized salt | Using iodized salt |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. There are many plant sources.** Iron and zinc are found in everyday foods such as beans, lentils and grains.

**2. Absorption can be improved.** Vitamin C increases the absorption of non-heme iron. Simple techniques, such as soaking legumes, reduce phytates.

**3. Eating meat doesn't prevent deficiency.** According to the WHO, about 30% of non-pregnant women aged 15 to 49 and about 40% of young children worldwide have anemia, and iron deficiency is the main nutritional cause. The figure doesn't prove that meat causes anemia, since most people eat meat; it shows that eating meat isn't enough to prevent it.

**4. Nutrition bodies consider the diet adequate.** The US Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics recognizes that well-planned vegan diets are adequate, with attention to these nutrients.

**5. Attention isn't impossibility.** Every diet has points of attention. Diets with meat carry risks linked to saturated fat and processed meat (G13).

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [WHO, anemia fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/anaemia) | About 30% of non-pregnant women aged 15 to 49 and 40% of children aged 6 to 59 months have anemia; iron deficiency is the main nutritional cause | Global data from 2019 |
| [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27886704/) | Well-planned vegan diets are adequate; vegetarians take in as much iron as omnivores, but tend to have lower stores | Official position |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: iron stores are lower

The most serious version points out that, on average, vegetarians and vegans have lower iron stores (ferritin), and that this can be a risk for vulnerable groups, such as women of childbearing age.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Lower stores aren't deficiency.** Lower ferritin within the normal range isn't anemia; what matters is monitoring.
2. **The risk can be monitored.** Regular blood tests and, when needed, supplementation solve it.
3. **Vulnerable groups need attention with meat too.** Anemia in women is common on every diet.

### (f) Back to the moral question

If iron and zinc can be obtained from plants with planning, the need to eat animals disappears. What remains is the moral question: does looking after one's own nutrition justify others being killed, when there are alternatives?

| Moral | Invented necessity | Uses a manageable risk as a justification for harm |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Doesn't worry about anemia in meat eaters.** The concern only comes up with vegans.
2. **Eats beans.** One of the main sources of iron in many diets.
3. **Accepts other points of attention in their own diet.** Every diet has risks that require care.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a diet requires attention to some nutrient, it's inadequate".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diets with meat | They'd be inadequate, because they require attention to fiber, saturated fat and anemia | No one concludes this |
| Diets in pregnancy | They'd be inadequate, because they require extra folic acid and iron | No one concludes this |
| Any diet | It would be inadequate | Every diet requires some care |

## 11. What the argument gets right

Iron and zinc deserve attention in vegan diets, and it's true that iron stores tend to be lower. Information about sources, combinations and blood tests is part of a responsible transition.

But attention isn't impossibility. With a varied diet, deficiency is preventable, and anemia remains a common problem among meat eaters too.
