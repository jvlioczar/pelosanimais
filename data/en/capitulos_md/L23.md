# L23. "Most vegans give up"

**Thesis:** difficulty sticking with something says nothing about whether it's morally right. A 2014 Faunalytics survey found that about 84% of vegetarians and vegans abandon the diet, many in the first months. That shows change is hard and support is lacking, not that the change is wrong. Many people also give up trying to quit smoking, and no one concludes smoking is good.

**Family:** L. Attacks on veganism and vegans · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Almost everyone goes back to eating meat. Not even you can stick with it."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Most people who adopt a vegetarian or vegan diet abandon it.
2. **P2.** If so many give up, the diet must be hard, unsatisfying or harmful.
3. **C.** So veganism isn't a realistic or adequate option.

P1 is supported by surveys. P2 has a point: the difficulty is real. The problem lies in the conclusion: how hard it is to keep up a practice doesn't say whether it's right.

The argument is **valid** as an observation about difficulty. It fails as a moral argument.

## 3. Answer for children

Lots of people try to learn a musical instrument and give up. Does that mean playing music is bad? No! It means it's hard and some people need more help. Veganism is similar: some people give up, but that doesn't change the fact that animals feel.

## 4. Short answer

It's true that many people give up: a 2014 Faunalytics survey found that about 84% of vegetarians and vegans abandon the diet, many in the first months. The reasons most cited were dissatisfaction with the food, health and social issues, which shows a lack of support and information. But that says change is hard, not that change is wrong. Many people give up trying to quit smoking, and no one concludes smoking is good.

**Question to ask back:** "If many people give up something hard, does that make it wrong to try?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument confuses difficulty with wrongness:

| Fact | What it shows | What it doesn't show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Many give up | That keeping up the change is hard | That the change is wrong |
| Many give up early | That the initial phase is the hardest | That the diet is inadequate |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Difficulty isn't wrongness.** Many good practices are hard to keep up, such as quitting smoking or exercising.

**2. The reasons point to a lack of support.** In the Faunalytics survey, the main reasons were dissatisfaction with the food (32%), health (26%) and social issues (13%). Many problems could be solved with recipes, planning and community.

**3. Many give up early.** About a third of those who gave up kept the diet for three months or less, the hardest phase.

**4. Giving up calls for support, not abandoning the question.** If people give up for lack of recipes, planning and company, the answer is to offer those.

**5. The moral question doesn't depend on adherence.** Animals still feel, however many people manage to change.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Faunalytics, study of current and former vegetarians and vegans](https://faunalytics.org/a-summary-of-faunalytics-study-of-current-and-former-vegetarians-and-vegans/) | 84% of vegetarians and vegans give up; 34% within three months; the reasons most cited are dissatisfaction with the food, health and social issues | A survey of more than 11,000 adults in the US |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: giving up signals health problems

The most serious version points out that 26% of those who gave up cited health, and that this may indicate the diet doesn't suit many people.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Health problems usually come from lack of planning.** Such as missing B12 or too little energy (G13).
2. **Many had no supervision.** Which calls for more information, not abandonment.
3. **The moral question remains.** Even where there are real difficulties, they justify exceptions, not a general rule (D7).

### (f) Back to the moral question

The number of people who give up shows that change is hard, and that those who try deserve support. But it doesn't answer the question about the animals. Those who give up still have the same moral reason that led them to try.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in L23 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Appeal to the crowd | If many give up, the practice is wrong |
| Logical | Confusing difficulty with wrongness | Difficulty becomes a moral argument |
| Epistemological | Ignoring the reasons | Doesn't consider why people give up |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Doesn't use the argument for other hard habits.** Such as quitting smoking.
2. **Values persistence in other areas.** Study, sport, work.
3. **Recognizes the diet needs support.** Which calls for help, not abandonment.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains L23 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirmation bias | Remembers the ex-vegans and forgets those who persist | General literature on confirmation bias |
| Motivated reasoning | Others giving up excuses one's own attempt | General literature on motivated cognition |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if most people give something up, it's wrong".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quitting smoking | It would be wrong, because many relapse | No one concludes this |
| Weight-loss diets | They'd be wrong, because many give up | No one concludes this |
| Regular exercise | It would be wrong, because many stop | No one concludes this |

## 11. What the argument gets right

The high dropout rate is real, and it says something important: change is hard, and many people don't get the support they need. Animal advocates should take this seriously, offering recipes, nutrition information and a welcoming community.

But the difficulty doesn't change the moral question. Animals still feel, and everyone who tries, even if they stumble, is already answering that question.
