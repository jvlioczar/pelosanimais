# H1. "Vegans' soy is destroying the Amazon"

**Thesis:** about 77% of the world's soy becomes animal feed, and only about 7% becomes foods such as tofu and soy milk. Meat eaters consume far more soy, indirectly, than vegans. And the main driver of tropical deforestation is pasture for cattle: between 2005 and 2013, it accounted for about 41% of deforestation in the tropics.

**Family:** H. Environment, ecology and agriculture · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Tofu causes deforestation. You eat the soy that destroys forests."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** The expansion of soy contributes to deforestation in the Amazon and the Cerrado.
2. **P2.** Vegans eat soy products, such as tofu and soy milk.
3. **P3.** So vegans contribute to deforestation.
4. **C.** So veganism isn't environmentally superior.

P1 is true. So is P2. P3 is true on a small scale. The problem lies in the conclusion: it ignores where almost all soy goes, and who consumes it.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails because it leaves out that livestock farming consumes most of the soy and is the main driver of deforestation.

## 3. Answer for children

Most of the world's soy doesn't become tofu: it becomes food for pigs, chickens and cattle. And many forests are cut down to make pasture for cattle. So people who eat meat use much more soy and land than people who eat tofu.

## 4. Short answer

About 77% of the world's soy becomes animal feed, and only about 7% becomes tofu, soy milk and other foods. Meat eaters consume far more soy, indirectly, than vegans. And the main driver of tropical deforestation is pasture: about 41% of deforestation in the tropics between 2005 and 2013. If the concern is the forest, the problem lies on the plate with meat.

**Question to ask back:** "If 77% of soy becomes feed, who eats more soy on average: the vegan or someone who eats chicken and pork?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument ignores where soy goes:

| Where soy goes | Approximate share |
| --- | --- |
| Animal feed | 77% |
| Oil, biofuels and industrial uses | About 16% |
| Direct foods (tofu, soy milk, tempeh, edamame) | About 7% |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Most soy feeds animals.** According to Our World in Data, 77% of the world's soy is used as feed. Only a small share becomes direct human food.

**2. Meat uses far more soy.** Because animals convert feed into meat with large losses, people who eat chicken and pork indirectly consume far more soy than people who eat tofu.

**3. Pasture is the main driver.** Between 2005 and 2013, the expansion of cattle pasture was responsible for about 41% of tropical deforestation, according to a study by Pendrill and colleagues. Soy, oil palm and other oilseeds together accounted for about 18%, and much of that soy becomes feed.

**4. Plant diets use far less land.** According to Poore and Nemecek, a global vegan diet would reduce farmland by about 75%.

**5. Soy can come from sources less linked to deforestation.** It's possible to prefer traceable soy, and there are many other legumes. The Amazon Soy Moratorium, in force since 2006, already restricts purchases of soy from deforested areas in the Amazon, although the Cerrado remains under pressure.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Our World in Data, soy](https://ourworldindata.org/soy) | 77% of soy becomes feed; about 7% becomes direct human food | Global data |
| [Our World in Data, drivers of deforestation](https://ourworldindata.org/drivers-of-deforestation) | Cattle pasture caused about 41% of tropical deforestation; oilseeds, including soy and oil palm, about 18% | Based on Pendrill et al., 2019, data from 2005 to 2013 |
| [Our World in Data, land use and diets](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use-diets) | A global vegan diet would reduce farmland by about 75% | Based on Poore and Nemecek, 2018 |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: tofu soy can also cause deforestation

The most serious version grants all this, but says that, even in a small share, the soy vegans eat may come from deforested areas.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **The proportion matters.** Eating less meat reduces soy use far more than avoiding tofu.
2. **Origin can be chosen.** Vegans can prefer brands with traceable soy, or other legumes.
3. **The argument favors veganism.** If the concern is soy, the solution is to eat fewer animals.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The environmental argument, examined properly, favors plant diets. But beyond the forest, there are the animals: the soy that becomes feed nourishes billions of animals raised to die. The moral question returns: what justifies raising and killing them?

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in H1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Omitting data | Ignores where almost all soy goes |
| Logical | Tu quoque | Accuses vegans of something meat does on a far greater scale |
| Epistemological | Not knowing the supply chain | Ignores the soy in feed |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Eats soy-fed chicken and pork.** And consumes far more soy than a vegan.
2. **Doesn't worry about pasture.** The main driver of deforestation.
3. **Uses soybean oil.** Present in a great many kitchens.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a food contributes a little to a harm, whoever eats it is as responsible as whoever consumes most of it".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water | Someone who drinks a glass would be as responsible as an industry wasting millions of liters | No one concludes this |
| Fuel | Someone who cycles and sometimes takes the bus would pollute as much as someone who drives every day | No one concludes this |
| Soy | The vegan would be as responsible as the meat eater | The numbers show the opposite |

## 11. What the argument gets right

Soy expansion does contribute to deforestation, and where food comes from matters for everyone, vegans included. It's legitimate to demand traceability and to call for an end to deforestation linked to any crop.

But the data show that soy is mostly feed, and that pasture is the biggest driver of deforestation. If the concern is the forest, a plant diet is the choice that protects it most.
