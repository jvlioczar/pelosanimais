# J5. "What would we do with the animals if everyone went vegan?"

**Thesis:** no one goes vegan all at once across the whole world. Change is gradual, and with less demand, fewer animals are bred. Farmed animals exist in enormous numbers because they're bred for this (C8). Those who already exist could live in sanctuaries or until the natural end of their lives. The problem isn't what to do with them; it's that we keep bringing them into the world to die.

**Family:** J. Practicality, economy and society · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Release the cows into the streets? They'd have to kill them all at once."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** There are billions of farmed animals in the world.
2. **P2.** Many wouldn't survive if released into the wild.
3. **P3.** If everyone stopped eating meat, there'd be nothing to do with them.
4. **C.** So veganism is impractical or would lead to a catastrophe for the animals themselves.

P1 and P2 are true. The problem lies in P3: change would be gradual, and the animals' breeding is controlled by the farmers.

The argument is **valid** for an instantaneous change. Its **soundness** fails because that change doesn't happen.

## 3. Answer for children

People won't all stop eating meat on the same day. It happens little by little. When fewer people buy meat, farms breed fewer animals. The animals that already exist can live until they're old, some in sanctuaries, which are like homes for rescued animals.

## 4. Short answer

No one goes vegan all at once across the whole world: change is gradual. With less demand, farmers breed fewer animals, because breeding is controlled through insemination and hatcheries. The animals that exist can live out their lives, some in sanctuaries. There wouldn't be cows loose in the streets; there'd be fewer cows being born to die.

**Question to ask back:** "If hatcheries incubate eggs according to demand, what happens when demand falls?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument imagines a scenario that doesn't happen:

| How the argument imagines it | How change happens |
| --- | --- |
| Everyone stops eating meat at once | Consumption falls gradually |
| Billions of animals are left with nowhere to go | Fewer animals are bred each year |
| The animals would have to be released or killed | Existing ones live out their lives; new ones stop being born |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Change is gradual.** Meat consumption shifts over years and generations.

**2. Breeding is controlled.** Chickens hatch in hatcheries and cattle and pigs are born through insemination, in numbers set by demand (C8).

**3. The existing animals can live.** In sanctuaries, on small holdings or to the end of their cycle, while breeding declines.

**4. The question reverses the responsibility.** The problem isn't what to do with the animals if we stop eating them; it's that we keep breeding billions of them to die.

**5. Selectively bred breeds may decline.** Many production breeds have health problems caused by breeding (B10). Breeding fewer of them is no loss to the animals.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Bar-On, Phillips and Milo, 2018, *PNAS*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6016768/) | Farmed animals make up about 60% of mammal biomass | Shows that the number of animals results from production; also used in C8 |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: some breeds would disappear

The most serious version points out that, with the end of farming, many domestic breeds could disappear, and that this would be a loss of diversity.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Breeds aren't individuals.** What matters morally is the animals who feel, not the continuity of a breed (A18).
2. **Some could continue in sanctuaries.** Without being bred to die.
3. **Many breeds suffer because of selection.** Chickens who can barely walk lose nothing by not being born.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The question about the animals' fate is legitimate, but the answer is simple: stop breeding them for slaughter. The moral problem isn't what to do with them afterwards; it's continuing to bring them into the world to die.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in J5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Epistemological | Unrealistic scenario | Imagines an instantaneous change |
| Logical | Reversing responsibility | Treats a consequence of farming as an argument for continuing to farm |
| Moral | Species in place of the individual | Worries about the breed, not about who is born to die |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Knows production follows demand.** For any other product.
2. **Doesn't worry about the animals' fate today.** Which is slaughter.
3. **Doesn't think we must preserve other breeds at any cost.** Many domestic breeds have already disappeared.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains J5 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motivated reasoning | An imaginary scenario excuses real change | General literature on motivated cognition |
| Status quo bias | The current number of animals seems natural | [Samuelson and Zeckhauser, 1988](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/jrisku/v1y1988i1p7-59.html) |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if we stop using something, we'd have to destroy what already exists".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pedigree dogs | Ending breeding would require killing existing dogs | No one concludes this |
| Cart horses | Banning carts would require killing the horses | Cities set up adoption programs |
| Farmed animals | Ending consumption would require killing them | Breeding simply declines |

## 11. What the argument gets right

The question about the fate of existing animals is legitimate, and a responsible transition needs to answer it, with sanctuaries, public policy and care for the animals already alive.

But the scenario of billions of animals set loose doesn't happen, because change is gradual and breeding is controlled. The best way to care for farmed animals is to stop breeding them to die.
