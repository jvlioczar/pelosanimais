# J10. "Vegan food is bland"

**Thesis:** it's a generalization born of unfamiliarity. The flavor of food comes mainly from seasonings, techniques and combinations of ingredients, and almost all seasonings are plants: garlic, onion, chilies, herbs, spices. Many traditional dishes from around the world are already vegan or nearly so: chana masala, falafel, dal, pasta arrabbiata, bean chili, Ethiopian lentil stews, Brazilian *acarajé*. Vegan food isn't "just salad".

**Family:** J. Practicality, economy and society · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Just salad? Rabbit food. Am I supposed to eat grass?"

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Meat and dairy give flavor, texture and satiety to many dishes.
2. **P2.** Without them, many recipes are less tasty.
3. **P3.** The pleasure of eating is important for quality of life.
4. **C.** So a vegan diet reduces quality of life.

P1 and P3 are true. P2 is true for recipes built around meat, but not for well-made plant cooking. The problem lies in the conclusion: it generalizes from bad examples or from unfamiliarity.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P2, generalized.

## 3. Answer for children

What makes food tasty is the seasoning: garlic, onion, herbs, chili, oregano, cumin. All of that comes from plants! Well-seasoned beans, fries, popcorn, pasta with tomato sauce, bread, fruit: all of that is vegan and really tasty.

## 4. Short answer

Almost all the flavor in food comes from seasonings and techniques, and seasonings are plants: garlic, onion, chilies, herbs, spices. Many traditional dishes are already vegan or nearly so: well-seasoned beans, falafel, chana masala, dal, pasta arrabbiata, bean chili, garlic bread. Vegan food isn't "just salad". People who find it bland have often tried very little of it, or tried it badly made.

**Question to ask back:** "Are fries, seasoned beans and pasta with tomato sauce bland?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument confuses the absence of meat with the absence of flavor:

| Source of flavor | Is it a plant? |
| --- | --- |
| Garlic, onion, chilies, herbs, spices | Yes |
| Techniques: sautéing, roasting, grilling, smoking, fermenting | Yes, applicable to plants |
| Umami: tomato, mushrooms, soy sauce, miso | Yes |
| Fat: olive oil, oils, nuts, avocado | Yes |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Seasonings are plants.** Much of what makes food tasty comes from plants: garlic, onion, herbs, chilies and spices.

**2. Many traditional dishes are already vegan.** Around the world, there's an enormous variety of dishes without animal products or easily adapted.

**3. It's a generalization.** Often, the person saying it has tried few options, or badly prepared ones.

**4. Much of the flavor we associate with meat comes from technique and seasoning.** Smoked, grilled, browned, sautéed: all of this can be applied to vegetables and plant proteins. The result isn't identical to meat, but it isn't "bland".

**5. Many chefs and restaurants show otherwise.** Plant cooking has gained ground in award-winning restaurants and kitchens around the world.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Brazil's Ministry of Health, *Dietary Guidelines for the Brazilian Population* (2014, in Portuguese)](https://bvsms.saude.gov.br/bvs/publicacoes/guia_alimentar_populacao_brasileira_2ed.pdf) | Values traditional cooking based on plant foods such as rice, beans, cassava, vegetables and fruit | An example of how rich a plant-based cuisine can be |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: some flavors are irreplaceable

The most serious version grants that vegan food can be tasty, but says certain flavors, such as a good barbecue or an aged cheese, have no plant equivalent.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **It changes the argument.** It stops being "bland" and becomes "doesn't have exactly that flavor" (J11).
2. **There are new flavors to discover.** The swap isn't only a loss; it's also a discovery.
3. **The pleasure of a flavor doesn't justify a death.** The central argument returns to J11.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Even if some specific flavor were irreplaceable, the question would be whether the pleasure of a dish justifies an animal's life. And in practice, plant cooking offers so much pleasure that the question rarely needs to be asked.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in J10 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Hasty generalization | Judges all vegan cooking by a few experiences |
| Epistemological | Unfamiliarity | Ignores plant dishes and techniques |
| Moral | Pleasure as justification | Flavor outweighs the animal's life |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Eats many vegan dishes.** Without noticing.
2. **Seasons meat with plants.** The flavor comes from them.
3. **Likes fries, popcorn and bread.** Which are vegan.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains J10 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food neophobia | Unfamiliar foods seem less tasty | General literature on food neophobia |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if I tried a bad version, the whole category is bad".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Japanese cuisine | It would be bad because of one poorly made sushi | No one concludes this |
| Dishes with meat | They'd be bland because of one poorly seasoned steak | No one concludes this |
| Vegan food | It would be bland because of one unseasoned salad | The same error |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that there is bland vegan food, just as there is bland food with meat. For people who grew up with meat-centered dishes, early experiences can disappoint, and learning to cook with plants takes some time.

But plant cooking is vast, creative and full of flavor. Most of what makes food tasty already comes from plants.
