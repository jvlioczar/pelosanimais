# G9. "Soy causes female hormones and cancer"

**Thesis:** soy isoflavones are phytoestrogens, not estrogen, and they act differently in the body. A 2021 meta-analysis of 41 studies concluded that neither soy nor isoflavones alter testosterone or estrogen in men. As for breast cancer, studies in humans link soy consumption to the same or lower risk.

**Family:** G. Health and nutrition · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Soy feminizes men. Soy causes breast cancer. Soy is GMO poison."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Soy contains isoflavones, which can bind to estrogen receptors.
2. **P2.** Animal studies, using high doses, have shown hormonal effects.
3. **P3.** Hormonal effects can alter fertility and increase the risk of hormone-sensitive cancers.
4. **C.** So eating soy is risky.

P1 and P2 are true. The problem lies in P3 as applied to humans: studies in people, eating normal amounts of food, don't confirm these risks.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails because it carries results from rodent studies at high doses over to normal human consumption.

## 3. Answer for children

Soy has some substances that look a little bit like hormones, but they aren't the same and don't do the same things. Scientists have studied this a lot and found that eating soy doesn't change people's hormones. In Asia, people have eaten tofu and other soy foods for a very long time.

## 4. Short answer

Soy isoflavones are phytoestrogens, which aren't estrogen and act differently. A 2021 meta-analysis of 41 studies concluded that neither soy nor isoflavones alter testosterone or estrogen in men. Studies in humans link eating soy to the same or lower risk of breast cancer. The scares came from rodent studies at high doses, which don't carry over well to people.

**Question to ask back:** "If studies in people show no effect on hormones, why trust the scare that came from studies in rats more?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument confuses three things:

| Claim | What the evidence says |
| --- | --- |
| Isoflavones are estrogen | No: they're phytoestrogens, with different and much weaker effects |
| Soy alters male hormones | No, according to a meta-analysis of 41 studies in men |
| Soy increases breast cancer | No; in humans, eating soy foods is linked to the same or lower risk |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Phytoestrogens aren't estrogen.** Isoflavones bind to estrogen receptors selectively, and in some tissues they can even block the effect of stronger estrogen.

**2. There's no effect on male hormones.** In 2021, a meta-analysis of 41 studies concluded that, regardless of dose and duration, neither soy protein nor isoflavones altered total testosterone, free testosterone, estradiol or estrone in men.

**3. Soy doesn't increase breast cancer.** The American Cancer Society explains that rodent studies don't carry over well to humans, and that in people, soy foods are linked to the same or lower risk of cancer.

**4. Studies of those who eat the most soy don't show the alleged risk.** In Asian populations, where tofu, miso and tempeh are common, soy consumption is linked to the same or lower risk of breast cancer. Tradition alone wouldn't prove safety; what carries weight is these studies.

**5. Most soy goes to animals.** According to Our World in Data, about 77% of the world's soy is used as animal feed, and only a small share becomes direct human food, such as tofu and soy drinks. This says nothing about hormones, since meat eaters don't take in the isoflavones in animal feed; it says something about where the demand for soy, and the deforestation linked to it, comes from (H1).

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Reed et al., 2021, *Reproductive Toxicology*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33383165/) | Neither soy nor isoflavones affect male reproductive hormones | Meta-analysis of 41 studies |
| [American Cancer Society, soy and cancer risk](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/latest-news/soy-and-cancer-risk-our-experts-advice.html) | Soy foods are linked to the same or lower cancer risk; rodent studies don't apply well to humans | Official guidance |
| [Our World in Data, soy](https://ourworldindata.org/soy) | About 77% of soy is used as animal feed | Global data |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: isoflavone supplements are something else

The most serious version points out that concentrated isoflavone supplements have much higher doses than foods, and that the American Cancer Society itself doesn't recommend them.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **It applies to supplements, not foods.** Tofu, soy milk and tempeh aren't concentrated supplements.
2. **A vegan diet doesn't require soy.** There are many other legumes.
3. **Caution about supplements doesn't change the conclusion about foods.** The American Cancer Society, which doesn't recommend the supplements, considers soy foods safe.

### (f) Back to the moral question

If soy doesn't carry the risks attributed to it, and a vegan diet doesn't even need it, the argument loses its force. The moral question remains: does fear of a food justify raising and killing animals?

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Eats meat from soy-fed animals.** Soy is at the base of livestock farming.
2. **Doesn't fear other foods with phytoestrogens.** They're in many vegetables, grains and beer.
3. **Trusts rodent studies only in this case.** On other subjects, demands studies in humans.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a food has hormone-like substances, it alters hormones".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hops in beer | Beer would feminize men | No one concludes this |
| Cow's milk | Which contains the cow's hormones, would also be dangerous | Those who fear soy rarely fear milk |
| Flaxseed and grains | They'd also be dangerous | No one concludes this |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that isoflavones interact with estrogen receptors, and that concentrated supplements deserve caution. Concern about hormonal health is legitimate, and the subject was studied precisely because of it.

But studies in humans eating soy foods don't confirm the fears. Soy is safe, a vegan diet doesn't even depend on it, and most of the world's soy feeds the animals who become meat.
