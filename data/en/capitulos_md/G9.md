# G9. "Athletes need meat"

**Thesis:** what matters for performance and muscle gain is the amount of protein and energy, not its source. A 2021 study compared young vegans and omnivores eating the same amount of protein over 12 weeks of resistance training and found no difference in muscle mass or strength. There are elite vegan athletes in many sports.

**Family:** G. Health and nutrition · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Nobody builds muscle without meat. There's no such thing as a vegan bodybuilder."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Athletes need more protein than the average person.
2. **P2.** Animal proteins are high quality and rich in leucine, which is important for muscle synthesis.
3. **P3.** With plant protein, it's harder to meet an athlete's needs.
4. **C.** So athletes need to eat meat.

P1 and P2 are true. P3 has a point: better planning may be needed. The problem lies in the conclusion: the evidence shows that, with enough protein, the source makes no difference.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails because P3 describes a planning difficulty, not an impossibility.

## 3. Answer for children

To get strong, the body needs protein and plenty of food. That protein can come from beans, lentils, tofu and other plants. There are champion athletes who don't eat any meat at all!

## 4. Short answer

What matters for muscle is the amount of protein and energy, not its source. In 2021, a study compared young vegans and omnivores eating the same amount of protein over 12 weeks of resistance training and found no difference in muscle mass or strength. There are elite vegan athletes in weightlifting, running, football and other sports. With planning, a vegan diet meets athletes' needs.

**Question to ask back:** "If vegans and omnivores gain the same muscle with the same protein, what does meat add?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument confuses the need for more protein with a need for meat:

| What the athlete needs | Can it come from plants? |
| --- | --- |
| More protein | Yes: legumes, soy, tofu, seitan, plant protein powders |
| Enough leucine | Yes, with an adequate amount of protein |
| Enough energy | Yes |
| Iron, B12, creatine | Yes, with planning and supplementation when needed |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. The source of protein doesn't change the result.** In 2021, Hevia-Larraín and colleagues followed young vegans and omnivores through 12 weeks of resistance training, with protein intake matched at 1.6 g per kilogram per day. There was no difference in muscle mass, fiber area or strength. To reach that amount, the vegans used soy protein powder.

**2. With enough protein, the difference is small or nil.** A 2018 meta-analysis found no difference between supplementing soy protein or animal protein in gains in mass and strength from resistance training. Another, from 2021, also found no difference in strength or absolute lean mass, but found a small advantage for animal protein in the lean mass of younger adults. The difference appears in some studies, is small, and can be offset with more plant protein.

**3. There are elite vegan athletes.** In many sports, vegan athletes compete and win at a high level. This shows the diet doesn't prevent performance.

**4. Nutrition bodies include athletes.** The US Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics considers well-planned vegan diets adequate for athletes too.

**5. "Nobody builds muscle without meat" is false.** There are vegan bodybuilders, and the phrase describes a prejudice, not a fact.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Hevia-Larraín and colleagues, 2021, *Sports Medicine*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-021-01434-9) | With the same protein intake, vegans and omnivores had the same gains in mass and strength over 12 weeks | A small study (38 young men), conducted at the University of São Paulo |
| [Messina and colleagues, 2018, *IJSNEM*](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.2018-0071) | No difference between soy protein and animal protein in gains in mass and strength | Meta-analysis of supplementation trials |
| [Lim and colleagues, 2021, *Nutrients*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7926405/) | No difference in strength or absolute lean mass; a small advantage for animal protein in the lean mass of adults under 50 | Meta-analysis; the result in younger people comes from a subgroup analysis |
| [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27886704/) | Well-planned vegan diets are adequate for athletes too | Official position |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: it's harder to reach the protein

The most serious version grants that vegans can build muscle, but says it's harder to reach high protein intakes from plant foods alone, and that many need supplements.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Omnivorous athletes also use supplements.** Whey is a supplement; there are plant equivalents.
2. **Harder isn't impossible.** It's a matter of planning, as with any athlete's diet.
3. **The difficulty doesn't justify the harm.** The effort of planning weighs less than the animals' lives.

### (f) Back to the moral question

If the source of protein doesn't change the result, meat isn't necessary even for athletes. What remains is a choice: does the convenience of a more concentrated protein source justify raising and killing animals?

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Uses protein supplements.** Which aren't meat either.
2. **Doesn't ask where a meat-eating athlete who eats badly gets protein.** The concern only comes up when the athlete is vegan.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a source is more concentrated, it's necessary".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whey | It would be necessary for all athletes | Many don't use it |
| Sugar | It would be necessary for energy, because it's concentrated | There are other sources |
| Meat | It would be necessary for protein | The error is the same |

## 11. What the argument gets right

Athletes have greater needs, and it's true that reaching high protein intakes takes more planning on a vegan diet. It's also true that studies on the subject are still relatively small.

But the available evidence and high-level vegan athletes show that meat isn't necessary. The protein muscles need can come from plants.
