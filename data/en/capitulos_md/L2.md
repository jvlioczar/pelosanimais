# L2. "Veganism is a cult, a religion"

**Thesis:** labeling a position a "cult" is a way of not assessing it. Veganism rests on public arguments that anyone can examine: animals feel, we can live without killing them, and causing unnecessary suffering is wrong. A religion asks for faith; veganism asks us to look at the facts and at the consistency of our own values.

**Family:** L. Attacks on veganism and vegans · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "You preach like fanatics. That's indoctrination."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Some vegans speak of veganism with fervor, rituals and group identity similar to religious ones.
2. **P2.** Religious beliefs can't be imposed on those who don't share them.
3. **P3.** Veganism is a belief of that kind.
4. **C.** So vegans shouldn't try to persuade others.

P1 may be true in some cases. The problem lies in P3: veganism doesn't depend on faith, but on facts and arguments everyone can assess.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P3.

## 3. Answer for children

A religion asks people to believe in things you can't see. Veganism is different: it talks about things you can check, like "animals feel pain" and "you can live well without eating meat". Anyone can think about this and reach their own conclusion.

## 4. Short answer

Calling it a cult is a way of not discussing it. A religion asks for faith; veganism rests on claims that can be checked: animals feel, well-planned vegan diets are adequate, and causing unnecessary suffering is wrong, which almost everyone already accepts. Defending a position with conviction doesn't make it religious. If it did, any cause defended with passion would be a cult.

**Question to ask back:** "Which part of the argument depends on faith rather than facts and reasons?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument labels instead of assessing:

| Feature of a religion | Does veganism have it? |
| --- | --- |
| Belief in entities or revealed truths | No |
| Sacred authority | No |
| Verifiable claims | Yes: animal sentience, nutritional adequacy |
| A widely accepted moral principle | Yes: don't cause unnecessary suffering |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Labeling isn't refuting.** Calling a position a cult doesn't show it's wrong.

**2. Veganism rests on public arguments.** Animal sentience (A5), nutritional adequacy (G1) and the principle of not causing unnecessary suffering can be assessed by anyone.

**3. Conviction isn't fanaticism.** Firmly defending a moral position is common in many causes.

**4. Eating meat also involves beliefs.** They're simply invisible because they're dominant (E9).

**5. Vegans are diverse.** There are religious vegans and atheist vegans, from many cultures and political positions.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [The New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness (2024)](https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration/declaration) | Strong scientific support for consciousness in mammals and birds | Shows the basis of veganism is verifiable |
| [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27886704/) | Well-planned vegan diets are adequate | An empirical basis, not a basis in faith |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: the group behavior is religious

The most serious version grants that veganism's reasons are public, but says the social behavior of many vegans resembles that of religious groups: strong identity, their own language, exclusion of those who don't follow.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Every group has social dynamics.** Sports fans, parties and professions do too.
2. **Group behavior doesn't change the reasons.** The question is still about the animals.
3. **It's a collective ad hominem.** It judges the idea by its followers.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The cult label avoids the question. But the question doesn't require faith: animals feel, and we can live without killing them. What justifies carrying on?

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in L2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Labeling | Classifies in order not to assess |
| Logical | False analogy | Compares moral conviction with religious faith |
| Epistemological | Ignoring the empirical basis | Disregards the facts veganism rests on |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Has strong moral convictions.** And doesn't consider them a religion.
2. **Champions other causes with passion.** Without thinking it's fanaticism.
3. **Follows cultural food norms.** Which are also shared beliefs.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a position is defended with conviction, it's religious".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The anti-smoking movement | It would be a cult | Absurd |
| Defending human rights | It would be a religion | Absurd |
| Veganism | It would be a cult | The same error |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that some vegans speak with fervor and that groups with a strong identity can become closed. It's also true that no one likes to feel indoctrinated, and that respectful communication works better.

But veganism doesn't ask for faith. It asks us to look at facts and at the consistency of our own values, something anyone can do.
