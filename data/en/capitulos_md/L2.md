# L2. "Vegans are all hippies"

**Thesis:** it's a stereotype, and even if it were true it wouldn't answer anything. What happens to animals doesn't depend on the lifestyle, clothes or politics of those who defend them. Vegans are a varied group, and research shows that prejudice against them is real and strong. Labeling someone a hippie is a way of not discussing the argument.

**Family:** L. Attacks on veganism and vegans · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Vegans are all hippies, living on incense and peace and love. You can't take them seriously."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Veganism is associated with a counterculture holding unrealistic ideas about society.
2. **P2.** Ideas coming from unrealistic groups tend to be unreliable.
3. **C.** So vegans' conclusions about animals don't deserve to be taken seriously.

P1 has a historical origin: natural food movements and the counterculture of the 1960s and 1970s helped spread vegetarianism. But animal ethics has other roots, and veganism today is far more varied. The bigger problem lies in P2, which judges the idea by the image of those who hold it.

The argument is **invalid**: it's an attack on the person (ad hominem), not on the argument.

## 3. Answer for children

Imagine someone said: "I won't listen to you because you wear a colorful T-shirt." Does that make sense? The T-shirt doesn't change whether what you said is right or wrong. It's the same with vegans: how the people who defend animals dress or live doesn't change the fact that animals feel pain.

## 4. Short answer

There are vegans of every kind: athletes, doctors, business owners, people on the right and on the left. But even if they were all hippies, the pig would still feel pain just the same. The style of the speaker doesn't change whether the argument is right. Labeling someone is a way of not discussing what they said.

**Question to ask back:** "If the same idea were said by someone in a suit, would it become more correct?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument swaps the question about the idea for a question about who holds it:

| Question | About what | Relevant to the animals? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What are vegans like? | Lifestyle, appearance, politics | No |
| Do animals suffer from exploitation? | Facts about the animals | Yes |
| Is it just to cause that suffering for a taste? | Ethics | Yes |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. It's ad hominem.** Judging an idea by the image of those who hold it is a classic error. An animal's pain doesn't depend on anyone's haircut.

**2. It's a stereotype.** Vegans are found in every profession, income bracket and political position. The image of the "hippie" describes, at most, a part of a part.

**3. The prejudice is real and documented.** Research shows that vegans and vegetarians are the target of negative attitudes equal to or greater than those directed at several other groups. The hippie label is one way of expressing that prejudice.

**4. Animal ethics has academic foundations.** The central argument of veganism comes from moral philosophy, discussed in universities by authors from different schools, not from a countercultural aesthetic.

**5. Even hippies can be right.** That a group has wrong ideas on some subjects doesn't show it's wrong on all of them. Each claim has to be assessed on what it says.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [MacInnis and Hodson, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/1368430215618253) | Omnivores evaluated vegetarians and vegans equally or more negatively than several common prejudice target groups; vegans were evaluated more negatively than vegetarians | *Group Processes & Intergroup Relations*, 20(6) |
| [Minson and Monin, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550611415695) | Meat eaters tend to disparage vegetarians because they expect to be judged by them | *Social Psychological and Personality Science*, 3(2) |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: the lifestyle puts people off

The most serious version says: even if the label is unfair, the hippie image makes veganism less attractive, and that harms the cause. It would be better for vegans to look more "normal".

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **It's a question of communication, not of truth.** It can be useful to think about how the message lands, but that doesn't change whether the message is right (K8).
2. **The image no longer matches reality.** There are vegans of every profile, and showing that works better than asking everyone to fit one mold.
3. **Whoever uses the label isn't assessing the argument.** The objection serves to end the conversation, not to improve communication.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Even if every vegan were a hippie, the question would remain the same: is it just to make animals suffer and die for the pleasure of taste? The answer doesn't depend on who asks it.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in L2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Ad hominem | Attacks the image of the advocate, not the argument |
| Logical | Hasty generalization | Attributes to all vegans the style of some |
| Epistemological | Stereotype | Uses a label instead of information about the group |
| Moral | Changing the subject | Swaps the question about animals for one about people |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Doesn't judge other ideas by the looks of their advocates.** No one rejects medicine because a doctor has long hair.
2. **Knows vegans who aren't hippies.** The stereotype doesn't describe even the people the person knows.
3. **Recognizes that other groups are victims of stereotypes.** But uses one against vegans.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains L2 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stereotype and prejudice | A label replaces assessment of the argument | [MacInnis and Hodson, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/1368430215618253) |
| Do-gooder derogation | Ridiculing those with a moral stance eases the feeling of being judged | [Minson and Monin, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550611415695) |
| In-group bias | The out-group is seen as homogeneous | Literature on out-group homogeneity |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "an idea held by people with a certain lifestyle doesn't deserve to be taken seriously".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Civil rights | Causes championed by long-haired students in the 1960s would be dismissible | Many of them are now consensus |
| Science | A discovery made by an eccentric would be false | The history of science shows otherwise |
| Any cause | Labeling its advocates would be enough to refute it | No one accepts this for their own causes |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's right that veganism has historical roots intersecting with the counterculture, and that a movement's public image affects how its ideas are received. Thinking about communication is legitimate.

But the image of those who hold an idea doesn't decide whether it's true. Animals feel pain whoever says so.
