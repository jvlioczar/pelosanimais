# G19. "Fruitarianism isn't healthy, so veganism isn't either"

**Thesis:** veganism isn't fruitarianism. An all-fruit diet is genuinely restrictive and risky, especially for children, and it's honest to say so. But a vegan diet includes beans, lentils, grains, nuts, seeds, vegetables, fortified foods and a vitamin B12 supplement, and nutrition bodies consider it, when well planned, adequate at every stage of life. Criticizing the most extreme diet in the group says nothing about the others.

**Family:** G. Health and nutrition · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Those people who only eat fruit get sick. That's veganism; it isn't healthy."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** All-fruit diets cause deficiencies in protein, vitamin B12, calcium, iron, zinc and other nutrients.
2. **P2.** Fruitarianism is a form of plant-based eating, advocated by some vegans.
3. **P3.** If one form of plant-based eating is dangerous, the others probably are too.
4. **C.** So veganism isn't healthy.

P1 is true, and vegans shouldn't deny it. P2 is true only for a minority. The problem lies in P3, which generalizes from the most restrictive diet to all of them.

The argument is **invalid**: from the flaws of one specific diet, the flaws of every diet without animal products don't follow.

## 2. How it can show up

| Context | Typical form |
| --- | --- |
| Everyday conversation | "People who only eat fruit get sick." |
| Everyday conversation | "Veganism is fruit and salad; that doesn't sustain anyone." |

| Disguised version | "I know someone who got sick being vegan." (which is G13) |

## 3. Answer for children

Imagine someone who eats only bread, nothing else. Their body would be missing a lot, wouldn't it? That doesn't mean bread is bad, or that people who eat bread are wrong. It means eating just one thing isn't enough. It's the same with vegans: eating only fruit isn't good, but vegans eat beans, rice, vegetables, nuts and lots more.

## 4. Short answer

I agree that eating only fruit isn't healthy, especially for children. But that isn't veganism. A vegan diet has beans, lentils, chickpeas, rice, nuts, vegetables and a B12 supplement, and nutrition bodies consider it, when well planned, adequate at every stage of life. It would be like saying eating meat is harmful because a strict carnivore diet is risky.

**Question to ask back:** "If someone ate only meat and got sick, would you conclude that every diet with meat is dangerous?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument takes the part for the whole:

| Diet | What it includes | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fruitarianism | Mostly fruit, sometimes nuts and seeds | Restrictive; risk of deficiencies, especially in children |
| A planned vegan diet | Legumes, grains, nuts, seeds, vegetables, fruit, fortified foods, B12 supplement | Considered adequate at every stage of life by nutrition bodies |
| Veganism (as an ethical position) | Not exploiting animals, as far as possible and practicable | Not a specific diet |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Veganism isn't a specific diet.** It's an ethical position against the exploitation of animals. There are many ways to eat without animal products, and most vegans aren't fruitarians.

**2. The criticism of fruitarianism is valid, and doesn't reach the rest.** All-fruit diets can cause deficiencies in protein, B12, calcium, iron, zinc and other nutrients, and they're unsuitable for children. That says something about that diet, not about eating beans and rice.

**3. The position of nutrition bodies.** The Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, the largest association of nutrition professionals in the United States, states that appropriately planned vegetarian diets, including vegan diets, are healthful and nutritionally adequate at every stage of life. The crucial point is planning, including B12 (G4).

**4. The same logic would apply to meat eaters.** A strict carnivore diet, meat only, is also restrictive and has risks. No one would conclude from it that every diet with meat is dangerous (G16).

**5. Vegans shouldn't romanticize fruitarianism either.** Saying "humans are frugivores by nature" is a weak argument this guide asks people to stop using (V30). Defending animals doesn't require defending extreme diets.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Melina, Craig and Levin, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2016.09.025) | Appropriately planned vegetarian, including vegan, diets are healthful, nutritionally adequate and appropriate for all stages of life | Position of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics |
| [NIH, Office of Dietary Supplements, vitamin B12](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/) | Natural sources of B12 are limited to animal foods; vegans need fortified foods or supplements | |
| [Wikipedia, "Fruitarianism"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruitarianism) | Gathers sources on the risks of fruitarianism: deficiencies in protein, B12, calcium, iron, zinc and fatty acids, and unsuitability for children | Used as an overview, with the sources cited in the article |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: plant-based diets demand too much care

The most serious version says: even if veganism isn't fruitarianism, any diet without animal products requires planning and supplementation, and many people don't do it right. In practice there would be more risk than with the usual diet.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Every diet requires some care.** Usual diets also have common problems, such as too much salt and ultra-processed food and too little fiber.
2. **Supplementing is normal.** Iodized salt, fortified flour and the supplementation of farm animals themselves show that fortifying and supplementing are part of many diets.
3. **Difficulty isn't impossibility.** That some people plan poorly doesn't show the diet is inadequate for those who plan well.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Even if a vegan diet required more attention, the question would remain the same: does the effort of planning one's meals justify breeding and killing animals that feel? Health can correct facts about diet, but it doesn't decide whether it's just to exploit someone.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in G19 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Composition, part for the whole | Takes the most restrictive diet as if it were all of veganism |
| Logical | Straw man | Attacks fruitarianism and concludes against veganism |
| Epistemological | Generalizing from extreme cases | Uses cases of malnutrition as the rule |
| Moral | Health as a moral justification | Uses a nutritional risk to dismiss the question of harm to animals |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Doesn't judge every diet with meat by the strict carnivore diet.** But judges all of veganism by fruitarianism.
2. **Accepts supplements and fortified foods.** Such as iodized salt and enriched flour.
3. **Eats fruit, beans and rice.** Vegan foods that are part of their own diet.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains G19 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Availability heuristic | Extreme, shocking cases stick in memory | General literature on availability |
| Rationalization through the 4Ns | "Necessary" justifies continuing to eat animals | [Piazza et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.appet.2015.04.011) |
| Confirmation bias | Seeks the worst examples to confirm one's opinion | General literature on confirmation bias |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if the most extreme version of a diet is harmful, every version is".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diets with meat | The strict carnivore diet would condemn every diet with meat | No one accepts this |
| Fasting | The risks of prolonged fasting would condemn skipping a meal | Absurd |
| Sport | The risks of ultramarathons would condemn walking | Absurd |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's right that fruitarianism is a risky diet, which can cause serious deficiencies and is unsuitable for children. Vegans shouldn't promote it as a model or use the idea that humans are "frugivores by nature".

But veganism isn't fruitarianism. A varied, well-planned vegan diet with B12 is considered adequate by nutrition bodies. And even if it required more care, that wouldn't answer the question of what we do to animals.
