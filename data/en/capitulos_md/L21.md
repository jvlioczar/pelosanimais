# L21. "Comparing animal farming to slavery or the Holocaust is offensive"

**Thesis:** the objection is substantially right: these comparisons can hurt people and communities who suffered atrocities, and they should be made with great care, or avoided. But structural analogy must be distinguished from moral equivalence. Pointing out similarities of mechanism, such as treating beings as things, isn't saying the victims are worth the same or that the suffering is equal. And the case for animals doesn't depend on these comparisons.

**Family:** L. Attacks on veganism and vegans · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "You trivialize the Holocaust. That comparison is racist."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Slavery and the Holocaust were atrocities against human beings, whose memory must be respected.
2. **P2.** Comparing these atrocities with the treatment of animals can suggest the human victims are worth no more than animals.
3. **P3.** That suggestion offends descendants and survivors, and reproduces the historical dehumanization they suffered.
4. **C.** So these comparisons are offensive and should be rejected.

P1 is true. P2 and P3 point to a real risk: in many situations, these comparisons hurt. The problem is concluding from this that the question of animals can be dismissed.

The argument is **valid** as a criticism of a way of communicating. It fails when used to reject the moral question about animals.

## 3. Answer for children

Some very sad things happened to people in history, and it's important to remember them with respect. Comparing those tragedies with other things can hurt those who suffered. That's why it's better to talk about animals without using those comparisons. Animals deserve care for their own sake.

## 4. Short answer

These comparisons can hurt, and it's fair to ask for care with them, or to avoid them. But two things need separating: pointing out similarities of mechanism, such as treating sentient beings as things, isn't saying the victims are worth the same. Some people close to these atrocities have made such comparisons themselves. In any case, the defense of animals doesn't depend on these analogies: it stands on its own.

**Question to ask back:** "Setting the comparisons aside, what do you think of what happens to the animals?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument mixes a legitimate criticism of form with a rejection of content:

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Can these comparisons hurt? | Yes, and they should be used with care or avoided |
| Is comparing mechanisms equating victims? | Not necessarily: analogy isn't equivalence |
| Does the question of animals depend on these comparisons? | No |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Analogy isn't equivalence.** Comparing structures, such as objectification, property and the denial of interests, isn't claiming the victims are worth the same.

**2. Criticizing the form doesn't refute the content.** If the comparison is inappropriate, the answer is not to use it, not to dismiss the question of animals.

**3. Some people close to these atrocities made the comparison.** Writers and people who lived through atrocities have drawn comparisons of this kind. That doesn't oblige anyone to make them, but it shows they aren't necessarily disrespectful.

**4. The case for animals stands without analogies.** Sentience, the needlessness of the harm and moral consistency are enough (A8).

**5. Care is a duty for those who defend animals.** Especially toward communities still living with the consequences of these atrocities.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Spiegel, *The Dreaded Comparison* (1988)](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3235509W) | Discusses the comparison between human slavery and animal exploitation | Mirror Books; written by an author who defends the comparison |
| [Patterson, *Eternal Treblinka* (2002)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternal_Treblinka) | Discusses historical parallels between the treatment of animals and the Holocaust | Lantern Books; a controversial work |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: the harm of the comparison outweighs the benefit

The most serious version says that even if the analogy is structurally valid, it hurts so much and alienates so many people that it shouldn't be used.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **It may be right about strategy.** Many animal advocates agree and avoid these comparisons.
2. **It's about communication, not about the animals.** The moral question remains.
3. **There are other ways to speak.** Animal suffering can be described on its own terms.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Even without any historical comparison, the moral question remains: animals feel, and we make them suffer and die without need. Is that fair?

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in L21 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Confusing analogy with equivalence | Every comparison is read as equality |
| Logical | Changing the subject | From the question of animals to the way of speaking |
| Moral | Dismissing the question | The inappropriateness of the comparison becomes a reason to ignore the animals |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Uses analogies on other subjects.** Without implying equivalence.
2. **Does the person accept the question without the comparisons?** If not, the objection wasn't only about form.
3. **Doesn't care about the animals when no comparison is made.**

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains L21 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affect heuristic | The emotional reaction to the comparison replaces assessment | General literature on the affect heuristic |
| Speciesism | Any comparison between humans and animals seems offensive | [Caviola, Everett and Faber, 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29517258/) |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a comparison offends, the question being compared can be dismissed".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exaggerated political comparisons | Political questions could be ignored | No one concludes this |
| Medical analogies | Diseases compared to wars would stop mattering | Absurd |
| Animals | The question of animals could be ignored | The same error |

## 11. What the argument gets right

This objection is largely right. Comparisons with slavery and the Holocaust can deeply hurt people and communities still living with the consequences of these atrocities, and animal advocates have used them disrespectfully. Care here is a duty.

But criticizing the form doesn't erase the content. The defense of animals can and should be made without instrumentalizing human suffering, and it still stands.
