# K10. "Vegans will always be a minority"

**Thesis:** being a minority doesn't make a position wrong, and the number of people who agree with something doesn't decide whether it's just. Many moral changes began with minorities that seemed to have no chance. And you don't need to be a majority to reduce suffering: each person who stops buying animal products reduces demand, and animal protection laws have been passed without a vegan majority.

**Family:** K. Effectiveness and individual responsibility · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "You'll always be a minority. The world will never go vegan, so why bother?"

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Vegans are a small part of the population, and that has changed little over the decades.
2. **P2.** Social change only happens when the majority comes on board.
3. **P3.** It's unlikely the majority will become vegan in the near future.
4. **C.** So veganism won't change anything, and it isn't worth adopting.

P1 is true today almost everywhere. P3 is plausible. The problem lies in P2 and the conclusion: important changes happen without a majority, and the question of what's right doesn't depend on how many agree.

The argument is **invalid** as a moral objection: from a position being rare it doesn't follow that it's wrong or useless.

## 3. Answer for children

Sometimes only a few children in the class think it isn't right to leave a classmate out of a game. They're few, but they're right, and often the others end up changing their minds. Being few doesn't mean being wrong. And even if the others don't change, whoever doesn't leave the classmate out is already doing the right thing.

## 4. Short answer

Being a minority doesn't make something wrong. Many moral changes began with a few people who seemed to have no chance. And you don't need to be a majority to reduce suffering: each person who stops buying animal products lowers demand, and animal protection laws have been passed without a vegan majority.

**Question to ask back:** "If something is unjust, does it stop being unjust because few people oppose it?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument mixes two questions:

| Question | Type | Depends on the number of supporters? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is it just to exploit animals? | Moral | No |
| Will veganism become the majority? | Prediction | Yes, by definition |

The second question is interesting, but it doesn't answer the first.

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Popularity doesn't decide what's right.** That a position is held by few doesn't show it's wrong. It's the appeal to the majority (D6).

**2. Change begins with minorities.** Many moral and legal changes, such as ending practices once accepted by the majority, began with small groups. In experiments on social conventions, a committed minority of about 25% managed to change the group's norm. It's a laboratory result, not a prediction, but it shows norms can tip without a majority.

**3. You don't need a majority to reduce harm.** Each person who stops buying animal products reduces, on average, demand and the number of animals bred (K1). The effect exists even if veganism never becomes the majority.

**4. Laws change before the majority does.** The European Union banned battery cages for laying hens in a 1999 directive, and the United Kingdom recognized the sentience of several kinds of animals in law in 2022, without a vegan majority in either place.

**5. The prediction may be wrong.** Technologies such as cultivated meat and precision fermentation may make products free of exploitation cheaper and more common. There's no guarantee, but there's no guarantee of the opposite either (K5).

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Centola et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aas8827) | In experiments, a committed minority of about 25% changed the convention adopted by the group | *Science*, 360; a laboratory result |
| [European Union, Directive 1999/74/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/1999/74/oj) | Lays down standards for laying hens, banning conventional battery cages | |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: without a majority, suffering stays almost the same

The most serious version says: even if each vegan reduces demand a little, as long as the majority eats meat, billions of animals will keep being killed. The individual effort would be almost irrelevant next to the scale.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Almost irrelevant isn't irrelevant.** For each animal that isn't bred, the difference is total.
2. **The argument works against any cause.** No individual ends hunger or poverty alone, and helping still makes sense (K1).
3. **The moral question remains.** Even if it were useless, taking part in something unjust would still be taking part in something unjust.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Even if vegans were always a minority, the question would be the same: is it just to make animals suffer and die for the pleasure of taste? The number of people who agree doesn't change the answer.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in K10 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Appeal to the majority | Uses the number of supporters as a criterion of truth |
| Logical | False dilemma | Either veganism becomes the majority, or it changes nothing |
| Epistemological | Prediction taken as certainty | Treats the future as already decided |
| Moral | Confusing effectiveness with justice | From the chance of winning it concludes what's right to do |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Admires those who championed unpopular causes in the past.** But uses unpopularity against veganism.
2. **Votes, donates or helps without changing everything alone.** And demands that veganism change everything at once.
3. **Recognizes that social norms change.** And assumes this one never will.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains K10 | Evidence |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a position is a minority one, it's wrong or useless".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unpopular causes of the past | Those who championed them as a minority would have been wrong | History judges otherwise in many cases |
| Donations | Giving would be useless, because most people don't give | Each donation helps someone |
| Any social change | None could ever begin | Every change starts with a few |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's right that vegans are a minority today, that deep cultural change is slow and that the majority probably won't become vegan in the near future. It's also reasonable to think about strategies that reduce suffering even without mass adoption, such as laws, technologies and institutional change.

But being a minority doesn't decide what's right, and you don't need to be a majority to make a difference. Each animal that isn't bred to die is a real difference.
