# H14. "Sustainable fishing and aquaculture solve it"

**Thesis:** fishing and aquaculture have problems no label solves. According to the FAO, more than a third of marine fish stocks are overfished, and the share has been rising. Bycatch kills dolphins, turtles and birds. Aquaculture uses meal and oil from wild fish as feed. And above all, fish are sentient beings, killed by the billions through suffocation or crushing.

**Family:** H. Environment, ecology and agriculture · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "I buy certified fish. Farmed fish don't affect the ocean."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Some fish stocks are well managed and certified as sustainable.
2. **P2.** Aquaculture can reduce pressure on wild fish.
3. **P3.** So it's possible to eat fish without depleting the oceans.
4. **C.** So eating sustainable fish isn't a problem.

P1 and P2 are true in some cases. The problem lies in the conclusion: even well-managed fishing has bycatch, aquaculture depends on wild fish, and the argument ignores the suffering of the fish themselves.

The argument is **valid** as far as fish stocks go. Its **soundness** fails when it concludes there's no problem.

## 3. Answer for children

Many fish are disappearing from the sea because people catch too many. Dolphins and turtles also get caught in the nets by accident. And fish raised in tanks eat feed made from other fish from the sea. Besides, fish feel pain and fear, like other animals.

## 4. Short answer

According to the FAO, more than a third of marine fish stocks are overfished, and the share keeps growing. Even well-managed fishing kills dolphins, turtles and birds as bycatch. Aquaculture uses meal and oil from wild fish in feed. And fish are sentient beings, killed by the billions, often by suffocation. No label changes that.

**Question to ask back:** "Does the sustainability label say anything about how the fish died?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument talks about the stock, not the fish:

| Problem | Does "sustainable" fishing solve it? |
| --- | --- |
| Overfishing | Partly, in well-managed stocks |
| Bycatch | Not entirely |
| Aquaculture feed made from wild fish | No |
| The fish's suffering and death | No |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Overfishing is large and growing.** According to the FAO's 2024 report, more than a third of marine fish stocks are overfished, an increase on the previous report.

**2. Bycatch continues.** Nets and hooks catch non-target species, including dolphins, turtles, sharks and seabirds.

**3. Aquaculture depends on the sea.** Many farmed fish, such as salmon, are carnivores and still eat feed with meal and oil from wild fish, although the share of plant ingredients has been growing.

**4. Fish are sentient.** The New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness recognizes a realistic possibility of consciousness in all vertebrates, including fish (A2).

**5. The number of fish killed is enormous.** Estimates suggest that between about 1 and 2.7 trillion wild fish are caught each year, many killed by suffocation or crushing.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [MSC, on the FAO's SOFIA 2024 report](https://www.msc.org/en-us/media-center/blog/news/2024/07/08/four-things-we-learned-about-the-state-of-the-worlds-fisheries) | The share of overfished stocks increased compared with the previous report; more than a third of monitored stocks aren't fished sustainably | Checked from the summary; the MSC is a fisheries certifier |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: herbivorous farmed fish

The most serious version grants the problems with fishing, but says herbivorous farmed fish, such as tilapia, don't depend on wild fish and have a low environmental impact.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Environmental impact is only part of it.** Farmed fish suffer from high stocking density, disease and slaughter often without stunning.
2. **There's pollution and disease.** Aquaculture can pollute rivers and spread disease to wild fish.
3. **There are plant alternatives.** Protein and omega-3 can come from plants and algae (G7).

### (f) Back to the moral question

Even if fishing were perfectly sustainable, every fish caught would be a sentient individual killed. Sustainability talks about the species and the stock, not the fish. The moral question remains: what justifies its death when there are alternatives?

| Level | Error | How it appears in H14 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Shift of focus | Talks about the stock, not the fish |
| Epistemological | Overconfidence in labels | Assumes certification solves every problem |
| Moral | The individual treated as a resource | The fish becomes part of a stock to be managed |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Doesn't always buy certified fish.** Eating out, they rarely know where it came from.
2. **Cares about dolphins and turtles.** Which die as bycatch.
3. **Ignores aquaculture feed.** Which comes from the sea.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains H14 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mind denial | Fish seem to have less mind than other animals | [Bastian et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21980158/) |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a population is well managed, killing its individuals isn't a problem".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stray dogs | Killing dogs would be acceptable if the population held steady | No one accepts this |
| Wild animals | Hunting would be acceptable whenever the population was stable | This is the argument of H16 |
| Fish | Fishing would be acceptable if the stock held steady | Ignores the individual |

## 11. What the argument gets right

Concern for the oceans is legitimate, and there are real differences between well-managed and predatory fishing. Labels and management policies can reduce environmental harm, and some forms of aquaculture have a lower impact.

But the sustainability of the stock isn't the sustainability of the fish. Each fish is someone who feels, and no label changes the fact that they were killed.
