# H2. "Harvests kill animals; vegans kill too"

**Thesis:** it's true that agriculture kills animals, such as rodents, insects and birds, during planting and harvest. But livestock farming uses far more cropland, because the animals eat feed, as well as using enormous pastures. About 80% of the world's farmland serves the production of meat, dairy and farmed fish. Eating plants directly tends to cause fewer harvest deaths than eating animals fed on plants.

**Family:** H. Environment, ecology and agriculture · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "How many mice died for your salad? Vegans have blood on their hands too."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Agriculture kills animals during soil preparation, planting, pesticide application and harvest.
2. **P2.** Vegans eat agricultural products.
3. **P3.** So vegans also cause animals' deaths.
4. **C.** So the vegan diet isn't morally superior.

P1, P2 and P3 are true. The problem lies in the conclusion: it ignores that eating animals requires even more agriculture, and therefore even more harvest deaths, on top of the deaths of the farmed animals themselves.

The argument is **valid** to show that no diet is entirely innocent. Its **soundness** fails when it concludes the diets are equivalent.

## 3. Answer for children

When machines harvest the fields, some little animals can die. That's sad. But farm animals eat a lot of crops. So to make meat, even more has to be planted, and more little animals die in the harvest. Eating the plants directly means fewer little animals die.

## 4. Short answer

It's true: agriculture kills animals. But livestock farming needs much more agriculture, because the animals eat feed, and it occupies immense pastures. About 80% of the world's farmland serves the production of meat, dairy and farmed fish. Eating plants directly uses less land and tends to cause fewer harvest deaths. The question isn't which diet is perfect, but which causes fewer deaths.

**Question to ask back:** "If harvests kill animals, doesn't eating animals fed on harvests kill even more?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument compares the vegan diet with a cost-free diet, not with the real diet with meat:

| Diet | Harvest deaths | Deaths of farmed animals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vegan | From crops for human food | None |
| With meat from feed-fed animals | From crops for human food and from crops for feed, which is larger | Billions of animals a year |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Livestock farming uses most of the farmland.** According to Our World in Data, about 80% of the world's farmland, between pasture and feed crops, serves the production of meat, dairy and farmed fish, which provide only 17% of calories.

**2. The animals eat feed.** To get one calorie of meat, the animal must be given several calories of feed. This multiplies the area planted and, with it, tends to multiply harvest deaths. Estimates of how many animals die per hectare are very uncertain, but the difference in area is large enough to matter.

**3. A vegan diet would greatly reduce farmland.** According to Poore and Nemecek, a global vegan diet would reduce farmland by about 75%.

**4. There are also the deaths of the farmed animals themselves.** On top of harvest deaths, livestock farming directly kills tens of billions of land animals a year.

**5. The argument confuses accidental and intentional deaths.** Even where there are harvest deaths, they're side effects, while slaughter is the goal (F4).

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Our World in Data, land for agriculture](https://ourworldindata.org/global-land-for-agriculture) | Livestock uses 80% of farmland and provides 17% of calories and 38% of protein | Global data |
| [Our World in Data, land use and diets](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use-diets) | A global vegan diet would reduce farmland by about 75% | Based on Poore and Nemecek, 2018 |
| [Matheny, 2003, *Least Harm*, record in the FAO's AGRIS database](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65dfadbd0f3e94b9e5db4c33) | Replies to Davis (2003): per-hectare counts ignore how many people each area feeds; corrected, they favor vegetarianism | Checked from the abstract |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: pasture-raised meat may kill less

The most serious version, defended by the philosopher Steven Davis in 2003, says cattle raised only on pasture, without feed, would cause fewer wild animal deaths per calorie than crops, because pastures aren't harvested.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **The numbers were disputed.** Matheny showed that Davis compared deaths per hectare, not per calorie produced. Per calorie, crops for direct human consumption come out better.
2. **Pasture requires far more land.** Pasture-raised meat uses an enormous area, often won through deforestation (H1).
3. **Almost no one eats only pasture-raised meat.** Most meat comes from animals fed on feed.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The argument recognizes that the death of animals matters, and that's the central point of veganism. If it matters, the consistent choice is the one that causes fewer deaths. The moral question returns: if eating plants kills fewer, why choose what kills more?

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in H2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Tu quoque | Accuses the other instead of comparing diets |
| Logical | Nirvana fallacy | Since no diet is perfect, all would be equivalent |
| Epistemological | Leaving out feed | Ignores that the animals eat crops |
| Moral | Confusing accidental and intentional | Equates harvest deaths with slaughter |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Says they care about the mice in the harvest.** But eats animals raised and slaughtered.
2. **Ignores the cropland for feed.** Which is larger than that for human food.
3. **Wouldn't reduce their own contribution.** Even knowing that eating plants would cause fewer deaths.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if every option causes some harm, they're all equivalent".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transport | A bicycle and a truck would pollute equally, because both cause some harm | No one concludes this |
| Energy | Solar and coal would be equivalent | No one concludes this |
| Diets | Vegan and meat-eating diets would be equivalent | The data show the opposite |

## 11. What the argument gets right

The argument gets one important thing right: agriculture kills animals, and no diet is entirely innocent. Vegans should recognize this and support farming practices that reduce those deaths. The philosophical debate on the subject is serious.

But comparing real diets, eating plants directly uses less land and causes fewer deaths. If harvest deaths matter, they're one more reason to stop eating animals.
