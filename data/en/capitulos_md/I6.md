# I6. "Traditional medicine has used animal parts for millennia"

**Thesis:** being old doesn't prove that something works, nor that it's fair. Bear bile is extracted from bears kept in cages for years; more than 12,000 bears are on farms of this kind in China and Vietnam. Rhino horn is made of keratin, the same protein as human fingernails. For the few animal compounds with a real effect, such as ursodeoxycholic acid from bile, synthetic or plant-based versions already exist.

**Family:** I. Other uses: testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companionship · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Traditional Chinese medicine has used bear bile for thousands of years. If it didn't work, nobody would use it."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Traditional medicine has used animal parts for centuries.
2. **P2.** A practice that lasts so long must have some effect.
3. **P3.** Banning these practices disrespects non-Western cultures.
4. **C.** So using animal parts in traditional medicine is acceptable.

P1 is true. P2 is an appeal to tradition: many practices lasted centuries without working. P3 raises a real concern, but respect for cultures doesn't license any harm (D6).

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P2.

## 3. Answer for children

In some places, bears are kept in small cages for many years so people can take a liquid from their bodies to use as medicine. But the medicine from that liquid that really works is already made in labs, without any bear. And a rhino's horn is made of the same material as our fingernails.

## 4. Short answer

Being old doesn't prove that something works, nor that it's fair. Bear bile is extracted from bears caged for years; more than 12,000 are on farms of this kind in China and Vietnam. Rhino horn is made of keratin, the same protein as fingernails. And for the few compounds with a real effect, such as ursodeoxycholic acid, synthetic versions already exist and are used in mainstream medicine.

**Question to ask back:** "If the useful compound in bile is already made without bears, why keep bears caged to extract it?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

| Product | Where it comes from | Alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bear bile | Caged bears, with repeated extraction | Synthetic ursodeoxycholic acid and herbs used in traditional medicine itself |
| Rhino horn | Illegal hunting | No demonstrated effect; it's keratin |
| Pangolin scales | Hunting and trafficking | No demonstrated effect |
| Civet coffee | Caged civets fed coffee cherries | Ordinary coffee |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Tradition isn't proof of efficacy.** Bloodletting and mercury were also used for centuries. Efficacy is checked with studies, not with time in use.

**2. The cost to animals is enormous.** According to Animals Asia, more than 12,000 bears are held on bile farms in China and Vietnam, many for years, in cages, with repeated extractions.

**3. What works already exists without animals.** Ursodeoxycholic acid, the active ingredient in bear bile, is produced synthetically and used in mainstream medicine. Traditional Chinese medicine itself has herbs that practitioners recommend as substitutes.

**4. Respecting a culture doesn't require cruelty.** There are traditional medicine practitioners who support ending the use of animal parts. Criticizing a practice isn't attacking a whole culture (D6).

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Animals Asia, what is bear bile farming](https://www.animalsasia.org/vn/our-work/end-bear-bile-farming/what-is-bear-bile-farming) | More than 12,000 bears held on bile farms in China and Vietnam | Animal protection organization working in the region |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: farming protects wild animals

The most serious version says that, without farms, demand would lead to hunting wild bears, and that farming would be a lesser evil.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Farming keeps demand alive.** Researchers debate whether farms reduce or stimulate hunting, since they normalize the product.
2. **A lesser evil is still an evil.** Bears caged for years suffer, even if others are spared.
3. **There's a better way.** Synthetic and plant substitutes meet the need without any bear.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The question is whether it's fair to keep animals suffering for remedies that don't work or that already exist without them. The age of a custom doesn't answer that question.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in I6 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Appeal to tradition | Time in use is taken as proof of efficacy |
| Epistemological | Not knowing the alternatives | Ignores that the useful compound is already synthesized |
| Moral | Culture as immunity | Any criticism is treated as disrespect |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Trusts tradition.** And rejects other old practices that fell out of use.
2. **Values what's natural.** And accepts animals caged for years.
3. **Respects the culture.** Without listening to the practitioners who call for ending these practices.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains I6 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Appeal to tradition | The old custom seems proven | General literature on argumentation |
| Placebo effect | Improvement attributed to the remedy may come from expectation | General literature on placebo |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a practice is old, it works and is acceptable".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bloodletting and mercury | They'd be good remedies | They were abandoned |
| Hunting rhinos for horns | It would be acceptable | It's a crime and pushed species to the brink of extinction |
| Herbs used in traditional medicine itself | They can be studied and used | No animal involved |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that traditional medicine is part of important cultures, that some of its compounds have real effects, and that criticism from outside can sound disrespectful.

But the useful part already exists without animals, and the part with animals causes enormous suffering. Respecting a culture includes recognizing those within it who want to change these practices.
