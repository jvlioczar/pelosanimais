# F9. "You also kill mosquitoes and rats at home"

**Thesis:** self-defense and real conflict are different from raising animals to eat. A mosquito that transmits dengue or malaria, or a rat that contaminates food, poses a concrete threat; a pig in a shed threatens no one. Even in cases of conflict, animal ethics asks us to prefer non-lethal methods when possible. And if killing mosquitoes were inconsistent, that would be a problem for whoever kills mosquitoes, not a justification for raising and slaughtering billions of animals.

**Family:** F. Edge cases and hypotheticals · **Part:** DIR

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "You use insect repellent. If there's a cockroach, you kill it. So you can't say anything."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Vegans also kill animals, such as mosquitoes, cockroaches and rats, when they cause problems.
2. **P2.** This shows they accept killing animals for human interests.
3. **P3.** Eating meat also serves a human interest.
4. **C.** So vegans can't condemn meat eaters without being inconsistent.

P1 is true in many cases. The problem lies in P2 and P3: the interest in not catching a disease or not having one's home contaminated is very different from the interest in a flavor.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P3, which equates interests of very different weights.

## 3. Answer for children

If a mosquito can give you a disease, keeping it away is protecting yourself. That's different from raising a little pig in a cramped place just to eat it later. In one case, we defend ourselves; in the other, we make the animal be born to die. And when we can, the best thing is to keep the critters away without killing them.

## 4. Short answer

Protecting yourself from a mosquito that carries disease, or from rats that contaminate food, is self-defense against a real threat. Raising an animal to eat isn't: they threaten no one. Even in these cases of conflict, animal ethics asks us to use non-lethal methods when possible, such as screens, repellents and sealing gaps. And if someone were inconsistent about mosquitoes, that wouldn't make it acceptable to raise and slaughter billions of animals.

**Question to ask back:** "Was the pig who became bacon threatening anyone?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument equates very different situations:

| Situation | Is there a threat? | Alternatives | Kind of harm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Disease-carrying mosquito | Yes, to health | Screens, repellents, removing breeding sites | Defense |
| Rats in the house | Yes, to health and food | Sealing gaps, live-catch traps | Defense |
| Pig raised for slaughter | No | Many: other foods | Exploitation |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Self-defense is a recognized exception.** Every ethics accepts defense against real threats, including between humans. That doesn't make it acceptable to harm those who threaten no one.

**2. The interests have different weights.** Avoiding dengue or contamination is a serious interest; the taste of meat is a lesser interest that can be met in other ways (J11).

**3. Animal ethics asks for non-lethal methods.** Prevention, screens, repellents and sealing gaps are preferable to killing. Animal advocates tend to use these methods.

**4. Inconsistency doesn't change what's right.** If someone kills mosquitoes without need, that's their problem, not permission for everyone to cause more harm. This is the tu quoque fallacy.

**5. The logic is different.** Raising animals for food brings billions of animals into existence every year precisely so they can die. Pest control at home responds to a concrete threat that already exists, and even so should prefer prevention and non-lethal methods.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Our World in Data, animals slaughtered](https://ourworldindata.org/how-many-animals-get-slaughtered-every-day) | Billions of animals are slaughtered every year for food | Shows the difference in scale |
| [The Vegan Society, definition of veganism](https://www.vegansociety.com/go-vegan/definition-veganism) | Veganism seeks to exclude exploitation "as far as is possible and practicable" | Defense against a real threat isn't exploitation |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: comfort is also an interest

The most serious version says vegans often kill cockroaches or mosquitoes not because of a real threat, but because they're a nuisance, and that this would be an interest as trivial as taste.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **If it's because of a nuisance, it's wrong too.** The criticism hits the behavior, not the principle.
2. **It doesn't justify meat.** That someone kills a cockroach because it's a nuisance doesn't make raising billions of animals acceptable.
3. **Consistency points toward more care.** The right conclusion is to treat all animals better, not worse.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in F9 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Tu quoque | Accuses the other instead of answering |
| Logical | False analogy | Equates defense against a threat with raising animals to eat |
| Moral | Interests of different weights treated as equal | Health and taste treated as equivalent |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Distinguishes self-defense from aggression between humans.** And doesn't apply that here.
2. **Doesn't think someone who uses repellent may raise animals to kill.** The comparison doesn't convince even the person making it.
3. **Prefers non-lethal methods when it's easy.** Many people shoo insects away instead of killing them.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains F9 | Evidence |

| Hypocrisy hunting | Finding inconsistency in others eases one's own | Linked to L4 |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if I kill an animal who threatens me, I may kill animals who don't threaten me".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-defense between humans | Whoever defended themselves could attack others | No one accepts this |
| Aggressive dogs | If one dog attacks, all dogs could be killed | No one accepts this |
| Wild animals | If one snake is a threat, all snakes could be hunted | Contrary to wildlife protection laws |

## 11. What the argument gets right

The argument points to a real issue: conflicts with animals exist, and killing pests also involves beings who may feel. Animal advocates shouldn't pretend this is irrelevant, and should prefer non-lethal methods whenever possible.

But self-defense against a real threat is different from raising animals to eat. The first case is a hard conflict; the second is a choice that can be avoided.
