# I11. "Trophy hunting funds conservation"

**Thesis:** little trophy hunting money reaches conservation or local communities. A 2013 report on Africa, citing research by pro-hunting bodies, found that hunting companies passed on about 3% of their revenue to communities. And even if the money did arrive, the argument instrumentalizes individuals: it kills an animal to protect the species, as if they were just a specimen.

**Family:** I. Other uses: testing, clothing, entertainment · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Hunters are the biggest conservationists. The license pays the park rangers."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Wildlife conservation needs money.
2. **P2.** Trophy hunters pay a lot for licenses.
3. **P3.** That money funds habitat protection and the fight against poaching.
4. **C.** So trophy hunting benefits conservation.

P1 and P2 are true. The problem lies in P3: the evidence suggests that a small share of the money reaches conservation and communities, and there are ways of funding that don't involve killing.

The argument is **valid** if P3 is true. Its **soundness** fails because P3 is largely false.

## 3. Answer for children

Some people pay lots of money to hunt lions or elephants and take the head home. They say the money protects the other animals. But little of that money reaches the people and parks that look after them. And animals can be protected through wildlife-watching tourism, without killing any.

## 4. Short answer

Little money reaches conservation: a 2013 report on Africa, citing research by pro-hunting bodies, found that hunting companies passed on about 3% of their revenue to local communities. Wildlife-watching tourism generates far more money and jobs without killing. And even if the money did arrive, killing an animal to protect the species treats the individual as a mere specimen.

**Question to ask back:** "If wildlife-watching tourism generates more income without killing, why would conservation need hunting?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument promises benefits the data don't confirm:

| Promise | What the data show |
| --- | --- |
| The revenue funds conservation | A small share reaches protected areas and communities |
| Hunting is important for the economy | The revenue is a tiny fraction of the GDP of the countries studied |
| Without hunting, animals lose their value | Wildlife-watching tourism generates more revenue |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Little money reaches communities.** The 2013 report *The $200 million question* cited research according to which hunting companies passed on about 3% of their revenue to local communities.

**2. The revenue is small for the economies.** The same report estimated trophy hunting revenue as a very small fraction of the GDP of the African countries studied.

**3. Hunting removes important individuals.** Hunters seek the biggest males, with the largest horns or manes, which can affect the social and genetic structure of populations.

**4. Wildlife-watching tourism is an alternative.** It generates ongoing revenue from the same living animals. Not every area attracts tourists, and that's the strongest point for those who defend hunting; the answer is to fund conservation by other means, not to kill animals to pay for it.

**5. The argument instrumentalizes individuals.** Killing an animal to "save the species" treats them as a specimen, not as someone (A10).

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Economists at Large, *The $200 million question* (2013)](https://www.tierimrecht.org/documents/57/Ecolarge-2013-200m-question.pdf) | Hunting companies passed on about 3% of their revenue to local communities; the revenue is a tiny fraction of GDP | Commissioned by animal protection organizations; critics note that some sources aren't public |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: in some areas, hunting is the only income

The most serious version points out that in remote areas unattractive to tourists, hunting may be the only source of income that gives wildlife value and prevents the habitat being turned into pasture.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **That calls for other forms of funding.** International funds and payments for conservation can fill that role.
2. **The case is limited.** It doesn't justify trophy hunting in general.
3. **It's still killing for fun.** The hunter pays for the pleasure of killing, not for conservation.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Even if trophy hunting funded conservation, it would still be killing an animal for pleasure. The moral question is whether it's fair to take a life for fun, and no spreadsheet answers that.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in I11 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Epistemological | Overestimating the benefits | Assumes the revenue reaches conservation |
| Logical | False dichotomy | Either hunting or no funding |
| Moral | The individual as a means | Kills one to protect the species |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Says they care about the species.** But kills its strongest individuals.
2. **Ignores wildlife-watching tourism.** Which generates more income.
3. **Wouldn't accept the logic for other animals.** Such as hunting dogs to fund shelters.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains I11 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motivated reasoning | The pleasure of hunting is justified as conservation | General literature on motivated cognition |
| Social dominance orientation | Killing large animals as a show of power | [Caviola, Everett and Faber, 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29517258/) |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if killing some generates money to protect others, it's justified".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The ivory trade | It would be justified to fund parks | It was banned for encouraging poaching |
| Hunting endangered species | It would be justified if paid for | Many countries ban it |
| Any species | It could be hunted to fund its conservation | The individual disappears |

## 11. What the argument gets right

Conservation needs money, and communities living alongside wildlife need real benefits to support it. In some places, regulated hunting has generated resources and incentives to maintain habitats.

But most of the money doesn't reach where it should, there are alternatives that don't involve killing, and the argument forgets the dead animal. Conservation can be funded without trophies.
