# H21. "If everyone goes vegan, we'll have to clear forests to grow crops"

**Thesis:** it's the other way round. Most of the world's farmland is used to raise animals, as pasture or to grow feed, and that land produces a small share of the calories we eat. According to the largest study of food's impacts, by Poore and Nemecek (2018), animal products occupy 83% of farmland and provide 18% of calories and 37% of protein; a global diet without animal products would reduce farmland by about 76%. Eating plants directly needs far less land than feeding plants to animals and then eating them.

**Family:** H. Environment, ecology and agriculture · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "If everyone goes vegan, there won't be enough land. They'll have to cut down the forest to grow all that soy and beans."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** A vegan diet depends on growing more plants for human consumption.
2. **P2.** Agriculture needs land, and agricultural expansion is one of the causes of deforestation.
3. **P3.** To feed billions of people on plants alone, new areas would have to be cleared.
4. **C.** So mass veganism would cause more deforestation.

P1 and P2 are true. P3 is false: it ignores that most farmland is already used for animals today, including to grow their feed. Without animal farming, that land is no longer needed.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P3.

## 3. Answer for children

To have meat, you have to grow lots of food for the animals to eat their whole lives, and leave lots of space for them too. If we eat the plants directly, we need much less land. That leaves room for forests to grow back.

## 4. Short answer

It's the other way round. Animal products occupy 83% of the world's farmland and provide only 18% of the calories we eat, because much of what is grown becomes feed. A global diet without animal products would reduce farmland by about 76%, according to the study by Poore and Nemecek (2018). Eating plants directly frees up land instead of requiring more.

**Question to ask back:** "If most soy becomes feed, who needs more land: the person who eats the soy or the person who eats the animal that ate it?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

| Use of the world's farmland | Share of area | Share of calories | Share of protein |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Animal products (pasture and feed) | 83% | 18% | 37% |
| Plants for direct human consumption | 17% | 82% | 63% |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Most land already serves animals.** Pastures and feed crops occupy most farmland. That's where the pressure on forests lies.

**2. Animals turn plants into food inefficiently.** An animal eats far more plant calories over their life than they provide as meat, milk or eggs. That's why the same food takes much more land when it passes through an animal.

**3. A plant-based diet frees up land.** According to Poore and Nemecek (2018), a global diet without animal products would reduce farmland by about 76%, an area equivalent to the United States, China, Australia and the European Union combined.

**4. Pasture on land unfit for crops doesn't change the math.** Some pastures are on land that couldn't be farmed (see H10). But that land wouldn't need to be used: it could regenerate as native vegetation.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Poore and Nemecek, 2018, *Science*](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaq0216) | Animal products occupy 83% of farmland and provide 18% of calories; diets without animal products would reduce farmland by about 76% | Dataset of about 38,000 farms in 119 countries |
| [Our World in Data, land use](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use) | Livestock uses most of the world's agricultural land, between pastures and feed crops | Synthesis of FAO data |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: the transition wouldn't be perfect

The most serious version says that in practice, a shift to plant-based diets could come with more monocultures and new land cleared in some regions, even if total area fell.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **The problem would be management, not land.** If the land needed falls sharply, clearing new areas would be a political choice, not a necessity.
2. **Monocultures already exist, and mainly serve feed.** Reducing demand for feed also reduces pressure for monocultures (see H4).
3. **The right comparison is with the current system.** And the current system is the one that takes up the most land.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Even if a plant-based diet needed the same amount of land, the question about animals would remain: is it fair to breed and kill them when we can live well without doing so? The land argument doesn't answer that question, and it gets the facts wrong too.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in H21 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empirical | Ignoring feed | Forgets that most crops feed animals |
| Logical | Reversed cause | Blames veganism for pressure that comes from livestock |
| Moral | Changing the question | Swaps the question about animals for one about land |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Fears deforestation.** And defends the system that clears the most land.
2. **Talks about a shortage of land.** And ignores that most land already serves feed.
3. **Blames vegans' soy.** And most soy becomes feed.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains H21 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invisibility of feed | Consumers see the soy field, not the animal that eats it | General literature on perception of impact |
| Status quo | The current system seems like the default, and change seems like the risk | General literature on decision-making |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "eating plants directly takes more land than eating animals fed on plants".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All the world's soy | Eating the soy would take more land than eating the chicken that ate it | The data show the opposite |
| A global plant-based diet | It would need more farmland | It would cut about 76% |
| Reforestation | It would be impossible without livestock | It's livestock that occupies the land |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that agriculture causes deforestation, that monocultures have serious impacts, and that a food transition needs to be well planned.

But most farmland already serves animals, and a plant-based diet would free up most of it. Veganism reduces pressure on forests rather than increasing it.
