# C11. "Cows need to be milked"

**Thesis:** a cow only produces milk after giving birth, and only produces so much milk because she was bred for it. The "need" to milk is created by the industry itself: it makes the cow pregnant, separates the calf, who would be the one drinking the milk, and then presents milking as a favor. It isn't help; it's a problem production created and the profit it makes from it.

**Family:** C. Nature, biology and evolution · **Part:** DIR

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "If you don't milk her, the cow suffers. We're helping the cow."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Dairy cows produce large amounts of milk.
2. **P2.** If the milk isn't removed, the udder becomes full, painful and prone to inflammation.
3. **P3.** Removing the milk relieves that suffering.
4. **C.** So milking is care for the cow, not exploitation.

P2 and P3 have some truth: a dairy cow who is already producing needs to be milked. The problem is the question the argument doesn't ask: why is she producing?

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P1, which presents as natural a production caused by induced pregnancy and genetic selection.

## 3. Answer for children

Cows, like human mothers, only make milk when they have a baby. The milk is for the calf. On dairy farms, the cow is made pregnant on purpose, the calf is taken away and people keep the milk. Saying we "help" the cow by taking her milk forgets that the milk was for her baby.

## 4. Short answer

Cows only give milk after giving birth, like any mammal. For that, they're made pregnant again and again, usually by artificial insemination, and the calves are usually separated from them soon after birth. They produce so much because they were bred for it. Milking solves a problem that production itself created. Helping the cow would be not putting her in that situation.

**Question to ask back:** "If the cow needs to be milked, why can't the calf drink the milk?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument starts in the middle of the story:

| What the argument shows | What came before |
| --- | --- |
| The cow has a full udder | She was made pregnant to produce milk |
| The milk has to be removed | The calf, who would drink it, was separated |
| She produces a lot of milk | She was genetically selected to produce far more than a calf needs |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. There's no milk without pregnancy.** Cows are mammals: they only produce milk after giving birth. To keep production going, the industry has to make them pregnant again and again.

**2. The milk belongs to the calf.** In dairy production, calves are usually separated from their mothers in the first hours or first day of life. Early separation is standard practice in much of the industry, precisely so the milk can be sold.

**3. We created the overproduction.** The amount of milk modern dairy cows produce results from genetic selection to produce far more than a calf would drink. It's the same pattern as broiler chickens (C10): the animal's body was shaped by production.

**4. Production has costs for the cow.** The European scientific authority lists mastitis, mobility problems and restricted movement among the main welfare consequences for dairy cows.

**5. The end is slaughter.** When production drops, the dairy cow is sent to slaughter, and the male calves, who won't produce milk, often become veal (B5).

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [EFSA, *Welfare of dairy cows* (2023)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10186071/) | Identifies mobility problems, mastitis, restricted movement and metabolic disorders among the main welfare consequences | Official EU scientific opinion |
| [RSPCA Australia, calf separation](https://kb.rspca.org.au/knowledge-base/why-are-calves-separated-from-their-mother-in-the-dairy-industry/) | Standard industry practice is to separate the calf from its mother within 24 hours of birth | An animal welfare organization that doesn't oppose drinking milk |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: the cows that already exist need milking

The most serious version grants all of this, but says that, as long as there are dairy cows in production, milking them is necessary, and that stopping drinking milk wouldn't help those cows.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Milking today doesn't require buying milk tomorrow.** Consumption funds new pregnancies, new separated calves and new cows in production.
2. **A cow who isn't made pregnant again stops producing.** The "need" ends when the cycle isn't renewed.
3. **Helping existing cows is different from exploiting new ones.** Sanctuaries also milk cows who arrive lactating, and they don't sell the milk.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in C11 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Omitted cause | Presents the effect, the full udder, without the cause, the induced pregnancy |
| Logical | Appeal to nature | Treats as natural a production created by selection |
| Epistemological | Not knowing the biology | Ignores that there's only milk after giving birth |
| Moral | Exploitation presented as care | Calls use help |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Knows mammals only produce milk after giving birth.** No one thinks women produce milk without having had children.
2. **Wouldn't "help" the cow by letting the calf drink.** Which shows the goal is the milk, not the cow.
3. **Accepts slaughtering the cow when production drops.** The care ends when the profit ends.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if we create a need, meeting it is a favor".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Any induced dependence | Whoever creates the problem would be a benefactor for relieving it | No one accepts this |
| Laying hens | Collecting the eggs would be a favor to a bird bred to lay almost one a day | Same pattern |
| Breastfeeding women | Taking a mother's milk to sell would be helping her | Absurd |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that a lactating cow needs her milk removed, and that leaving her with a full udder would cause pain. People who look after dairy cows often care about them sincerely, and good milking prevents real suffering.

But that need isn't natural or inevitable: it's created with each new induced pregnancy. Real care would be to stop putting cows in that situation.
