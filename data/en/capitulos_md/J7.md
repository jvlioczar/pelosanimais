# J7. "Vegan food is bland"

**Thesis:** it's a generalization born of unfamiliarity. The flavor of food comes mainly from seasonings, techniques and combinations of ingredients, and almost all seasonings are plants: garlic, onion, chilies, herbs, spices. Many traditional dishes from around the world are already vegan or nearly so: chana masala, falafel, dal, pasta arrabbiata, bean chili, Ethiopian lentil stews, Brazilian *acarajé*. Vegan food isn't "just salad". And most of what people already eat is plants: according to FAO data, about 83% of the calories available worldwide come from plants, and even in high-meat countries such as the United States, Australia and Brazil, about 72%. There are also signs that the "vegan" label itself makes food seem less tasty before the first bite.

**Family:** J. Practicality, economy and society · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Just salad? Rabbit food. Am I supposed to eat grass?"

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Meat and dairy give flavor, texture and satiety to many dishes.
2. **P2.** Without them, many recipes are less tasty.
3. **P3.** The pleasure of eating is important for quality of life.
4. **C.** So a vegan diet reduces quality of life.

P1 and P3 are true. P2 is true for recipes built around meat, but not for well-made plant cooking. The problem lies in the conclusion: it generalizes from bad examples or from unfamiliarity.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P2, generalized.

## 3. Answer for children

What makes food tasty is the seasoning: garlic, onion, herbs, chili, oregano, cumin. All of that comes from plants! Well-seasoned beans, fries, popcorn, pasta with tomato sauce, bread, fruit: all of that is vegan and really tasty. Even on the plate of someone who eats meat, almost everything comes from plants: the rice, the bread, the potatoes, the salad and the seasonings.

## 4. Short answer

Have you tried well-made vegan food? Almost all the flavor in food comes from seasonings and techniques, and seasonings are plants: garlic, onion, chilies, herbs, spices. Many traditional dishes are already vegan or nearly so: well-seasoned beans, falafel, chana masala, dal, pasta arrabbiata, bean chili, garlic bread. Vegan food isn't "just salad". And much of what you eat every day is already plants: worldwide, about 83% of available calories come from plants. There's also research suggesting that the "vegan" label on its own lowers expectations of taste. And if a meal were a little less tasty for you, would that outweigh an animal's life?

**Question to ask back:** "Are fries, seasoned beans and pasta with tomato sauce bland?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument confuses the absence of meat with the absence of flavor:

| Source of flavor | Is it a plant? |
| --- | --- |
| Garlic, onion, chilies, herbs, spices | Yes |
| Techniques: sautéing, roasting, grilling, smoking, fermenting | Yes, applicable to plants |
| Umami: tomato, mushrooms, soy sauce, miso | Yes |
| Fat: olive oil, oils, nuts, avocado | Yes |
| The base of the plate: rice, beans, bread, pasta, potatoes, cassava, corn | Yes, and it provides most of the calories |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Seasonings are plants.** Much of what makes food tasty comes from plants: garlic, onion, herbs, chilies and spices.

**2. Most of the plate is already plants.** According to FAO food balance data compiled by Our World in Data, in 2023 about 83% of the calories available for consumption worldwide came from plant foods. Even in high-meat countries the share is above two thirds: about 72% in the United States and Australia, 73% in Brazil and 67% in Germany. The measure is calories in the food supply, not what each person eats or how much room each food takes on the plate, and it includes sugar and oils. Even so, it shows that rice, bread, pasta, potatoes, corn, beans, fruit, vegetables and seasonings already make up most of almost everyone's diet. Meat tends to sit at the center of the plate, but it isn't most of it.

**3. Many traditional dishes are already vegan.** Around the world, there's an enormous variety of dishes without animal products or easily adapted.

**4. It's a generalization.** Often, the person saying it has tried few options, or badly prepared ones.

**5. Much of the flavor we associate with meat comes from technique and seasoning.** Smoked, grilled, browned, sautéed: all of this can be applied to vegetables and plant proteins. The result isn't identical to meat, but it isn't "bland".

**6. Many chefs and restaurants show otherwise.** Plant cooking has gained ground in award-winning restaurants and kitchens around the world.

**7. The label changes expectations.** There's research suggesting that the same food seems less tasty when it's announced as vegan. In a study published in *Appetite* in 2022, putting a "vegan" label on products that were already vegan, but that consumers didn't expect to be, changed how they saw them: according to summaries of the study, the products seemed healthier and more sustainable but less tasty, and intention to consume changed; when people already expected the product to be vegan, the label had no effect. In two field studies at a US university, dishes labeled "vegan" or "vegetarian" were chosen less often (*Appetite*, 2023). And in a burger tasting study published in 2023, the order of preference changed depending on whether participants knew what they were eating: blind, the pea protein burger was preferred to the beef-and-mushroom hybrid, the opposite of what happened in the informed tasting. These studies mostly measure expectations and choices, the effects vary with the product and with the eater's attitudes, and sometimes the product really is worse. But they suggest that part of the "bland" may come from the name, not the food.

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: some flavors are irreplaceable

The most serious version grants that vegan food can be tasty, but says certain flavors, such as a good barbecue or an aged cheese, have no plant equivalent.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **It changes the argument.** It stops being "bland" and becomes "doesn't have exactly that flavor" (J6).
2. **There are new flavors to discover.** The swap isn't only a loss; it's also a discovery.
3. **The pleasure of a flavor doesn't justify a death.** The central argument returns to J6.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Even if some specific flavor were irreplaceable, the question would be whether the pleasure of a dish justifies an animal's life. And in practice, plant cooking offers so much pleasure that the question rarely needs to be asked.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in J7 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Hasty generalization | Judges all vegan cooking by a few experiences |
| Epistemological | Unfamiliarity | Ignores plant dishes and techniques |
| Moral | Pleasure as justification | Flavor outweighs the animal's life |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Eats many vegan dishes.** Without noticing.
2. **Seasons meat with plants.** The flavor comes from them.
3. **Likes fries, popcorn and bread.** Which are vegan.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains J7 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food neophobia | Unfamiliar foods seem less tasty | General literature on food neophobia |
| Rationalization through the 4Ns | "Nice" as a justification | [Piazza et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25865663/) |
| Label effect | The name "vegan" lowers expected taste before the first bite | [Stremmel et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.appet.2022.106048) |
| Loss aversion and the endowment effect | The familiar dish is overvalued against the new | [Kahneman, Knetsch and Thaler, 1990](https://doi.org/10.1086/261737) |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if I tried a bad version, the whole category is bad".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Japanese cuisine | It would be bad because of one poorly made sushi | No one concludes this |
| Dishes with meat | They'd be bland because of one poorly seasoned steak | No one concludes this |
| Vegan food | It would be bland because of one unseasoned salad | The same error |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that there is bland vegan food, just as there is bland food with meat. For people who grew up with meat-centered dishes, early experiences can disappoint, and learning to cook with plants takes some time.

But plant cooking is vast, creative and full of flavor. Most of what makes food tasty already comes from plants.
