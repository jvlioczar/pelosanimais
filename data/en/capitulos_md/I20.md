# I20. "Circus animals are well treated and part of the family"

**Thesis:** many circus performers have a real bond with the animals, but traveling life doesn't meet the needs of animals such as elephants, lions and bears: constant transport, small cages and training for acts they wouldn't do on their own. That's why more than 40 countries restrict the use of wild animals in circuses, and in Brazil several states and cities have banned it. Circuses without animals, such as Cirque du Soleil, show that the show doesn't depend on them.

**Family:** I. Other uses: testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companionship · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Circus animals are part of the family. The performers have looked after them since they were little, they travel together and they're well treated."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Circus animals are cared for by the same families for generations.
2. **P2.** They get food and veterinary care.
3. **P3.** The bond with their trainers shows they don't suffer.
4. **C.** So using animals in circuses is acceptable.

P1 and P2 may be true. P3 confuses bond with welfare: an animal can be attached to those who keep it and still live badly.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P3.

## 3. Answer for children

In the wild, an elephant walks many kilometers a day with its family. At the circus, it travels from city to city in a truck and spends a lot of time locked up, to do tricks it wouldn't do on its own. Today there are beautiful circuses, with acrobats and clowns, and no animals at all.

## 4. Short answer

Many performers have a real bond with the animals, but traveling life doesn't meet the needs of elephants, lions and bears: constant transport, small cages and training for acts they wouldn't do alone. That's why more than 40 countries restrict wild animals in circuses, and in Brazil several states and cities have banned them. Circuses without animals, such as Cirque du Soleil, show the show doesn't depend on them.

**Question to ask back:** "If an elephant walks kilometers a day in the wild, which family can give it that while traveling in a truck?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

| The animal needs | The traveling circus offers |
| --- | --- |
| Large areas to walk and explore | Cages and temporary enclosures |
| Company of its own species | Isolation or artificial groups |
| A stable routine | Constant travel and change |
| Natural behavior | Training for artificial acts |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. A bond isn't welfare.** Animals can become attached to those who keep them and still suffer from confinement and transport. The bond doesn't change the living conditions.

**2. These animals' needs don't fit in a circus.** Elephants, big cats and bears need space, movement and company that traveling life can't provide.

**3. Many countries and cities have decided to ban it.** More than 40 countries restrict the use of wild animals in circuses, and Bolivia was the first to ban all animals, in 2009. Brazil has no federal law, but several states and cities ban it.

**4. The show doesn't depend on animals.** Hugely successful contemporary circuses, such as Cirque du Soleil, have never used animals.

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: circuses with animals are a tradition and a livelihood

The most serious version points out that circus families have lived from this work for generations, and that bans threaten a cultural tradition and these families' livelihood.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **The tradition can continue without animals.** Circus art is much bigger than animal acts (D1).
2. **The livelihood can be kept.** Many circuses have adapted and keep running without animals.
3. **The question is about the animals.** A valuable tradition doesn't license keeping animals in conditions that don't meet their needs.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The question is whether it's fair to keep animals caged and on the road to entertain the public, when the same show can exist without them.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in I20 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Bond as proof of welfare | The animal's attachment is used as if it showed it lives well |
| Epistemological | Not knowing the species' needs | Assumes food and vets are enough |
| Moral | Entertainment above welfare | The show justifies life on the road |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Says the animal is family.** And keeps it in cages and trucks.
2. **Values circus tradition.** And ignores that it carries on without animals.
3. **Wants children to know animals.** And shows animals doing what they'd never do in the wild.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains I20 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Appeal to tradition | A circus with animals seems like the "real" circus | General literature on argumentation |
| Selective anthropomorphism | The animal's attachment is read as satisfaction | General literature on animal cognition |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if the animal is attached to whoever keeps it, its life is good".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chained animals attached to their owner | They'd live well | The attachment doesn't change the chain |
| Bans in more than 40 countries | They'd be unjustified | They were based on welfare |
| Circuses without animals | They carry on the tradition | Without the cost to animals |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that many performers look after the animals with affection, that there are real bonds, and that the circus is an important cultural tradition and many families' livelihood.

But wild animals' needs don't fit a traveling life, and circus art lives on without them.
