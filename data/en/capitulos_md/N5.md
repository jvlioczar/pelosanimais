# N5. "I admire you, but I can't do it"

**Thesis:** this phrase concedes the moral point: if there were nothing admirable, there would be nothing to admire. What remains is the gap between agreeing and doing, which is usually smaller than it seems. "I can't" almost always means "I can't all at once", and gradual changes, with support, work better than the all-or-nothing idea. It's one of the best openings for a conversation, not the end of it.

**Family:** N. Ex-vegans: why I gave up · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "I really admire you, honestly. But I can't do it. I've tried and I couldn't."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Veganism is admirable.
2. **P2.** I tried, or I imagine I would try, and couldn't keep it up.
3. **P3.** No one is obliged to do what they can't do.
4. **C.** So I'm not obliged to be vegan.

P1 is an important concession. P3 is true: ought implies can. The problem lies in P2: "I can't" usually means "it's hard" or "I didn't manage the first time", not "it's impossible". What is hard is still possible, especially step by step and with help.

The argument is **valid**, but not **sound**: it confuses hard with impossible.

## 2. How it can show up

| Context | Typical form |
| --- | --- |
| Everyday conversation | "I admire you, but I can't do it." |
| Everyday conversation | "I wish I had your willpower." |

## 3. Answer for children

When someone says "I can't", sometimes they mean "it's hard". Riding a bike also seemed impossible at first, and we learned little by little, with someone holding on. With animals it's similar: you can start with one meal, then another, with help.

## 4. Short answer

Thank you. And if you admire it, you already agree with the most important thing: that animals matter. The good news is that no one needs to do everything at once. You can start with one meal a day, one product, one week. I didn't change in a day either. Want me to show you where I started?

**Question to ask back:** "If it didn't have to be all at once, what could you change this very week?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The phrase has two parts, and the first is the more important one:

| Part | What it says | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "I admire you" | Veganism is good | The person agrees with the moral point |
| "But I can't do it" | It's too hard | The question has become practical, not moral |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Admiration concedes the moral point.** No one admires someone for doing something indifferent or wrong. If the person admires it, they already recognize there's a reason to change (E12).

**2. Hard isn't impossible.** "Ought implies can" applies to the impossible, not to the hard. Many hard things are required of everyone: not lying, keeping promises, caring for those who depend on us.

**3. You don't have to manage everything at once.** The all-or-nothing idea gets in the way. Cutting down gradually, swapping one product a week and learning a few recipes lets the change last.

**4. Willpower counts for less than support.** In the Faunalytics survey, 84% of former vegetarians and vegans didn't take part in any group or organization. The difference between those who succeed and those who give up usually lies in support, recipes and a network, not in some special strength.

**5. It's an opening, not an end.** Someone who says "I admire you" is open. Responding with warmth and practical help tends to work better than responding with reproach.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Faunalytics, 2014](https://faunalytics.org/a-summary-of-faunalytics-study-of-current-and-former-vegetarians-and-vegans/) | 84% of former vegetarians and vegans didn't take part in groups; 37% are interested in going back | Survey carried out in 2014 in the United States |
| [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Weakness of Will"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/) | Weakness of will is acting against one's own better judgment | A topic discussed since Aristotle |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: everyone has their own story, and some people really can't

The most serious version says: there are people with eating disorders, poverty, illnesses or routines that make change much harder. Demanding the same of them as of those who have time and money is unfair.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **In those cases, the right thing is to do what's possible.** No one asks for the impossible. But most people who say "I can't" aren't in that situation.
2. **Even in hard cases there's something to do.** Cutting down, swapping some products, supporting collective changes.
3. **Difficulty doesn't change what is right.** It changes how much can be asked of each person, not whether animals matter.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Someone who says "I admire you" has already answered the moral question: animals matter, and sparing them is good. What's missing is the path. The useful conversation, from here on, is about how, not whether.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in N5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Confusing hard with impossible | Treats difficulty as inability |
| Logical | False dichotomy | Either everything at once, or nothing |
| Moral | Akrasia | Agrees with the moral point and doesn't act |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Admires it, but doesn't do it.** If it were indifferent, there would be nothing to admire.
2. **Manages other hard things.** Work, study, raising children.
3. **Thinks it's a matter of willpower.** But hasn't tried with support.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains N5 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Status quo bias | The current habit seems impossible to change | [Samuelson and Zeckhauser, 1988](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/jrisku/v1y1988i1p7-59.html) |
| Dissociation | Admires in the abstract, and doesn't think of the animal on the plate | [Benningstad and Kunst, 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31830517/) |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if I admire something but find it hard, I have no reason to do it".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quitting smoking | Someone who admires people who quit would have no reason to try | No one concludes this |
| Giving blood | Someone who admires donors would have no reason to donate | Admiration usually leads to trying |
| Studying | Someone who admires people who study could skip studying | Absurd |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's right that changing is hard, that willpower alone is rarely enough and that some people face real obstacles. It's also right to admire: it's a generous response, and it deserves to be received generously.

But admiration is already agreement. What's missing isn't persuasion, but help taking the first step.
