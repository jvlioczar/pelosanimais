# J16. "There are more important human problems"

**Thesis:** it's the fallacy of priority. Eating plants takes no time, money or energy away from any other cause. No one has to choose between fighting hunger and no longer paying for animals to be killed: it's possible to do both, and many people do. Besides, livestock farming is linked to serious human problems, such as deforestation, climate change, pandemics and dangerous working conditions.

**Family:** J. Practicality, economy and society · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "With so many children going hungry... Fight for people first."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** There are serious human problems, such as hunger, war and poverty.
2. **P2.** Moral resources and attention are limited.
3. **P3.** Human problems should have priority.
4. **C.** So concern for animals distracts from what matters more.

P1 is true. So is P2, in part. The problem lies in the conclusion: no longer eating animals doesn't use up resources that would go to other causes.

The argument is **valid** if veganism competed with other causes. Its **soundness** fails because it doesn't.

## 3. Answer for children

You can help people and animals at the same time. Eating beans instead of meat doesn't stop anyone from donating food, helping a friend or studying to change the world. You don't have to pick just one good thing to do.

## 4. Short answer

Eating plants takes no time or money away from any other cause. No one has to choose between fighting hunger and no longer paying for animals to be killed: it's possible to do both, and many people do. And livestock farming is linked to serious human problems: deforestation, climate change, pandemic risk and dangerous work. Caring for animals is often caring for people too.

**Question to ask back:** "What would you stop doing for people if you ate beans instead of meat?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument assumes causes compete:

| Action | Does it use up resources from other causes? |
| --- | --- |
| No longer eating animals | No: it's a swap on the plate, often cheaper |
| Campaigning for animals | Some time, like any cause |
| Donating to fight hunger | Not prevented by veganism |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. It's the fallacy of priority.** That there are more serious problems doesn't make smaller ones irrelevant. If it held, almost no cause could be defended.

**2. Not causing harm isn't a competing cause.** No longer eating animals is an abstention, not a project that takes up time.

**3. Livestock farming affects humans.** Deforestation (H1), emissions (H6), antibiotic resistance and the risk of diseases that pass from animals to humans are human problems.

**4. The argument often serves to end the conversation.** Even when it comes from someone devoted to another cause, it's still a false choice: not eating animals doesn't compete with any other struggle.

**5. Many people champion several causes.** Animal advocates also work for human rights, against hunger and for social justice.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Our World in Data, land for agriculture](https://ourworldindata.org/global-land-for-agriculture) | Livestock uses about 80% of farmland and provides 17% of calories | Shows the link between livestock and food security |
| [Our World in Data, food choice versus eating local](https://ourworldindata.org/food-choice-vs-eating-local) | Food production is a large source of emissions, with beef among the highest-impact foods | Shows the link between livestock and climate |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: animal activism takes energy

The most serious version grants that eating plants doesn't compete with other causes, but says animal activism takes time and money that could go to human causes.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **It changes the subject.** The question was about eating animals, not about activism.
2. **All causes compete for time.** And no one demands that only the most serious be championed.
3. **Animal suffering is enormous.** Billions of sentient beings a year, which makes the cause relevant.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The argument doesn't say animals don't matter; it says other things matter more. But no longer paying for animals to be killed takes nothing away from those other things. The moral question remains: if it harms no one, why not stop?

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in J16 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Fallacy of priority | If there's something more serious, the rest doesn't matter |
| Logical | False dichotomy | Either humans or animals |
| Epistemological | Ignoring connections | Doesn't see the link between livestock and human problems |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Devotes time to things less serious than hunger.** Such as hobbies, sport or local politics.
2. **Isn't fighting hunger.** Uses the argument without acting.
3. **Cares about cruelty to dogs.** Without thinking it distracts from human causes.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains J16 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motivated reasoning | The priority of other causes excuses change | General literature on motivated cognition |
| Speciesism | Human interests always seem more important | [Caviola, Everett and Faber, 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29517258/) |
| Zero-sum bias | Caring for animals is seen as taking something from humans, though the two do not compete for the same resources | [Meegan, 2010](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2010.00191) |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "as long as there are bigger problems, smaller ones don't matter".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fighting racism | It wouldn't matter while there's hunger | No one accepts this |
| Protecting children | It wouldn't matter while there are wars | No one accepts this |
| Cruelty to dogs | It wouldn't matter | Those who use the argument disagree |

## 11. What the argument gets right

Human problems are serious and urgent, and no one should be indifferent to hunger, poverty and violence. It's also true that some people present the animal cause in ways that seem to disdain humans, which is a mistake.

But no longer eating animals doesn't compete with any of those causes. It's possible to care for people and animals at the same time, and often one helps the other.
