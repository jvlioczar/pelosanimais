# K9. "Vegans should fight for laws, not diets"

**Thesis:** it's a false dichotomy. Personal consistency and political action don't compete; they complement each other. Many animal protection laws were won by people who had also changed their own lives. And those who say change must only be political can be asked the same question: are they supporting the laws they say they prefer? Often, the argument serves to do neither one nor the other (E4).

**Family:** K. Effectiveness and individual responsibility · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Diet is individualism. Do politics instead of pestering people."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Change at scale depends on laws, public policy and structural change.
2. **P2.** Individual consumer choices have limited impact.
3. **P3.** Activists' time and energy are limited.
4. **C.** So vegans should focus their efforts on politics, not on individual choices.

P1 is true. So is P2, in part. So is P3. The problem lies in the conclusion: not eating animals doesn't take time away from political action, and the two reinforce each other.

The argument is **valid** as a strategic argument for activists. It fails as a personal excuse for eating animals.

## 3. Answer for children

You can do both: choose food that doesn't hurt animals and also ask leaders to make laws that protect animals. One helps the other. People who look after animals every day are more eager to fight for them.

## 4. Short answer

You don't have to choose. Not eating animals takes no time away from political action, and the two reinforce each other: many animal protection laws were won by people who had also changed their own lives. Besides, those who prefer the political route can support the laws already under discussion. If you think laws are the way, you can support both.

**Question to ask back:** "Would you support laws to protect farmed animals, if they were proposed?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument presents two actions as mutually exclusive:

| Action | Does it take time from the other? |
| --- | --- |
| No longer eating animals | No: it's an everyday choice |
| Fighting for laws and policies | Not prevented by individual choice |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. It's a false dichotomy.** Changing one's own consumption and fighting for political change aren't alternatives.

**2. The two reinforce each other.** Personal consistency gives political action credibility, and changes in consumption create demand that makes new policies easier.

**3. Laws depend on social support.** Legal change happens when enough people are convinced. Individual consumption is one way to express and spread that conviction.

**4. The argument often serves to do nothing.** The useful question is whether the person using it supports the legal changes they say they prefer. If they do, great, and nothing stops them from also changing their own consumption (E4).

**5. Individual choice has a real effect.** Each purchase has an expected effect on production (K1).

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Kagan, *Do I Make a Difference?* (2011)](https://campuspress.yale.edu/shellykagan/papers) | Each purchase has a real expected effect on production | Shows the value of individual choice |
| [EU Cosmetics Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2009/1223/oj) | Bans animal testing of cosmetics and the sale of cosmetics tested on animals in the EU | An example of regulatory change driven by public pressure; also used in I2 |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: focusing on consumption is a trap

The most serious version says that "conscious consumption" talk shifts onto individuals a responsibility that belongs to companies and governments, and that the vegan movement sometimes falls into that trap.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **The criticism is of an exclusive focus, not of individual choice.** No one has to choose.
2. **No longer paying for deaths isn't luxury "conscious consumption".** It's refusing to take part in a harm.
3. **The question to the individual remains.** Even if laws are the main route.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Campaigning for laws for animals is great, and it's consistent with not eating them. The moral question doesn't wait for the laws: until they change, what justifies paying for animals to be killed?

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in K9 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | False dichotomy | Either laws or diet |
| Logical | Practical contradiction | Calls for laws, but opposes them |
| Moral | Delegating responsibility | Change would be up to others only |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Doesn't support laws protecting farmed animals.** In most cases.
2. **Says the state can't ban what they eat.** While calling for laws.
3. **Makes individual choices on other matters.** Without thinking that prevents politics.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains K9 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diffusion of responsibility | Change is up to others | General literature on diffusion of responsibility |
| Motivated reasoning | Politics becomes an excuse for not changing | General literature on motivated cognition |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a change must be political, there's no individual duty".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Racism | No one would need to change personal attitudes, only laws | No one accepts this |
| Pollution | No one would need to reduce their own pollution | No one accepts this |
| Animals | No one would need to change their plate | The same error |

## 11. What the argument gets right

Political and structural changes are essential, and the animal movement should invest in them. Protection laws, an end to harmful subsidies and transition policies can reach far more animals than isolated individual choices.

But fighting for laws doesn't excuse personal consistency, and personal consistency doesn't prevent fighting for laws. Each strengthens the other, and neither justifies continuing to pay for animals to be killed.
