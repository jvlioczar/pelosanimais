# I27. "I fish what I eat; it's more honest than buying at the store"

**Thesis:** taking responsibility for what you eat is more consistent than outsourcing the killing, and that deserves recognition. But being honest about a harm doesn't make it necessary. A fish taken out of water usually dies by asphyxia, which doesn't cause immediate loss of consciousness: in a study of trout, researchers estimated about 10 minutes of moderate to intense pain per fish. Knowing where your food comes from doesn't change what the fish went through.

**Family:** I. Other uses: testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companionship · **Part:** DIR

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "I fish, take it home and eat it. At least I know where it comes from, unlike people who buy packaged fish at the store."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Whoever catches their own fish takes direct responsibility for the animal's death.
2. **P2.** Whoever buys at the store hands that responsibility to others and avoids thinking about it.
3. **P3.** Taking responsibility is morally better than avoiding it.
4. **C.** So fishing for food is an honest, and acceptable, way to eat fish.

P1, P2 and P3 can be granted. The problem is the jump to "acceptable": being more honest than another practice doesn't show that the practice is fair.

The argument is **invalid**: the conclusion goes beyond the premises.

## 3. Answer for children

When a fish comes out of the water, it can't breathe. It flaps around because it's suffocating, and that can go on for many minutes. Catching your own fish is being honest about where food comes from, but the fish feels the same fear and the same lack of air.

## 4. Short answer

You take responsibility, and that's more consistent than pretending store-bought fish was born packaged. But being honest about a harm doesn't make it necessary. A fish out of water usually dies by asphyxia, which doesn't make it lose consciousness right away: in a study of trout, researchers estimated about 10 minutes of moderate to intense pain per fish. If you have other food options, the question remains: why cause that?

**Question to ask back:** "If you know where it comes from and how the fish dies, does that lessen what it feels?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument compares two ways of eating fish and concludes that one of them is acceptable:

| Question | What the argument answers | What's left out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is it more honest than buying at the store? | Yes | Honesty isn't a justification |
| Does the fish suffer? | Usually not asked | Hook, asphyxia, slow death |
| Is it necessary? | Usually not asked | For most people, there are alternatives |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Asphyxia is slow.** Out of water, a fish doesn't lose consciousness right away. In a study of rainbow trout published in *Scientific Reports* in 2025, researchers estimated about 10 minutes of moderate to intense pain per fish killed by air asphyxia, ranging from about 2 to 22 minutes.

**2. In other species, it can last longer.** Fishcount's report *Worse things happen at sea* cites a Dutch study in which herring, cod and plaice took 55 to 250 minutes to die by asphyxia.

**3. Fish feel pain.** The New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness (2024) recognizes a realistic possibility of consciousness in all vertebrates, including fish (A2).

**4. Honesty isn't a justification.** Someone who does something wrong openly is more consistent than someone who hides it, but the act is still wrong. Consistency is a step, not the end of the question.

**5. There's a way to reduce the harm, but not to remove it.** Someone who fishes can kill the fish immediately with a blow to the head instead of letting it suffocate. That reduces suffering; the question about necessity remains.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: fishing for food is more natural and has less impact than industry

The most serious version says that catching your own food on a small scale avoids industrial fishing, bycatch and waste, and that it's the oldest and most natural way of getting food.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Less environmental impact isn't less suffering for the fish.** A fish caught on a small scale goes through the same hook and the same asphyxia (H17).
2. **Being old and natural doesn't make something right (C1).** Many natural, old practices were abandoned once they stopped being necessary.
3. **Where there's no alternative, the situation changes.** Communities that depend on fishing to survive are in a different position (J18). For anyone with other options, the question remains.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The question isn't whether fishing is more honest than buying, but whether it's fair to put a fish through minutes of suffocation when there are other ways to eat. Being consistent about the harm is a good start for asking whether it needs to happen.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in I27 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Selective comparison | Comparing only with the worst (buying without thinking) makes the practice look acceptable |
| Epistemological | Not knowing about asphyxia | Assumes fish die quickly out of the water |
| Moral | Honesty as justification | Being frank about the harm is treated as if it made it legitimate |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Values knowing where it comes from.** And avoids looking at how long the fish takes to die.
2. **Criticizes those who outsource the killing.** And wouldn't apply the same reasoning to other animals they could kill with their own hands.
3. **Wouldn't accept suffocating a mammal.** Letting a dog or a rabbit die without air for minutes would be cruelty. And the evidence of pain in fish is solid (A2): what matters is what the animal feels, not how much it resembles us.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if I do it myself and own it, the practice is acceptable".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Killing a dog with your own hands to eat it | It would be acceptable | Almost no one accepts that |
| Hunting by people who don't need to | It would be acceptable | See I28 and H19 |
| Subsistence fishing with no alternative | It would be understandable | Here there is real need |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that taking responsibility for what you eat is more consistent than outsourcing the killing and avoiding thinking about it. People who fish know the fish was a living animal, and many people who eat packaged fish lack that clarity. It's also true that killing the fish immediately reduces suffering.

But honesty leads to the next question rather than closing it: if the fish feels and there are other ways to eat, why put it through this?
