# I7. "Aquariums and dolphin parks are harmless"

**Thesis:** dolphins and orcas travel dozens of kilometers a day in the ocean and live in complex social groups. No tank reproduces that. In captivity, these animals develop repetitive behaviors and health problems. That's why countries such as Canada banned keeping and breeding cetaceans in captivity in 2019. Aquariums with fish also involve capture, confinement and death.

**Family:** I. Other uses: testing, clothing, entertainment · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Dolphins enjoy doing shows. Aquariums teach about the ocean."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Aquariums and dolphin parks bring the public closer to marine life.
2. **P2.** This generates interest in and support for ocean conservation.
3. **P3.** The animals receive food, veterinary care and protection.
4. **C.** So aquariums and dolphin parks are beneficial and don't harm the animals.

P1 is true. P2 has mixed evidence. P3 is true but incomplete: basic care doesn't make up for the lack of space and social life.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails because P3 ignores what the animals lose.

## 3. Answer for children

The dolphin seems to smile, but that's just the shape of its mouth. In the sea, it swims long distances with its family every day. In a tank, it just swims in circles in the same place. That's why some countries decided not to let dolphins and whales be kept in tanks anymore.

## 4. Short answer

Dolphins and orcas swim dozens of kilometers a day and live in complex social groups. No tank reproduces that, and in captivity they develop repetitive behaviors and health problems. The dolphin's "smile" is just the shape of its mouth. That's why Canada banned keeping and breeding cetaceans in captivity in 2019. To learn about the ocean, documentaries and responsible wildlife watching don't require locking anyone up.

**Question to ask back:** "Can a tank, however big, compare with the ocean the dolphin would travel?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument considers what the animals receive, not what they lose:

| What the animal receives | What the animal loses |
| --- | --- |
| Regular food | Hunting and exploring |
| Veterinary care | Space to swim dozens of kilometers |
| Protection from predators | Social life with its family and group |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. The space is incompatible.** Dolphins and orcas travel great distances in the ocean. A tank is a tiny fraction of that space.

**2. Social life is lost.** Cetaceans live in stable family groups, with complex communication. In captivity, they're separated and regrouped for convenience.

**3. Captivity causes suffering.** Repetitive behaviors, aggression and health problems are frequently reported in captive cetaceans.

**4. The "smile" is anatomy.** The shape of a dolphin's mouth doesn't express emotion.

**5. The law is changing.** In 2019, Canada passed a law banning the keeping and breeding of whales, dolphins and porpoises in captivity, except for rescue and rehabilitation.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Parliament of Canada, Bill S-203](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/42-1/s-203) | Bans keeping and breeding cetaceans in captivity in Canada | Passed in 2019 |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: rescued animals need care

The most serious version points out that some animals are rescued injured and can't return to the sea, and that aquariums care for them.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **That justifies rehabilitation centers and sanctuaries.** Not shows or captive breeding.
2. **Most of the animals weren't rescued.** Many were captured or born in captivity.
3. **There are marine sanctuaries.** Protected coastal areas offer much more space.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The argument talks about education and care, but what's at stake is the entire life of animals confined so that others can see them. The moral question remains: what justifies locking up someone who would live freely in the ocean?

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in I7 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Omission | Considers what the animal receives, not what it loses |
| Epistemological | Convenient anthropomorphism | Reads the shape of a mouth as happiness |
| Moral | Entertainment as justification | The audience's pleasure outweighs the animal's life |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Says dolphins are intelligent.** And accepts confining them in a tank.
2. **Wouldn't accept living in one room forever.** Even with food and a doctor.
3. **Values documentaries about the ocean.** Which show the animals free.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains I7 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| System justification | A traditional attraction seems harmless | Linked to B8 |
| Motivated reasoning | The pleasure of the show makes confinement acceptable | General literature on motivated cognition |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if an animal receives food and care, confinement doesn't harm it".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prisoners | They wouldn't be harmed if they had food and a doctor | No one accepts this |
| Dogs kept on chains | They'd be fine if fed | It's considered cruelty |
| Orcas in tanks | They'd be fine | Canada banned it |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that aquariums can spark interest in the oceans, and that some do rescue and rehabilitation of marine animals. Keepers are often sincerely devoted to the animals.

But for cetaceans and other wide-ranging animals, no tank is enough. Interest in the ocean can be sparked without locking up those who live in it.
