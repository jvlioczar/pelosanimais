# I37. "Feeding a dog or cat vegan food is animal abuse"

**Thesis:** the answer is different for dogs and cats. Dogs are omnivores, and in 2024 the British Veterinary Association recognized that they can be fed a vegan diet, as long as it is nutritionally complete and monitored by a vet. Cats are obligate carnivores, and the same association still advises against a vegan diet for them; the studies that exist rely on guardians' reports. In the future, food engineering and cellular agriculture, such as precision fermentation and cultivated meat, already approved for pet treats in the United Kingdom and Singapore, may meet cats' needs without exploiting animals; these technologies are still in their early days. Most importantly, the difficulty of feeding a cat doesn't change the question about the human plate: people can live well without eating animals.

**Family:** I. Other uses: testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companionship · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "A vegan who feeds their dog vegan food is an abuser. Dogs and cats are carnivores; they need meat."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Dogs and cats have species-specific nutritional needs.
2. **P2.** An inadequate diet causes disease and suffering.
3. **P3.** A diet without meat is inadequate for dogs and cats.
4. **C.** So feeding dogs and cats vegan food is abuse.

P1 and P2 are true. P3 is true for poorly planned home-made diets, but not for all: for dogs, complete vegan foods exist and are accepted by veterinary bodies; for cats, the evidence is weak and caution is justified.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** depends on the species: it fails for dogs, and for cats the concern has a basis.

## 3. Answer for children

Each animal needs the right food for them. Dogs can eat food without meat, if it has everything they need and a vet keeps an eye on them. Cats are different and need special care. Today, scientists are learning to make meat from a few cells, without having to kill any animal, and one day that might become food for cats. What matters is that the animal stays healthy, and that people, who can live well without meat, do their part.

## 4. Short answer

It depends on the species. Dogs are omnivores, and in 2024 the British Veterinary Association recognized that they can live on a complete vegan diet, with veterinary monitoring. Cats are obligate carnivores, and the same association advises against a vegan diet for them; but what they need is nutrients, and the taurine in ordinary cat foods is usually synthetic already. Cultivated meat and precision fermentation, still in their early days, may one day feed cats without exploiting animals. A poorly planned home-made diet is risky for both. And none of this changes the question of your plate: you can live well without eating animals. If the concern is for animals' welfare, why would it stop at dogs and cats?

**Question to ask back:** "If the concern is the animal's health, what does a complete food and a vet's monitoring fail to guarantee?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

| Species | What biology says | What veterinary bodies say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dog | Omnivore, digests starch and plant protein well | A vegan diet is possible if it is complete, with monitoring |
| Cat | Obligate carnivore: needs taurine, arachidonic acid and preformed vitamin A, which can be supplied without meat | They generally advise against a vegan diet |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Dogs aren't obligate carnivores.** Over the course of domestication, dogs adapted to digest starch. Complete vegan foods, formulated to meet their nutritional requirements, can keep them healthy.

**2. The veterinary position has changed for dogs.** In 2024, the British Veterinary Association recognized that dogs can be fed nutritionally complete vegan diets, warning about the lack of long-term data and recommending monitoring.

**3. Existing studies have limits.** A 2022 survey of 2,536 dogs, published in *PLOS ONE*, found equal or better health indicators in dogs on vegan diets, but the data come from the guardians themselves. A similar 2023 study of cats found no significant difference in the general indicators, with the same limitation and only 127 cats on vegan diets.

**4. For cats, caution is justified.** The nutrients cats need can be produced synthetically, but evidence of long-term safety is weak, and the main veterinary bodies advise against it. Anyone who wants to try should use only a complete commercial food and veterinary monitoring. A home-made meat-free diet for cats is risky.

**5. Food engineering and cellular agriculture may change the picture.** What a cat needs is nutrients, not the body of a dead animal. The taurine added to ordinary pet foods, including meat-based ones, is usually produced by chemical synthesis, and the European Food Safety Authority considers synthetic taurine effective in cat and dog diets. New techniques may go further: in precision fermentation, programmed yeasts or bacteria produce specific animal proteins, and the American company Bond Pet Foods has already tested in dogs a chicken protein made this way; in cultivated meat, an animal's cells grow in bioreactors without the animal having to be killed. In July 2024, the United Kingdom authorized the use of the company Meatly's cultivated chicken in pet food, and the first dog treats went on sale in London in February 2025; in June 2025, Singapore approved treats for dogs and cats with cultivated chicken from Friends & Family Pet Food. These are early steps: the approved products are treats, not complete diets, production is small and expensive, and there are still no long-term studies of cats fed this way. But they suggest that the cat dilemma may one day have a solution that doesn't depend on breeding and killing other animals.

**6. Pet food also involves exploitation.** Conventional pet foods use meat and by-products from animals who were bred and slaughtered. The dilemma is real for anyone living with a cat, and there is no answer without a cost.

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: the vegan is imposing their ethics on the animal

The most serious version says the animal didn't choose to be vegan, and that the guardian is using the dog or cat to fulfill their own convictions.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Every guardian chooses the animal's food.** Choosing a meat-based food is also the guardian's decision, and it costs other animals their lives.
2. **The criterion should be the animal's health.** For dogs, a complete vegan food can meet it; for cats, prudence means following veterinary advice.
3. **The difficulty with cats doesn't extend to people.** Humans aren't obligate carnivores, and can live well without eating animals.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The question of what to feed companion animals is hard, especially for cats. But it doesn't answer the main question: whether we, who can live well without eating animals, have a reason to keep doing so.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in I37 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Overgeneralization | Treats dogs and cats as if they had the same needs |
| Empirical | Unaware of complete foods | Assumes every vegan diet for dogs is home-made and incomplete |
| Logical | Changing the question (red herring) | Uses the cat's diet to discuss the human diet |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Says dogs are carnivores.** And dogs are omnivores.
2. **Worries about the dog's health.** And ignores that complete food and a vet take care of it.
3. **Uses the cat against the vegan.** And eats meat without being an obligate carnivore.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains I37 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Availability bias | The cat's case, which comes to mind first, becomes the rule for dogs too | [Tversky and Kahneman, 1973](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285%2873%2990033-9) |
| Naturalness bias | What the animal would eat in the wild always seems best | General literature on risk perception |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "a carnivorous animal can only be well fed with meat, and anyone who does otherwise abuses them".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dogs on a complete vegan food | They'd be abused | Veterinary bodies already accept it for dogs |
| Cats on a meat-free commercial food | The concern has a basis | Veterinary advice is against it |
| Humans | They'd need meat | Humans aren't obligate carnivores |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that dogs and cats have their own needs, that poorly planned home-made diets are dangerous, and that for cats the evidence on vegan diets is weak and caution is justified.

But for dogs there are complete foods accepted by veterinary bodies, for cats technology may open paths without exploitation, and the difficulty with cats changes nothing about the human plate.
