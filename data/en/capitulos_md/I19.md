# I19. "The fighting bull lives free in the field and dies fighting"

**Thesis:** the fighting bull lives a few years in the field, but a good life doesn't justify the death that follows: in the Spanish bullfight he is wounded by the picador's lance, the banderillas and the sword, in front of an audience, over about 20 minutes. It isn't a fair fight, because the bull is deliberately weakened before the final confrontation. And the idea that the breed would disappear without bullfighting doesn't help any particular bull. Colombia passed a law in 2024 banning bullfights from 2027, upheld by its Constitutional Court, and Spain abolished its National Bullfighting Prize in 2024.

**Family:** I. Other uses: testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companionship · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "The fighting bull lives four or five years loose in the pasture, like a king. He dies fighting, on his feet, not in a slaughterhouse. And without bullfighting, the breed dies out."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** The fighting bull lives for years in freedom, on extensive pastures, better than most beef cattle.
2. **P2.** In the arena he fights and can injure or kill the bullfighter, who also risks his life.
3. **P3.** Without bullfighting, the breed and the pastures where it lives would disappear.
4. **C.** So bullfighting is more dignified for the bull than ordinary slaughter, and should be preserved.

P1 is largely true. P2 is true about the bullfighter's risk, but the bull doesn't choose to fight and is weakened beforehand. P3 is debatable and, even if true, benefits no individual bull.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P2 and P3.

## 3. Answer for children

In a bullfight, the bull is taken into an arena full of people, stabbed several times and then killed with a sword. He doesn't understand what's happening and didn't choose to be there. Having lived well before doesn't make his pain hurt any less.

## 4. Short answer

It's true that the fighting bull lives a few years in the field. But a good life doesn't justify the death: in the Spanish bullfight he is wounded by the picador's lance, the banderillas and the sword, for about 20 minutes, for an audience. It isn't a fair fight, because he is deliberately weakened. And the breed disappearing harms no bull that already exists. Colombia has banned bullfights from 2027, and Spain abolished its national bullfighting prize in 2024.

**Question to ask back:** "If the fight were fair, why would the bull need to be weakened first?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

In the Spanish bullfight, the bull's death follows stages:

| Stage | What happens to the bull |
| --- | --- |
| Tercio de varas | The mounted picador drives a lance into the neck muscles, weakening him and making him lower his head |
| Tercio de banderillas | Pairs of banderillas, sticks with barbed points, are driven into his back |
| Tercio de muerte | The bullfighter tries to kill him with the sword; if he fails, he uses other blows until death |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. A good life doesn't justify a painful death.** The argument compares bullfighting with the slaughterhouse, not with life. A death less bad than another is still an imposed death, and this one is carried out with prolonged pain and fear, for entertainment.

**2. It isn't a fair fight.** The bull doesn't choose to enter the arena, and the first stages exist to weaken him before the final confrontation. It is almost always he who dies.

**3. Preserving the breed doesn't help the bull.** A breed feels nothing; each bull does. Keeping a breed so that its members die in the arena isn't a benefit to them. And pastures and breeds can be preserved by other means, if society wants.

**4. Society is changing.** Colombia passed Law 2385 in 2024, banning bullfights from July 2027, and the Constitutional Court declared it constitutional in 2025. In Spain, the Ministry of Culture abolished the National Bullfighting Prize in 2024, citing rejection by broad sectors of society; there was a strong backlash, and the Senate and an industry foundation created an alternative prize. In Portugal, the bull isn't killed in the arena, with a legal exception in Barrancos.

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: bullfighting is art and heritage

The most serious version says bullfighting is an art with centuries of history, recognized by law as cultural heritage in Spain, and that judging it from outside means not understanding its meaning.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Being heritage doesn't make a practice fair.** Many old practices were abandoned once the harm they caused was recognized.
2. **The artistic meaning is for the audience, not for the bull.** What the bull experiences is pain and fear, whatever it means to those watching.
3. **Criticism also comes from within.** A large part of Spanish society and of other countries with a bullfighting tradition rejects bullfights, and it was those countries' parliaments and courts that approved restrictions.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The question is whether it's fair to wound and kill an animal, slowly and in front of an audience, so that death becomes a spectacle. The years in the pasture don't change the answer.

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Says the bull is respected.** And wounds him several times before killing him.
2. **Says it's a fight.** And deliberately weakens the opponent.
3. **Wants to preserve the fighting bull.** And breeds him to die in the arena.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if the animal lived well, a painful death as a spectacle is acceptable".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cockfights with well-raised roosters | They'd be acceptable | Brazil's Supreme Court banned them |
| Well-raised dogs killed as a spectacle | It would be acceptable | No one accepts that |
| Bullfighting without killing or wounding | The problem of fear and stress would remain | It's a step, not the end of the question |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that the fighting bull usually lives for years on extensive pastures, in better conditions than many beef cattle, and that bullfighting has centuries of history and meaning for many people.

But a good life doesn't justify a slow, painful death turned into a spectacle, and preserving a breed is no benefit to any bull. Several countries are already leaving this tradition behind.
