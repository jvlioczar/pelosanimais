# H11. "Local, organic meat is sustainable"

**Thesis:** "organic" and "local" say little about meat's emissions and land use. The lowest-impact beef, from the 10% most efficient producers, emits about 9 kilos of CO₂ equivalent per 100 grams of protein; the highest-impact peas, about 0.8. Even the best beef emits more than ten times as much as the highest-impact peas. And pasture-raised meat usually requires even more land.

**Family:** H. Environment, ecology and agriculture · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "I buy organic. Grass-fed beef is sustainable. Eat local."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Intensive livestock farming causes serious environmental harm.
2. **P2.** Organic, local, pasture-raised meat avoids some of that harm, such as pesticides, antibiotics and transport.
3. **P3.** So that meat is sustainable.
4. **C.** So those who eat it don't contribute to the environmental problems of livestock farming.

P1 is true. So is P2, in part. The problem lies in P3: avoiding some harms doesn't make production low-impact, and emissions and land use remain high.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P3.

## 3. Answer for children

Organic meat doesn't use certain medicines and poisons, and that's good. But the cow still releases gases and needs a lot of space to live. Even the most carefully produced meat pollutes much more than growing beans or peas.

## 4. Short answer

Organic and local help in some ways, but they don't change the essentials: meat emits a lot and uses a lot of land. According to Poore and Nemecek's data, the most efficient beef emits about 9 kilos of CO₂ equivalent per 100 grams of protein; the least efficient peas, about 0.8. Even the best beef emits more than ten times as much as the highest-impact peas. And pasture-raised meat usually requires even more land.

**Question to ask back:** "If the lowest-impact meat emits ten times more than the highest-impact plant, sustainable compared with what?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument compares "good" meat with "bad" meat, not with the alternatives:

| Comparison | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Organic versus conventional meat | Fewer pesticides and antibiotics; similar or higher emissions |
| Pasture-raised versus feedlot | More land per kilo; emissions per kilo often similar or higher |
| Best beef versus worst plant protein | About 9 against 0.8 kg of CO₂ equivalent per 100 g of protein |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. The best meat still emits a lot.** According to Our World in Data, the 10% of beef producers with the lowest impact emit about 9 kg of CO₂ equivalent per 100 g of protein. The 10% of pea producers with the highest impact emit about 0.8.

**2. Organic doesn't mean low emissions.** Organic certification concerns pesticides, fertilizers and antibiotics, not methane emissions or land use.

**3. Pasture-raised meat uses more land.** Pasture-raised cattle grow more slowly and occupy more area. At scale, this would require even more land, and more deforestation (H1, H9).

**4. Local weighs little.** Transport is a small fraction of meat's emissions (H5).

**5. "Good" meat is a small fraction of consumption.** Most meat comes from conventional production (B4).

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Our World in Data, less meat or sustainable meat](https://ourworldindata.org/less-meat-or-sustainable-meat) | The lowest-impact beef emits about 9 kg of CO₂ equivalent per 100 g of protein; the highest-impact peas, about 0.8 | Based on Poore and Nemecek, 2018 |
| [Our World in Data, land use and diets](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use-diets) | Beef requires about 50 to 100 times more land per kilocalorie than plant foods | Based on Poore and Nemecek, 2018 |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: it's better than conventional meat

The most serious version grants that organic, local meat isn't low-impact, but says it's better than conventional meat in some respects, such as welfare and chemical use.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Better than the worst isn't good.** The relevant comparison is with plant alternatives.
2. **It isn't always better.** On emissions and land use, pasture-raised meat can be worse than conventional.
3. **The animal is still killed.** A better life doesn't change the ending (B4, B3).

### (f) Back to the moral question

Even the most sustainable meat still comes from an animal raised and killed. The environmental question is important, but it isn't the only one: if meat were neutral for the planet, the question about the animal would remain. And it still has no answer.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in H11 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Selective comparison | Compares good meat with bad meat, not with plants |
| Epistemological | Confusing labels | Treats "organic" as "low emissions" |
| Moral | Moral licensing | The better choice seems to excuse the even better one |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Doesn't always buy organic.** Outside the home, meat almost never is.
2. **Ignores land use.** Which is greater for pasture-raised meat.
3. **Accepts that plants emit less.** But doesn't change the choice.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a product is better than its conventional version, it's sustainable".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An efficient gasoline car | It would be sustainable, because it's better than an old car | It still emits a lot |
| An "organic" cigarette | It would be healthy | No one concludes this |
| Organic meat | It would be sustainable | It still emits far more than plants |

## 11. What the argument gets right

There are real differences between production systems: fewer pesticides, fewer antibiotics and sometimes better welfare. People who look for organic, local meat care about the impact of what they eat, and that concern is valuable.

But for the climate and for land, the best meat is still far worse than plants. And for the animal, the ending is the same.
