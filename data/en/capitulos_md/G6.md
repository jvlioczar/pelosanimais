# G6. "Milk is necessary for your bones"

**Thesis:** we need calcium, not milk. Calcium is found in dark green vegetables such as kale and broccoli, in tofu made with calcium salts, in fortified plant drinks, in sesame and in legumes. About two thirds of the world's adults lose the ability to digest lactose well, and billions of people live without dairy. Strong bones also depend on vitamin D, physical activity and not smoking.

**Family:** G. Health and nutrition · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Milk makes bones strong. You'll get osteoporosis. Children need milk."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Calcium is essential for bone health.
2. **P2.** Milk is a rich, well-absorbed source of calcium.
3. **P3.** Without milk, it's hard to reach the recommended amount of calcium.
4. **C.** So milk is necessary for bone health.

P1 and P2 are true. The problem lies in P3: there are many plant and fortified sources of calcium, and the need is for the nutrient, not the food.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P3.

## 3. Answer for children

Calcium helps make bones strong. It's in milk, but also in kale, broccoli, tofu, sesame and plant milks with added calcium. Playing, running and getting some sun also help your bones a lot.

## 4. Short answer

We need calcium, not milk. Calcium is found in kale, broccoli, tofu made with calcium salts, fortified plant drinks, sesame and beans. About two thirds of the world's adults digest lactose poorly. And bone health also depends on vitamin D, physical activity and not smoking. Milk is one source of calcium, not the only one.

**Question to ask back:** "If calcium exists in vegetables and fortified foods, is the need for calcium or for cow's milk?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument confuses the nutrient with the source:

| What bones need | Possible sources |
| --- | --- |
| Calcium | Milk, but also kale, broccoli, calcium-set tofu, fortified plant drinks, sesame, almonds, beans |
| Vitamin D | Sunlight, fortified foods, supplements |
| Mechanical load | Physical activity |
| Adequate protein | Legumes and other sources |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Calcium exists outside milk.** Low-oxalate dark green vegetables, such as kale and bok choy, have well-absorbed calcium. Tofu made with calcium salts and fortified plant drinks provide amounts similar to milk.

**2. Most of the world's adults don't digest lactose well.** About 65% to 70% of adults lose lactase, the enzyme that digests milk sugar; keeping it into adulthood is a trait of some populations. Billions of people live without dairy.

**3. Bones depend on more than calcium.** Vitamin D, physical activity, adequate protein and not smoking are important factors for bone health.

**4. Nutrition bodies consider the diet adequate.** With planned calcium sources, vegan diets meet requirements.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Harvard T. H. Chan School of Public Health, calcium](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/calcium/) | Lists non-dairy calcium sources, such as calcium-set tofu, fortified drinks, kale and bok choy, and explains that spinach has low absorption because of oxalates | A reference page on nutrition |
| [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27886704/) | Well-planned vegan diets, with calcium sources, are adequate | Official position |
| [Tong and colleagues, 2020, *BMC Medicine*](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-020-01815-3) | In EPIC-Oxford, vegans had a higher risk of fractures than meat eaters, especially hip fractures | The authors point to lower calcium and protein intake and lower body weight as possible factors |
| [Misselwitz and colleagues, 2017, review on lactase](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28421381/) | About 65% to 70% of the world's adults don't retain lactase | A review in *Current Gastroenterology Reports* |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: vegans may take in too little calcium

The most serious version cites a real finding: in the large British EPIC-Oxford study (2020), vegans had more fractures than meat eaters, especially hip fractures. On average, they also weighed less and took in less calcium and protein. And it's easy to fall below the recommended amount without planning calcium sources.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **The finding calls for attention, not milk.** The authors themselves link the risk to low calcium and protein intake, which can be corrected with plant and fortified sources, vitamin D and physical activity. But it's a point that deserves care and shouldn't be minimized.
2. **The risk points to planning.** Information about calcium sources solves most of the problem.
3. **The moral question remains.** Even if calcium were easier to get from milk, that wouldn't justify what milk production does to cows and calves (C11).

### (f) Back to the moral question

Milk isn't necessary for the bones of those with access to other calcium sources. And milk production involves repeated pregnancies, separating calves and slaughtering cows. If there are other ways to get calcium, the question is whether that cost to the animals is justified.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in G6 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Confusing nutrient and source | "We need calcium" becomes "we need milk" |
| Epistemological | Not knowing calcium sources | Ignores vegetables and fortified foods |
| Moral | Invented necessity | Uses a need that has alternatives to justify harm |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Knows many people are lactose intolerant.** And live well without milk.
2. **Doesn't drink milk from other species.** Cow's milk is a cultural choice.
3. **Doesn't think adults of other mammals need milk.** Adult cows don't drink it.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a food is a source of a nutrient, it's necessary".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oranges | They'd be necessary for vitamin C | There are many other sources |
| Fish | It would be necessary for omega-3 | This is the argument of G7 |
| Milk | It would be necessary for calcium | The error is the same |

## 11. What the argument gets right

Calcium is essential, and milk is indeed a rich source of it. Whoever gives up dairy needs to learn about alternative sources, and studies show that very low calcium intakes increase risks. This is a legitimate concern.

But what bones need is calcium, vitamin D and movement, not cow's milk. With plant and fortified sources, bones do fine, and so do cows.
