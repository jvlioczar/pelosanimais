# J4. "Eating out, traveling and parties become impossible"

**Thesis:** the social cost exists, but it's usually exaggerated. Vegan options in restaurants, delivery apps and supermarkets grow every year, and adapting is quick: you learn what to order, what to bring and how to talk about it. Parties and gatherings carry on, with one more dish on the table. A social inconvenience, however real, doesn't outweigh an animal's life.

**Family:** J. Practicality, economy and society · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "What about dinner at my in-laws'? I'll be isolated."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Eating is a social activity, tied to gatherings, parties and travel.
2. **P2.** Dietary restrictions make taking part harder and can cause conflict.
3. **P3.** Social isolation is a real cost to wellbeing.
4. **C.** So veganism imposes too high a social cost.

P1, P2 and P3 are true to some extent. The problem lies in the conclusion: the cost is usually smaller than imagined and shrinks over time.

The argument is **valid** if the cost were high and permanent. Its **soundness** fails because it tends to be moderate and temporary.

## 3. Answer for children

When a child is allergic to peanuts, they still go to parties: they bring a snack, tell their friends, and everyone has fun. It's similar for someone who's vegan. At first you need to get a bit organized, but then it gets easy. And real friends like you for who you are, not for what you eat.

## 4. Short answer

The social cost exists, but it's usually smaller than it seems and shrinks over time. Vegan options in restaurants, delivery apps and supermarkets grow every year. At parties you can bring a dish for everyone; when traveling you can look things up in advance. Family and friends usually adapt. People with allergies and religious restrictions do this all the time.

**Question to ask back:** "Do people with allergies or religious restrictions stop going to parties and traveling?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument imagines the worst case:

| Situation | What usually happens |
| --- | --- |
| Restaurants | Many have vegan or adaptable options |
| Parties | Bringing a dish solves it and often pleases everyone |
| Travel | Research in advance and apps make it easier |
| Family | Initial resistance usually fades over time |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. The cost is real, but smaller than imagined.** Most of the difficulties come at the start.

**2. The options are growing.** Restaurants, delivery apps and supermarkets offer more and more vegan options.

**3. Other people do the same.** People with allergies, celiac disease, religious restrictions or medical diets live with dietary restrictions and keep socializing.

**4. The social cost shrinks over time.** Family and friends get used to it, and the person learns to handle the situations.

**5. Inconvenience doesn't justify harm.** Even if real, social discomfort doesn't outweigh an animal's life.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Faunalytics, study of current and former vegetarians and vegans](https://faunalytics.org/a-summary-of-faunalytics-study-of-current-and-former-vegetarians-and-vegans/) | 13% of those who quit cited social issues | Shows the social cost is real for some, and that support matters |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: family conflict can be serious

The most serious version points out that, for some people, family resistance is strong and lasting, and that conflict at home can greatly affect wellbeing.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **That calls for patience and strategy.** Not for giving up.
2. **Many families change over time.** Sometimes the vegan person inspires others.
3. **Veganism is "as far as is possible".** Those facing extreme situations may need flexibility (D7).

### (f) Back to the moral question

The social cost is real, but it's a cost to the person who changes, not a moral reason to carry on. The question remains: does the discomfort of being different at a party justify an animal's death?

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in J4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Epistemological | Overestimating the cost | Takes the worst case as the rule |
| Logical | False dichotomy | Either meat or isolation |
| Moral | Social comfort as justification | The discomfort outweighs the animal's life |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Respects other people's restrictions.** Such as allergies or religion.
2. **Has adapted to other social changes.** Such as moving city or job.
3. **Values family.** And families usually adapt.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains J4 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social conformity | Standing out from the group feels threatening | Classic social psychology literature |
| Status quo bias | Current social life seems the only possible one | [Samuelson and Zeckhauser, 1988](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/jrisku/v1y1988i1p7-59.html) |
| Bandwagon effect | Following the group's menu seems the only way to belong | [Questão de Ética, vol. X, ch. 1](https://questaodeetica.biblioverse.org/en/v10/01-biases-that-lead-us-to-neglect-the-problems-with-the-most.html) |
| Loss aversion | Losing the familiar dish weighs more than gaining others | [Kahneman, Knetsch and Thaler, 1990](https://doi.org/10.1086/261737) |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a choice has a social cost, there's no obligation to make it".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stopping drinking | It wouldn't be necessary if it caused awkwardness at parties | No one concludes this |
| Standing up for someone against an offensive joke | It wouldn't be necessary, because it causes discomfort | No one concludes this |
| No longer eating animals | It wouldn't be necessary | The same error |

## 11. What the argument gets right

The social cost is real, and it shouldn't be played down. Eating is a social act, and standing apart from it can cause conflict and loneliness, especially at first. Those going through this deserve support, not judgment.

But the cost is usually smaller and more temporary than imagined, and it shrinks with time and with growing options. It isn't a reason to keep harming animals.
