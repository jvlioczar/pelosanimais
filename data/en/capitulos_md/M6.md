# M6. "Vegans are annoying and bossy; I don't want to be part of that group"

**Thesis:** being policed, pressured or attacked by other vegans is a real experience, and it drives people away. But you can leave a group without leaving the principle: not exploiting animals doesn't require joining anyone, visiting any forum, or even liking other vegans. Animals are not the activists, and the behavior of some humans doesn't change what happens to them. Someone who left because of the people can change company, or keep none at all, and go on not eating animals.

**Family:** M. Ex-vegans: why I gave up · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "I left because of the vegans themselves. It's constant policing, everyone trying to be purer than everyone else. I don't want to be mistaken for those people."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Some of the vegans I spent time with were hostile: they watched, pressured and humiliated anyone who slipped.
2. **P2.** No one is obliged to stay in a group that harms them.
3. **P3.** Being vegan means being part of that group.
4. **C.** So I have no reason to stay vegan.

P1 may be true, and often is. P2 is true. The problem lies in P3: veganism is a practice (not using animals, as far as possible), not a membership. The practice can be kept without the group, as many people do.

The argument is **valid**, but not **sound**: P3 confuses a principle with the people who defend it.

## 3. Answer for children

Imagine you like looking after animals and you join an animal protection club. But some kids in the club are bossy and argue about everything. You can leave the club, and that's fair. But the animals didn't argue with you. You can keep looking after them on your own, or with other friends.

## 4. Short answer

I'm sorry it went that way, and you're not the only person who says so: there are hostile groups, and no one has to put up with them. But notice that the quarrel was with people, not with animals. You can leave the group, mute the accounts, never go to another meetup, and still not eat animals. When someone goes back to eating meat, the ones who pay are the animals, not the annoying vegans.

**Question to ask back:** "Was it the group or the idea that pushed you away? Can you keep the idea without being part of the group?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument bundles three things that can be separated:

| Thing | What it is | Can it be dropped without dropping the others? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The group | People, accounts, forums, collectives | Yes |
| The label | Calling yourself vegan in public | Yes (M7) |
| The practice | Not eating or using animals | It's the only one that reaches the animals |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Leaving the group isn't leaving the principle.** No one needs a membership card to not eat animals. The practice happens at the store, in the kitchen and on the plate, not in meetings. Many vegans have never taken part in any collective.

**2. Animals are not the activists.** In L1 the guide already deals with people who reject veganism out of dislike for vegans: it's the messenger effect, judging an idea by who says it. Here the case is more delicate, because the person knew the group from the inside. Even so, the structure is the same: the hen in the cage is no better off because an activist was rude in a comment thread.

**3. "Vegans" are many different groups.** There are welcoming collectives and quarrelsome ones, people who cook together and people who inspect each other. Online, the most aggressive voices show up more, and stick in memory more, than the thousands of people who simply live their lives without eating animals.

**4. Hostility inside the movement is a real and recognized problem.** A 2019 study of 17 animal rights activists in the United States, by Paul Gorski, Stacy Lopresti-Goodman and Dallas Rising, listed among the causes of burnout factors from within the movement itself: hostility between activists, racism and sexism in organizations, and a culture that demands sacrifice and looks down on self-care. This guide also criticizes infighting and purity policing (K6, L11, L21, L22). People who complain about them are right to complain.

**5. Those who gave up were usually on their own.** In the 2014 Faunalytics study, 84% of former vegetarians and vegans weren't actively involved in any group or organization. In a later study by the same organization, which followed 222 people through their first six months of change, social strategies helped most those who knew few vegetarians or vegans. What tends to be missing is good company, not more policing.

**6. There are practical ways out.** Change groups. Prefer in-person gatherings, such as potlucks and volunteer days at sanctuaries, to online arguments. Follow recipe accounts instead of controversy accounts. Or have no community at all: a recipe notebook and a B12 supplement are enough.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Faunalytics, 2014](https://faunalytics.org/a-summary-of-faunalytics-study-of-current-and-former-vegetarians-and-vegans/) | 84% of former vegetarians and vegans weren't involved in groups; 63% disliked sticking out from the crowd; 37% are interested in going back | Survey carried out in 2014 in the United States |
| [Faunalytics, 2022](https://faunalytics.org/going-veg-barriers-and-strategies/) | Among 222 people in transition, the share who knew at most one vegetarian or vegan fell from 24% to 9% in six months; social strategies helped those with a small network | United States and Canada; a more committed sample than average |
| [Gorski, Lopresti-Goodman and Rising, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/14742837.2018.1561260) | Interviews with 17 activists: causes inside the movement contribute to burnout | A small qualitative study; it doesn't measure frequency |
| [MacInnis and Hodson, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/1368430215618253) | Vegans are the target of negative attitudes from meat eaters | Shows that the "annoying vegan" image also comes from outside (L1) |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: a movement is known by its fruits

The most serious version says: if an idea produces so many intolerant people, maybe something is wrong with the idea. An ethic that makes people worse at dealing with others doesn't deserve support.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **No cause would pass that test.** Human rights movements, religions, parties and fan communities all have intolerant members. If that refuted ideas, none would be left.
2. **The principle doesn't tell anyone to humiliate anyone.** "Don't make animals suffer unnecessarily" contains no instruction to police your peers. Those who police do so on their own account, and often against what the movement itself recommends.
3. **If the criticism holds, it calls for reform, not for abandoning the animals.** A hostile movement needs to change. That conclusion is about the movement. About what is done to animals, it says nothing.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Suppose every vegan in the world were unbearable. Sows would still be in crates, fish would still suffocate on boats, and the question would be the same: is it fair to do this to someone who feels, when it can be avoided? The answer doesn't depend on how likeable the person asking is. Someone who left because of the people has every right to protect themselves from them, and can do that without making the animals pay the price.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in M6 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Ad hominem (against a group) | Rejects the practice because of the behavior of those who defend it |
| Logical | False dichotomy | Either I stay in the hostile group, or I go back to eating animals |
| Epistemological | Hasty generalization | Takes a few groups, usually online ones, for all vegans |
| Moral | Shifting the cost | The complaint is against humans, and the animals pay |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **The quarrel was with people, and the response hits the animals.** Those who caused the hurt feel nothing when the other person goes back to eating meat.
2. **Criticizes bossiness, but lets the group decide.** If the practice depends on approving of other vegans, they are still in charge of the plate.
3. **In other cases, the person separates things well.** Someone who leaves a gym because of arrogant members doesn't conclude that exercise is bad for you.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains M6 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Messenger effect | Dislike of those who defend the idea contaminates the idea | [Questão de Ética, vol. X, ch. 6](https://questaodeetica.biblioverse.org/en/v10/06-some-biases-that-can-influence-the-choice-of-strategies.html) |
| Availability bias | The fights stay in memory; the peaceful company doesn't | General literature on heuristics |
| Stereotype | "Those people" treats millions of individuals as a single block | [MacInnis and Hodson, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/1368430215618253) |
| Psychological reactance | Being pressured creates the urge to do the opposite | General literature on reactance |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if the people who defend a cause treated me badly, I can abandon what the cause asks for".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A recycling group with arrogant neighbors | Going back to dumping trash in the river | You leave the group, not the practice |
| A church full of gossip and judgment | Someone who left could start lying and stealing | Many people leave the community and keep the values |
| An anti-racist collective with internal fights | Someone who left could go back to discriminating | No one would accept this: the victims aren't to blame for the fights |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's right that hostile vegans and groups exist, that purity policing hurts and that it drives people away, dedicated activists included. It's also right that no one should stay where they are mistreated, and that the movement has a duty to look in the mirror: welcoming people who slip works better, and is fairer, than humiliating them.

But what the argument justifies is leaving the group, not going back to funding what happens to animals. They weren't in the argument, they policed no one, and they are the only ones who lose when hurt caused by people turns into a change on the plate.
