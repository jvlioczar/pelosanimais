# G3. "If you need B12 supplements, it isn't natural"

**Thesis:** vitamin B12 is produced by bacteria, not by animals. Farmed pigs and chickens receive B12 in the vitamin premixes added to their feed, because they don't get it from plants. In cattle, B12 is produced by bacteria in the rumen, and cobalt is often added to the feed so this can happen. So much of the B12 meat eaters get also comes from supplementation, just indirectly. And "natural" is no criterion of health or morality: we use vaccines, glasses and treated water.

**Family:** G. Health and nutrition · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "A diet that needs pills is wrong. No natural diet needs supplements."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Vitamin B12 is essential, and lacking it causes serious harm.
2. **P2.** Vegan diets don't provide B12 without supplements or fortified foods.
3. **P3.** A diet suited to the human species should provide all nutrients without supplements.
4. **C.** So the vegan diet isn't suited to the human species.

P1 and P2 are true: vegans need a reliable source of B12. The problem lies in P3: no scientific criterion says a healthy diet must do without supplements, and the B12 in meat also comes, in large part, from supplementation.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P3.

## 3. Answer for children

Vitamin B12 is made by tiny bacteria, not by animals. Farm animals also get this vitamin in their food, because plants don't have it. So people who eat meat also get the vitamin from a supplement, it just passes through the animal first. Taking the vitamin directly is simpler.

## 4. Short answer

B12 is produced by bacteria, not by animals. Farmed pigs and chickens receive B12 in their feed precisely because they don't get it from plants, and ruminants often receive cobalt so the bacteria in their stomachs can produce it. Much of the B12 meat eaters get also comes from supplementation, just indirectly. And "natural" isn't the criterion: we use vaccines, treated water, iodized salt and fortified flour without thinking this makes our lives wrong. Supplementing isn't a flaw in the diet; it's looking after your health, ideally with professional guidance.

**Question to ask back:** "If the animals you eat get B12 in their feed, doesn't your diet also depend on a supplement?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument ignores where B12 comes from:

| Who | Where the B12 comes from |
| --- | --- |
| Bacteria | They produce B12 |
| Farmed pigs and poultry | They receive B12 in their feed |
| Cattle and sheep | Rumen bacteria produce B12 from cobalt, which is often added to feed or mineral licks |
| Meat eaters | They get the B12 the animals received |
| Vegans | They get B12 from supplements or fortified foods, directly |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. B12 comes from bacteria.** Neither plants nor animals produce B12. Animals get it from bacteria, from soil, water or their own gut flora.

**2. Farmed animals receive supplements.** In modern livestock farming, pigs and poultry receive B12 in the vitamin premixes in their feed, and ruminants often receive cobalt. Part of the B12 in meat is thus a supplement that has passed through an animal.

**3. "Natural" isn't a criterion.** Chapter C1 shows that natural isn't synonymous with good. We use many life-saving technologies that aren't natural.

**4. Supplementing and fortifying are common in every diet.** Salt is iodized; wheat and corn flour are fortified with iron and folic acid in many countries; many milks and cereals have added vitamins; farmed animals receive vitamin and mineral premixes in their feed. And much of what we eat is already a blend of ingredients. There's nothing wrong with supplementing, as long as it's to look after health, ideally with guidance from a doctor or dietitian. No one thinks iodized salt makes everyone's diet inadequate.

**5. Meat eaters also have B12 deficiency, but at a much lower rate.** An estimated 10% to 15% of people over 60 are deficient, mainly because they absorb less B12 from food; that's why supplementation is recommended for them too. Among vegetarians and vegans, the deficiency found in studies ranged from 11% to 90%, depending on the criterion, at a time when many didn't supplement. The comparison shows both things: eating meat doesn't guarantee enough B12, and those who eat no animal products run a much greater risk if they don't take the supplement.

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: the risk of forgetting the supplement

The most serious version grants all this, but says depending on a supplement creates a risk: whoever forgets or doesn't know can develop serious deficiencies, while meat eaters are protected without thinking about it.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **The risk is solved with information.** A cheap supplement or fortified foods solve the problem.
2. **Meat eaters can be deficient too.** Meat is no guarantee, especially in older people.
3. **Manageable risks don't justify the harm.** The inconvenience of remembering a supplement doesn't outweigh the animals' lives.

### (f) Back to the moral question

B12 is the most serious point in the nutrition debate, and it has a simple solution. If a cheap supplement is enough to make raising and slaughtering animals unnecessary, the moral question remains: what justifies their suffering?

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Uses iodized salt and fortified flour.** Supplemented foods are part of any diet.
2. **Eats meat from supplemented animals.** The B12 in meat comes, in part, from feed.
3. **Accepts medicines and vaccines.** Which aren't natural either.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "a diet that needs a supplement isn't adequate".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Iodized salt | Almost everyone's diet would be inadequate | Iodine is added to prevent deficiency |
| Older people who eat meat | Their diet would be inadequate, since many need supplemental B12 | No one concludes this |
| Farmed animals | Their diet would be inadequate, because they receive B12 | And it's the diet that supplies the meat |

## 11. What the argument gets right

B12 is the point where the argument is most right: vegans need a reliable source of this vitamin, and ignoring that can cause serious harm. Animal advocates should always say so clearly.

But needing a supplement doesn't make a diet wrong. The B12 in meat also comes from supplements and bacteria, and the vegan solution is simple, cheap and safe.
