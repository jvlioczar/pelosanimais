# K7. "I don't kill; I just eat"

**Thesis:** it's outsourced morality. Whoever pays for an act takes part in it. Slaughter happens because someone buys the meat, and the slaughterhouse worker does, to order, what the consumer doesn't want to see. Among humans, no one accepts that commissioning a harm is innocent just because someone else carried it out.

**Family:** K. Effectiveness and individual responsibility · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "I just buy it. The butcher is the one who kills."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Those who kill the animals are the slaughterhouse workers.
2. **P2.** The consumer doesn't take part directly in the slaughter.
3. **P3.** Moral responsibility falls on whoever performs the act.
4. **C.** So those who only eat meat aren't responsible for the animals' deaths.

P1 and P2 are true. The problem lies in P3: moral responsibility isn't limited to whoever carries out the act; it includes whoever commissions and funds it.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P3.

## 3. Answer for children

Imagine someone pays another person to break a classmate's toy. The one who paid is to blame too, right? Meat is similar: whoever buys it pays for the animal to be killed, even without seeing it.

## 4. Short answer

Whoever pays for an act takes part in it. Slaughter happens because someone buys the meat; the slaughterhouse worker does, to order, what the consumer doesn't want to see. Among humans, no one accepts that commissioning a harm is innocent just because someone else carried it out. And if you couldn't kill the animal yourself, perhaps that says something about the act you're paying for.

**Question to ask back:** "If you wouldn't kill the animal with your own hands, why pay for someone else to?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument separates the act from the order:

| Role | What they do | Responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slaughterhouse worker | Carries out the slaughter | Direct, but under economic pressure |
| Company | Organizes production | Large |
| Consumer | Pays and creates the demand | Real: they place the order |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Paying is taking part.** Moral responsibility includes whoever commissions and funds an act, not only whoever carries it out.

**2. Slaughter exists because of the purchase.** Without demand, there'd be no slaughter (K2).

**3. The worker does what the consumer doesn't want to do.** Slaughterhouse work is dangerous and grueling, and it's delegated to others precisely because it's hard to face.

**4. Distance makes denial easier.** Psychology shows that people dissociate meat from the animal and the slaughter to reduce discomfort.

**5. Among humans, outsourcing doesn't absolve.** Commissioning a crime is a crime.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Benningstad and Kunst, 2020, *Appetite*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31830517/) | People dissociate meat from its animal origin to reduce discomfort | A systematic review |
| [Kagan, *Do I Make a Difference?* (2011)](https://campuspress.yale.edu/shellykagan/papers) | Each purchase has a real expected effect on production | Also used in K1 and K2 |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: one purchase has a minimal effect

The most serious version grants that whoever buys takes part in some sense, but says the effect of an individual purchase is so small that the responsibility is practically nil.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **The expected effect is real.** As chapter K1 shows.
2. **Responsibility doesn't depend only on effect.** Taking part in a harmful practice counts too.
3. **Small contributions add up.** The slaughter of billions of animals is made of individual purchases.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The argument tries to shift the responsibility onto others. But the animal dies because someone paid for it. The moral question is for whoever pays: what justifies commissioning the death of another being?

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in K7 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moral | Outsourced morality | Assigns responsibility only to whoever carries out the act |
| Epistemological | Dissociation | Separates meat from the animal and the slaughter |
| Logical | False distinction | Treats ordering and carrying out as independent |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Doesn't accept that commissioning a crime is innocent.**
2. **Wouldn't kill the animal personally.** Which suggests they see something wrong in the act.
3. **Knows the slaughterhouse kills in order to sell.** And buys.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains K7 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if someone else carries out the act, whoever pays bears no responsibility".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commissioning a crime | It would be innocent | It's a crime |
| Buying products of slave labor | There'd be no responsibility | No one accepts this |
| Buying meat | There'd be no responsibility | The same error |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that consumers aren't directly present at the slaughter, and that companies and the system bear great responsibility. It's also true that slaughterhouse workers face hard conditions and deserve attention.

But distance doesn't erase participation. Slaughter is done for those who buy, and those who buy are part of the chain that decides the animal's death.
