# A24. "Species is biology, race is a social construct"

**Thesis:** the argument tries to show that speciesism isn't like racism, because species would be a real biological category and race a social construct. But being biological doesn't make a criterion fair. A slavery based on height would use a biological trait and be as unjust as any other. Racism is unjust because it gives someone's interests biased treatment, not because of where the concept of race came from. The same holds for speciesism.

**Family:** A. Sentience and moral status · **Part:** DIR

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "You can't compare speciesism with racism. Race doesn't exist, it's a social invention. Species really exists, it's biology."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Racism and sexism are unjust because they use categories that are social constructs, with no real biological basis.
2. **P2.** Species is a real biological category, with genetic, reproductive and evolutionary differences.
3. **C.** So using species as a moral criterion isn't unjust the way racism is.

Premise P2 has support: species is a scientific concept, even though there's debate about how to define it. And P1 echoes current criticism of the biological concept of race.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P1: what makes racism unjust isn't the origin of the concept.

## 3. Answer for children

Imagine a school where the shortest children had to carry the tallest children's backpacks. Height is something about the body; it's real. Even so, the rule would be unfair, wouldn't it? What makes a rule unfair isn't whether the difference is real or made up. It's someone suffering just because they were born different.

## 4. Short answer

Being biological doesn't make a criterion fair. Imagine enslaving people by height: height is biological, and that slavery would be as unjust as any other. Racism is wrong because it disregards someone's interests because of a trait that doesn't change how much they can suffer. Species does the same. And if it were ever shown that races exist biologically, racism would still be wrong.

**Question to ask back:** "If height is biological, would a slavery based on height be just?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

| Question | The argument's answer | The correct answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is species real? | Yes | Yes, in general |
| Is race real? | No | Scientific debate aside, it's irrelevant here |
| What makes discrimination unjust? | Using an invented category | Giving less weight to someone's interests because of an irrelevant trait |

The argument gets the facts right and the theory of injustice wrong.

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. It doesn't explain why the criterion's origin matters.** The argument assumes, without justifying it, that a biological criterion would be fair and a social one unfair.

**2. There are clearly unjust biological criteria.** Height, eye color, blood type and sex are biological. Using them to decide who may be enslaved or killed would be unjust.

**3. Racism doesn't depend on the social construction thesis.** If races were shown to have some biological basis, racism would be just as unjust as before. The injustice lies in giving people's interests biased treatment.

**4. Species doesn't measure what matters.** It doesn't tell us whether someone can suffer, or how much. So it's as irrelevant to moral consideration as race (A10).

**5. The comparison is of structure, not of victims.** Saying speciesism and racism have the same logical structure doesn't equate the victims or minimize racism (L7).

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: species carries real capacities with it

The most serious version says species doesn't matter in itself, but because it comes with real differences in capacity: language, reason, culture. Race brings none of that; species does.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Then the criterion is capacity, not species.** The argument becomes A1 or A11, and runs into species overlap: there are humans without these capacities.
2. **The capacities cited don't measure suffering.** Language and culture don't say how much someone suffers when confined or killed.
3. **The analogy with racism holds.** Racists also claimed real differences in capacity between races. The error wasn't only in the facts, but in using irrelevant capacities to deny interests.

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Would reject discrimination by height.** Which is also biological.
2. **Condemns sexism.** Which rests on real biological differences between the sexes.
3. **Says species is just biology.** And gives it a moral weight biology doesn't give.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if the criterion is biological, discriminating by it isn't unjust".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Height | Enslaving short people would be acceptable | No one accepts this |
| Sex | Denying women rights would be acceptable | Rejected as sexism |
| Species | Exploiting animals would be acceptable | This is the argument's conclusion |

## 11. What the argument gets right

Species are biological categories, and there are real differences between humans and other animals. Those differences justify different treatment in many cases: a pig doesn't need to vote or go to school.

But different treatment isn't disregard. A difference of species doesn't change the fact that a pig can suffer, and that's what matters when we decide whether it's fair to make him suffer.
