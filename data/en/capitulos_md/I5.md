# I5. "Pig organs will save thousands of lives"

**Thesis:** the organ shortage is a real and serious problem, and xenotransplantation is a serious attempt to solve it. But it requires breeding genetically modified pigs, in sterile facilities, to remove their organs. The first patients, in 2022 and 2024, lived about two months. Before creating a new form of exploitation at scale, there are paths that don't require killing animals: increasing human organ donation, artificial organs and organs grown from cells.

**Family:** I. Other uses: testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companionship · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "People are dying on the transplant list. If a pig heart saves a life, what's the harm?"

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Thousands of people die waiting for an organ.
2. **P2.** Organs from genetically modified pigs can fill that gap.
3. **P3.** Saving human lives is worth more than these pigs' lives.
4. **C.** So xenotransplantation is justified.

P1 is true. P2 is a promise still being tested. P3 depends on there being no alternatives, and there are.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** depends on P2 and P3, both debatable.

## 3. Answer for children

Some people need a new heart or kidney, and sometimes there's no donor. Scientists are trying to use organs from modified pigs. But for that they need to breed pigs just to take their organs. Other scientists are trying to make artificial organs, or organs from the person's own cells, without needing animals.

## 4. Short answer

The organ shortage is real and serious, but xenotransplantation requires breeding genetically modified pigs, in sterile facilities, just to remove their organs. The first patients, in 2022 and 2024, lived about two months. Before creating a new exploitation at scale, there are paths that don't kill animals: increasing human organ donation, artificial organs and organs grown from cells.

**Question to ask back:** "If human organ donation increased, would it still be necessary to breed pigs for this?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

| Path to the organ shortage | Does it require breeding and killing animals? |
| --- | --- |
| Xenotransplantation with modified pigs | Yes |
| Increasing human organ donation | No |
| Artificial organs and mechanical devices | No |
| Organs grown from human cells | No, or far less |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. It's still experimental.** David Bennett received a genetically modified pig heart in January 2022, at the University of Maryland, and died two months later. Richard Slayman received a pig kidney in March 2024, at Massachusetts General Hospital, and died about two months later; the hospital said there was no indication that his death resulted from the transplant.

**2. The pigs are bred for this.** The organs come from pigs with multiple gene edits, kept in isolated facilities to avoid infections, and killed to remove the organs. In Bennett's case, a pig virus was found in the organ.

**3. There are alternatives that don't require animals.** Policies that increase human organ donation, such as the presumed consent adopted by several countries, artificial organs and organs grown from cells reduce the shortage without creating a new form of exploitation.

**4. The comparison with the plate is revealing.** "We already eat pigs" shows that pigs are already used without need. That doesn't make one more use acceptable; it shows how much of the current use is dispensable.

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: human lives are at stake now

The most serious version says patients are dying on the list today, and that animal-free alternatives are still far from solving the problem.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Xenotransplantation is also far off.** The first cases show there's still much to solve.
2. **Human donation can grow now.** Donation policies can save lives without breeding animals for it.
3. **A tragic case doesn't justify an industry.** Even if a single transplant were defensible, breeding pigs at scale to harvest organs is a new form of exploitation.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The question is whether it's fair to breed animals just to take their organs when there are paths that don't require it. Patients' urgency is real; it calls for investing in those paths.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in I5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | False dilemma | Either pig organs or deaths on the list |
| Epistemological | Treating a promise as a result | An experimental technique is presented as a solution |
| Moral | A new exploitation without weighing alternatives | Urgency makes it unnecessary to compare paths |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Wants to save those on the list.** And hasn't registered as an organ donor.
2. **Says we already eat pigs.** As if one unnecessary use justified another.
3. **Would accept breeding animals for organs.** But not pets.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if it can save human lives, breeding animals for it is justified".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Breeding dogs to harvest organs | It would be justified | Almost no one accepts that |
| Investing in human donation | It would stop being a priority | It's the path with no new victims |
| Organs grown from cells | The same goal, without breeding animals | This is the alternative |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that the organ shortage kills many people, that xenotransplantation is serious research, and that in the face of imminent death the choice is tragic.

But the solution doesn't need to be a new industry of animals bred to have their organs removed. Human donation, artificial and grown organs also aim to save these lives, without new victims.
