# I10. "In sport fishing the fish is released"

**Thesis:** releasing the fish doesn't undo what happened before. The hook wounds the mouth or throat, the fight causes exhaustion, the time out of water causes suffocation. A review of 274 estimates found an average mortality of 18% after release, with some cases as high as 95%. And even when the fish survives, the pain and stress were caused for fun.

**Family:** I. Other uses: testing, clothing, entertainment · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "I catch and release. It's just a little hook. Fishing is my therapy."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** In sport fishing, the fish is returned to the water.
2. **P2.** Most released fish survive.
3. **P3.** If the fish survives, there's no significant harm.
4. **C.** So sport fishing is harmless.

P1 is true. So is P2, on average. The problem lies in P3: surviving isn't the same as not having been hurt, and a significant share die afterwards.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P3.

## 3. Answer for children

Imagine a hook stuck in your mouth, pulling you to a place where you can't breathe. Even if they let you go afterwards, you'd be hurt and very scared. It's similar for fish: they feel pain, and some die after being released.

## 4. Short answer

Releasing the fish doesn't undo the hook in its mouth, the fight to exhaustion and the time without breathing out of water. A review of 274 estimates found an average mortality of 18% after release, with some cases as high as 95%, depending on where the hook wounds and how the fish is handled. And even when it survives, the fish felt pain and stress for someone's fun.

**Question to ask back:** "If the fish feels pain, does releasing it afterwards undo what it felt?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument assesses only the end of the story:

| Stage | What happens to the fish |
| --- | --- |
| Hooking | The hook pierces the mouth, throat or other tissue |
| The fight | Exhaustion and lactic acid build-up |
| Out of water | Suffocation, damage to the gills and the mucus layer |
| Release | Some die afterwards; survivors are injured and stressed |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. There's mortality after release.** Bartholomew and Bohnsack reviewed 274 estimates and found a median mortality of 11% and a mean of 18%, ranging from 0% to 95%.

**2. Where the hook lands matters a lot.** Fish hooked in the throat or gills die far more often. Natural bait, deep hooking and prolonged handling increase mortality.

**3. Fish feel pain.** The New York Declaration recognizes a realistic possibility of consciousness in all vertebrates, including fish (A2).

**4. The harm is caused for fun.** There's no necessity: sport fishing is recreation.

**5. "Therapy" can be found in other ways.** Being in nature, by the water, doesn't require hurting an animal.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Bartholomew and Bohnsack, 2005, *Reviews in Fish Biology and Fisheries*](https://www3.carleton.ca/fecpl/pdfs/Bartholomew%20Review.pdf) | Post-release mortality with a median of 11% and a mean of 18%, ranging from 0% to 95%, across 274 estimates | A review of 53 studies |
| [The New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness (2024)](https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration/declaration) | A realistic possibility of consciousness in all vertebrates, including fish | Signed by hundreds of researchers |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: good practice reduces mortality

The most serious version points out that circle hooks, barbless hooks, artificial lures and quick handling greatly reduce mortality.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Reducing mortality doesn't eliminate pain.** The fish is still hooked and pulled from the water.
2. **Not everyone follows good practice.** The average reflects real practice.
3. **The question remains.** Why hurt an animal for fun, even carefully?

### (f) Back to the moral question

Sport fishing is perhaps the clearest case of harm caused without necessity: it isn't for food, it's for fun. The moral question becomes sharp: is it fair to cause an animal pain and fear for recreation?

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in I10 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Omission | Considers the release and ignores what comes before |
| Epistemological | Not knowing the mortality | Assumes all released fish survive |
| Moral | Fun as justification | Recreation outweighs the animal's pain |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Wouldn't accept the same with dogs.** Hooking and releasing a dog would be considered cruelty.
2. **Knows the fish thrashes.** Which suggests pain and fear.
3. **Seeks nature for wellbeing.** And causes distress to another being.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if the animal survives, the harm doesn't matter".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Animal fights without deaths | They'd be acceptable | They're crimes |
| Cruelty to dogs who recover | It would be acceptable | It's a crime |
| Sport fishing | It would be harmless | The fish suffered and sometimes dies |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that releasing a fish is better than killing it, and that good practice greatly reduces mortality. Fishing also has real value for those who do it: contact with nature, calm and companionship.

But those values don't depend on hurting an animal. And the harm caused before the release is still caused for fun.
