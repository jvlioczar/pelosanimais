# L9. "Hitler was a vegetarian"

**Thesis:** it's the reductio ad Hitlerum: trying to discredit an idea by associating it with a hated figure. Even if Hitler had been vegetarian, that would say nothing about veganism, just as his not smoking doesn't make cigarettes good. And the historical question is disputed: there are accounts of him eating meat at various times, and historians debate whether "vegetarian" accurately describes his diet.

**Family:** L. Attacks on veganism and vegans · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Hitler loved animals. The Nazis passed animal protection laws."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Hitler and the Nazi regime expressed concern for animals.
2. **P2.** That same regime committed terrible crimes against humans.
3. **P3.** So concern for animals can coexist with contempt for humans, and isn't a sign of virtue.
4. **C.** So veganism has no moral superiority.

P1 and P2 have a historical basis, with debates. P3 is a reasonable observation: concern for animals doesn't guarantee virtue. The problem is that none of this says whether it's right to eat animals.

The argument is **valid** to show that no one is good just for caring about animals. It fails as an objection to veganism.

## 3. Answer for children

Some very bad people did ordinary things, like drinking water or liking dogs. That doesn't make those things bad. What matters is whether an idea is right, not who else thought something similar. Hurting animals without needing to doesn't become right just because a bad person also didn't eat meat.

## 4. Short answer

Even if Hitler had been vegetarian, that would say nothing about veganism, just as his not smoking doesn't make cigarettes good. It's the so-called reductio ad Hitlerum: associating an idea with a hated figure to avoid discussing it. And the historical question is disputed: there are accounts of him eating meat at various times. What matters is whether the arguments about the animals are good.

**Question to ask back:** "Hitler didn't smoke. Does that make cigarettes a good choice?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument tries to transfer a person's reputation to an idea:

| What the argument does | Why it doesn't work |
| --- | --- |
| Associates veganism with Hitler | An idea doesn't inherit the character of those who practice it |
| Uses disputed historical information | Even if true, it would be irrelevant |
| Suggests concern for animals is suspect | Many animal advocates have also been human rights advocates |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. It's the reductio ad Hitlerum.** Associating an idea with a hated figure doesn't show it's wrong.

**2. It's guilt by association.** An idea doesn't become wrong because of who practices it (J18).

**3. The historical question is disputed.** There are accounts of Hitler eating meat at different times, and historians debate whether "vegetarian" accurately describes his diet.

**4. The Nazi regime used animals in experiments.** Its supposed concern for animals was selective and instrumental.

**5. Concern for animals doesn't require contempt for humans.** A small neuroimaging study even suggested the opposite, that vegans and vegetarians react more strongly to human suffering; it's modest evidence, but nothing links concern for animals to contempt for people (L20).

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Berry, *Hitler: Neither Vegetarian Nor Animal Lover* (2004)](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL2701073W) | Argues that Hitler wasn't vegetarian in the strict sense | Pythagorean Publishers; the subject is debated by historians |
| [Filippi et al., 2010, *PLoS ONE*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2877098/) | Vegetarians and vegans showed greater activation of empathy-related areas when viewing scenes of human suffering | A neuroimaging study with 60 participants |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: concern for animals can be misanthropic

The most serious version says that in some cases concern for animals goes hand in hand with contempt for humans, and that the Nazi example shows this risk.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Any value can be distorted.** That doesn't make the value wrong.
2. **Most animal advocates also defend humans.** And empathy isn't zero-sum (L20).
3. **The question about the animals remains.** No historical figure answers it.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Associating veganism with Hitler is a way of avoiding the conversation. The moral question is the same, whoever ate or didn't eat meat: is it fair to make animals suffer and die when we don't need to?

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in L9 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Reductio ad Hitlerum | Associates the idea with a hated figure |
| Logical | Guilt by association | Judges the idea by who practiced it |
| Epistemological | Disputed historical claim | Presents as fact something that is debated |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Doesn't use the argument for other habits.** Hitler didn't smoke either, and no one defends cigarettes for that reason.
2. **Accepts today's animal protection laws.** Which exist in almost every country.
3. **Likes pets.** Like many people, including Hitler.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains L9 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do-gooder derogation | Associating vegans with the worst eases discomfort | [Minson and Monin, 2012](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/12/Minson---Monin,-2011.pdf) |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a hated figure practiced something, that practice is bad".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not smoking | It would be bad, because Hitler didn't smoke | Absurd |
| Liking dogs | It would be suspect, because Hitler had dogs | Absurd |
| Building highways | It would be bad, because the Nazis built motorways | Absurd |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that concern for animals doesn't make anyone virtuous, and that violent regimes have used animal protection rhetoric selectively. No one should assume that someone who cares for animals is automatically good.

But that says nothing about whether it's right to eat animals. The question needs to be answered with arguments, not associations.
