# H20. "Kangaroo meat is the most sustainable meat there is"

**Thesis:** kangaroo meat comes from wild animals, with no farming, little methane and no clearing for pasture, and it's true that its environmental impact is lower than beef's. But it comes from commercial shooting of wild animals at scale: in New South Wales alone, more than 500,000 kangaroos were killed in 2023. The rules allow shooting females with young, and the code provides for orphaned joeys to be killed by a blow to the head or decapitation. A lower environmental impact doesn't change what happens to the animals.

**Family:** H. Environment, ecology and agriculture · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Kangaroo is the greenest meat there is: it's wild, doesn't emit methane and there are too many kangaroos in Australia."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Kangaroos are wild and need no farming, pasture or feed.
2. **P2.** They emit much less methane than cattle and sheep.
3. **P3.** Populations are abundant and the harvest is regulated by quotas.
4. **C.** So eating kangaroo meat is the most sustainable choice.

P1 and P2 are true. P3 is partly true. The problem is that the conclusion treats environmental impact as the only question.

The argument is **valid** from an environmental standpoint. Its **soundness** fails when the conclusion is used as a moral justification.

| Context | Typical form |

## 3. Answer for children

In Australia, people go out at night to shoot kangaroos and sell the meat. When they kill a mother kangaroo, the joey in her pouch is killed too. Kangaroo meat pollutes less than beef, but the kangaroos are still being killed.

## 4. Short answer

It's true that kangaroo meat has a lower environmental impact than beef: they're wild animals, with no pasture or feed, and emit little methane. But it comes from commercial shooting at scale: in New South Wales alone, more than 500,000 kangaroos were killed in 2023. The rules allow shooting females with young, and the code provides for orphaned joeys to be killed by a blow to the head. A lower environmental impact doesn't change what happens to the animals.

**Question to ask back:** "If the problem is meat's environmental impact, is the answer to switch animals, or to eat foods that don't require killing any?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Is its environmental impact lower than beef's? | Yes |
| Does it require killing animals? | Yes, at scale |
| Are joeys affected? | Yes; there is no official count |
| Is there an alternative without killing? | Yes, plant foods |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. It's shooting at scale.** Australia's annual commercial quota runs into the millions, and the number of animals actually killed is below it. In New South Wales, the government recorded 509,671 kangaroos killed in 2023.

**2. Joeys are invisible victims.** According to RSPCA Australia, the commercial code of practice provides for the young of shot females to be killed, and there are no official statistics on how many; organizations' estimates run to hundreds of thousands a year.

**3. "There are too many kangaroos" is a disputed claim.** Populations vary greatly with droughts, and population control is a debate of its own, which doesn't depend on selling the meat (H19, N14).

**4. The best for the environment doesn't require killing.** Swapping beef for plant foods cuts the impact even further, without shooting.

| Source | What it says | Note |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: the harvest is regulated and populations are stable

The most serious version points out that quotas are set from population counts, that shooting is done by licensed shooters, and that kangaroo populations remain abundant.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Regulating isn't making harmless.** The rule sets how many die, not whether it's fair to kill them (B10).
2. **Oversight in the field is limited.** Shooting happens at night, in remote areas, and the fate of joeys isn't counted.
3. **The environmental question has a better answer.** If the goal is to cut meat's impact, plant foods do it without killing.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The question is whether it's fair to kill wild animals at scale, joeys included, for food, when there are foods that don't require it. A lower environmental impact doesn't answer that question.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in H20 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Selective comparison | Compares only with beef, not with plant foods |
| Epistemological | Ignoring the joeys | The impact on joeys is left out of the account |
| Moral | The environment as the only criterion | Lower impact justifies killing wild animals |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Values wild animals.** And supports killing them at scale.
2. **Talks about population control.** And depends on selling the meat to justify the shooting.
3. **Would reject shooting koalas.** And accepts shooting kangaroos.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains H20 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Functional categorization | A kangaroo seen as "pest" or "meat" loses moral weight | [Bratanova, Loughnan and Bastian, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21569805/) |
| Moral licensing | The "green" choice gives permission to ignore the rest | [Blanken, van de Ven and Zeelenberg, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167215572134) |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a meat has a lower environmental impact, eating it is the best choice".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Insects farmed at scale | They'd be the best choice | See H18 |
| Shooting any abundant wild animal | It'd be the best choice | Ignores suffering and the young |
| Plant foods | Their impact is even lower | Without killing animals |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that kangaroo meat has a lower environmental impact than beef, that kangaroos are wild and abundant in many areas, and that swapping beef for it would cut emissions.

But the right comparison is with foods that don't require killing animals. And no environmental calculation answers why kill wild animals, and their young, when there's no need.
