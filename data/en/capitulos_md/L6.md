# L6. "Meat is for real men"

**Thesis:** it's a gender stereotype. The link between meat and masculinity is cultural: a 2012 study showed, across six experiments, that many people associate meat, especially red meat, with masculinity, and that this shows up even in the grammar of several languages. But masculinity is neither a moral nor a nutritional criterion. Vegan strength athletes, fighters and bodybuilders show that strength doesn't depend on meat.

**Family:** L. Attacks on veganism and vegans · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Real men eat meat. Salad is for women."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** In many cultures, meat is associated with strength, virility and the male role.
2. **P2.** These associations are part of many men's identity.
3. **P3.** Giving up meat threatens that identity.
4. **C.** So it's understandable and legitimate for men to reject veganism.

P1 is true and documented. So is P2. P3 has a real psychological point. The problem lies in the conclusion: a cultural association isn't a moral reason, and the idea of masculinity can change.

The argument is **valid** as a psychological explanation. It fails as a justification.

## 3. Answer for children

There's no such thing as boys' food or girls' food. Boys and girls, men and women can eat anything. There are very strong male athletes who don't eat any meat at all. Being strong and brave has nothing to do with what you eat.

## 4. Short answer

The link between meat and masculinity is cultural, and it's been documented: a 2012 study showed, across six experiments, that many people associate red meat with men. But masculinity is neither a moral nor a nutritional criterion. There are vegan strength athletes, fighters and bodybuilders. Being a man doesn't depend on what's on the plate, and courage can also mean changing when you realize something is wrong.

**Question to ask back:** "What's masculine about paying for an animal to be killed?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument uses a cultural association as a justification:

| Claim | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Meat is associated with masculinity | True, in many cultures |
| So men should eat meat | Doesn't follow: a cultural association isn't a norm |
| Men who don't eat meat are weak | False: there are high-level vegan athletes |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. It's a stereotype.** The link between meat and masculinity is cultural, not biological or moral.

**2. The association is documented, and that explains it.** Rozin and colleagues showed in 2012 that many people associate meat, especially red meat, with masculinity, and that this shows up in several languages with grammatical gender.

**3. Strength doesn't depend on meat.** With enough protein, vegans gain mass and strength equally or very similarly to omnivores (G11).

**4. Masculinity isn't a moral criterion.** What's right or wrong doesn't depend on whether it's associated with men or women.

**5. Ideas of masculinity change.** Many behaviors once seen as "unmanly" are now common among men.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Rozin et al., 2012, *Journal of Consumer Research*](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jconrs:doi:10.1086/664970) | Six studies show a metaphorical association between mammal meat and masculinity | Includes an analysis of 23 languages with grammatical gender |
| [Hevia-Larraín and colleagues, 2021, *Sports Medicine*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-021-01434-9) | With the same protein intake, vegans and omnivores had the same gains in mass and strength | Also used in G11 |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: masculine identity matters to many men

The most serious version points out that for many men masculine identity is central, and that proposals threatening it provoke rejection, which harms the cause.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **It's a question of communication.** And it can be addressed with examples of strong, respected vegan men.
2. **Identity can include other values.** Such as protecting the weaker.
3. **The moral question remains.** No identity answers it.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The stereotype explains part of the resistance, but doesn't justify the animals' deaths. The moral question is the same for men and women: is it fair to make animals suffer and die when we don't need to?

| Logical | Stereotype | Associates a food with a gender |

| Epistemological | False link between meat and strength | Ignores vegan athletes |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Admires strong athletes.** Even vegan ones, once they meet them.
2. **Doesn't follow other gender stereotypes in everything.**
3. **Knows strength comes from training and nutrition.** Not from one particular food.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains L6 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Masculinity and meat | Meat is associated with masculinity | [Rozin et al., 2012](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jconrs:doi:10.1086/664970) |
| Group identity | Giving up meat seems to threaten one's social role | General literature on identity-protective cognition |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if something is associated with masculinity, men should do it".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not crying | Men shouldn't express emotions | Now widely criticized |
| Not caring for children | Men shouldn't care for their children | Now widely criticized |
| Eating meat | Men should eat meat | The same error |

## 11. What the argument gets right

The link between meat and masculinity is real, deep and documented, and it explains part of the resistance to veganism among men. Understanding this matters for communicating better and showing examples of vegan men.

But a cultural association isn't a moral reason. Masculinity can include strength, courage and also the choice not to cause unnecessary suffering.
