# L6. "Vegans care more about animals than people"

**Thesis:** it's a false dilemma and a generalization. Empathy isn't a zero-sum resource: caring about animals doesn't reduce caring about people. A small 2010 neuroimaging study found that vegetarians and vegans showed greater activation of empathy-related areas in response to human suffering too; it's a hint, not proof. And many animal advocates also work for human rights.

**Family:** L. Attacks on veganism and vegans · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "You cry for the cow but not for the homeless. Vegans hate people."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Some vegans show more indignation at animal suffering than at human suffering.
2. **P2.** Humans should have moral priority.
3. **P3.** Whoever reverses that priority has distorted values.
4. **C.** So veganism reflects distorted values.

P1 may be true of some. The problem lies in the conclusion: it generalizes from a few and assumes that caring about animals takes care away from humans.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails because it generalizes and assumes a competition that doesn't exist.

## 3. Answer for children

The heart has no limit on how many people or animals we can love. Caring for your dog doesn't make you love your family less. It's the same with vegans: caring for animals doesn't make anyone care less about people.

## 4. Short answer

Empathy isn't zero-sum: caring about animals doesn't reduce caring about people. A small 2010 study found that vegetarians and vegans showed greater activation of brain areas linked to empathy in response to human suffering too. Many animal advocates work for human rights, against hunger and for social justice. And no longer eating animals takes nothing away from anyone.

**Question to ask back:** "What would you stop doing for people if you stopped eating animals?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument assumes empathy is limited and competitive:

| Assumption | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Those who care about animals care less about humans | No evidence; there are hints of the opposite |
| Vegans in general despise humans | A baseless generalization |
| No longer eating animals takes something from humans | False (J6) |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. It's a false dilemma.** No one has to choose between animals and humans.

**2. Empathy isn't zero-sum.** Filippi and colleagues found in 2010 that vegetarians and vegans showed greater activation of empathy-related regions in response to scenes of human suffering, not less. The study is small, with 60 people, and measures brain activation, not behavior; but it points in the opposite direction from the stereotype.

**3. It's a generalization.** Judging all vegans by a few is unfair, as it would be for any group.

**4. Many animal advocates work for humans.** The animal movement has strong ties to social justice movements.

**5. Livestock farming also affects humans.** Slaughterhouse workers, communities affected by deforestation and public health (J6).

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Filippi et al., 2010, *PLoS ONE*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2877098/) | Vegetarians and vegans showed greater activation of the anterior cingulate cortex and inferior frontal gyrus in response to scenes of human suffering | A neuroimaging study with 60 participants |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: some vegans really do despise people

The most serious version points to real cases of vegans who make disdainful remarks about humans or compare people to animals offensively.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Some don't represent all.** There are unpleasant people in every group.
2. **Such behavior should be criticized.** Including by vegans themselves.
3. **It doesn't change the question about animals.** The question is still about them.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Caring about animals doesn't require despising anyone. The moral question stays the same: do animals deserve not to suffer and die without need? Answering yes takes nothing away from people.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in L6 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | False dilemma | Either animals or humans |
| Logical | Hasty generalization | Judges all vegans by a few |
| Epistemological | Empathy as zero-sum | Assumes caring for some reduces care for others |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Cares for pets.** And doesn't think that reduces care for people.
2. **Isn't helping the homeless person.** The argument serves to accuse, not to help.
3. **Admires people who care for both animals and people.**

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains L6 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do-gooder derogation | Discrediting those who act morally | [Minson and Monin, 2012](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/12/Minson---Monin,-2011.pdf) |
| Speciesism | Human interests always seem more important | [Caviola, Everett and Faber, 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29517258/) |
| Zero-sum bias | It assumes compassion is a fixed amount and that what goes to animals is missing for humans | [Meegan, 2010](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2010.00191) |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "whoever cares about one cause cares less about others".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Those who care for the elderly | They'd care less about children | Absurd |
| Environmentalists | They'd care less about people | Absurd |
| Vegans | They'd care less about humans | The same error |

## 11. What the argument gets right

Some vegans express themselves badly about humans, and disrespectful comparisons can hurt. Animal advocates should take care that their cause doesn't seem indifferent to human suffering.

But empathy isn't zero-sum, and the little evidence there is points away from the stereotype rather than toward it. There's no need to choose.
