# I32. "A shock collar is just a stimulus; it doesn't hurt the dog"

**Thesis:** a shock collar works because the stimulus is unpleasant: if it didn't bother the dog, it wouldn't stop barking. And it doesn't treat the cause of the barking, which is usually boredom, fear, separation anxiety or lack of exercise. A 2020 study compared training with an electronic collar to reward-based training and found no advantage for the collar; reward-based training had better results for some commands. Wales banned these collars in 2010, and England in 2024.

**Family:** I. Other uses: testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companionship · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "I put a shock collar on the dog to stop him barking. It's just a little vibration, it doesn't hurt."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Excessive barking causes conflicts with neighbors and can lead to the animal being abandoned.
2. **P2.** A shock collar reduces barking quickly.
3. **P3.** The stimulus is weak and causes no physical harm.
4. **C.** So using the collar is acceptable, and even protects the dog from being abandoned.

P1 is true. P2 may be true in the short term. P3 is self-contradictory: if the stimulus weren't aversive, it wouldn't work. And the conclusion ignores alternatives that treat the cause.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P3.

## 3. Answer for children

A dog barks for a reason: because it's alone, scared, bored or wants to play. A shock collar gives it a nasty fright every time it barks, but it doesn't fix what it's feeling. It's better to find out why it barks and help, with walks, games and company.

## 4. Short answer

The collar works because the stimulus is unpleasant: if it didn't bother the dog, it wouldn't stop barking. And it doesn't treat the cause, which is usually boredom, fear or separation anxiety. A 2020 study found no advantage of electronic-collar training over reward-based training, which had better results for some commands. Wales banned these collars in 2010, and England in 2024.

**Question to ask back:** "If the shock didn't bother the dog, why would it stop barking?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

| Common cause of barking | What the collar does | What solves the cause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Boredom, lack of exercise | Punishes the barking | Walks, play, enrichment |
| Separation anxiety | Punishes the barking and may increase fear | Gradual training, company, veterinary help |
| Fear or alertness | Links the stimulus to whatever causes fear | Desensitization, safety |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. The effect comes from discomfort.** The collar reduces barking because the dog learns to avoid an unpleasant stimulus. Calling it "just a stimulus" doesn't change how it works.

**2. The cause remains.** Barking is a symptom. Punishing it can silence the dog without solving the fear, boredom or anxiety, and can worsen its emotional state.

**3. Reward-based training works as well or better.** China, Mills and Cooper compared, in 2020, training with an electronic collar to other methods, with 63 dogs. The reward-based group responded better to some commands, and the authors found no advantage for the collar. Other researchers criticized limitations of the study.

**4. Some countries have banned them.** Wales banned shock collars in 2010, and England in February 2024.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [China, Mills and Cooper, 2020, *Frontiers in Veterinary Science*](https://doi.org/10.3389/fvets.2020.00508) | Reward-based training had better results for some commands, with no advantage for the electronic collar | 63 dogs; the study received methodological criticism |
| [The Kennel Club, ban in England](https://thekennelclub.org.uk/shockcollarsbanned) | Remote-controlled shock collars have been banned in England since 1 February 2024 | British dog organization |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: for some dogs, nothing else worked

The most serious version says some guardians have tried everything and that the collar was the last option before the dog was returned or abandoned.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Often "everything" didn't include treating the cause.** Help from a behavioral vet and a trainer who uses positive reinforcement solves many of these cases.
2. **Silencing the barking isn't solving the suffering.** An anxious, silent dog is still anxious.
3. **The comparison with abandonment doesn't justify the method.** The problem is the lack of support for the guardian, not the lack of shocks.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The question is whether it's fair to cause an animal discomfort to solve our own annoyance, without addressing what it's feeling. When there are methods that work without pain, the choice is clear.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in I32 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Internal contradiction | Says it doesn't bother the dog and relies on that discomfort to work |
| Epistemological | Not knowing the alternatives | Assumes only shocks work |
| Moral | The guardian's convenience above welfare | Silence is worth the animal's discomfort |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Says it doesn't hurt.** And expects the dog to stop because of it.
2. **Loves the dog.** And doesn't ask why it barks.
3. **Wouldn't use the same on a crying child.** And accepts it for the dog.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains I32 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Euphemism | "Stimulus" and "vibration" soften what is a shock | General literature on language and morality |
| Immediate reward | Quick silence seems proof that it worked | General literature on decision-making |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if the stimulus is weak and solves the problem, using it is acceptable".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shocks for children to stop crying | They'd be acceptable | No one accepts that |
| Bans in Wales and England | They'd be unjustified | They were based on welfare |
| Reward-based training | It solves the problem without discomfort | This is the alternative |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that excessive barking causes real conflicts, that guardians can feel they have no way out, and that the collar reduces barking in the short term.

But it works through discomfort, doesn't treat the cause, and there are methods that work without pain. The barking problem deserves to be solved from the dog's side.
