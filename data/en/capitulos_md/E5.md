# E5. "Veganism is opinion, not science"

**Thesis:** no moral position is "just science", including the position of eating meat. Science answers questions of fact, such as whether animals feel pain, and its answers support veganism: there is broad scientific support for vertebrate sentience and growing evidence for many invertebrates. Ethics answers questions of value, such as whether it's right to cause that pain without need. Both have methods, and neither is "just opinion".

**Family:** E. Relativism, freedom and evasions · **Part:** DIR

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "That's ideology, not fact. Science doesn't say eating meat is wrong."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Science describes facts; it doesn't tell us what we ought to do.
2. **P2.** Veganism is a claim about what we ought to do.
3. **P3.** What isn't science is just opinion.
4. **C.** So veganism is just opinion.

P1 and P2 are true: science alone doesn't produce moral norms. The problem lies in P3: if what isn't science is just opinion, then it's also just opinion that eating meat is acceptable, that stealing is wrong and that slavery was unjust.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P3, and the conclusion applies equally against the arguer's own position.

## 3. Answer for children

Science can find out whether a pig feels pain, and it already has: it does. But the question "is it right to hurt someone who feels pain?" is a different question. For that one, we use reason and ethics, which have their own rules too. And almost everyone agrees that hurting without need is wrong.

## 4. Short answer

Science doesn't say what's right, and it doesn't say so about any moral position, including eating meat. But it answers the factual questions that matter: do animals feel pain? Yes, and in 2024 scientists from several fields signed a declaration recognizing strong support for consciousness in mammals and birds. The moral question is whether it's right to cause that pain without need. That's ethics, which isn't science, but isn't "just opinion" either.

**Question to ask back:** "If everything that isn't science is just opinion, is saying slavery was wrong also just opinion?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The debate about eating animals involves two kinds of questions:

| Question | Type | What the answer says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do animals feel pain? | Fact: science | Yes, with strong support for mammals and birds and evidence for many others |
| Is it possible to live well without eating animals? | Fact: science | Yes, according to the main nutrition bodies |
| Is it right to cause suffering without need? | Value: ethics | Almost every ethical theory says no |

Veganism combines the scientific answers with a widely accepted moral principle.

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. It cuts both ways.** If ethics is just opinion, the claim that eating meat is acceptable is too. The argument favors no one.

**2. Ethics has methods.** Consistency, generalization, analysis of arguments and testing principles against similar cases are methods of moral philosophy. They make it possible to tell better positions from worse ones.

**3. The relevant facts are scientific.** The New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness (2024) states there is strong scientific support for attributing conscious experience to mammals and birds, and a realistic possibility for all vertebrates and many invertebrates.

**4. Almost everyone accepts the moral premise.** "Causing unnecessary suffering is wrong" is accepted by almost all people and theories. Veganism just applies that principle consistently.

**5. Asking for "the study" confuses categories.** There's no study proving slavery was immoral, and no one concludes that this is just opinion.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [The New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness (2024)](https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration/declaration) | Strong scientific support for consciousness in mammals and birds; a realistic possibility in all vertebrates and many invertebrates | Signed by hundreds of researchers |
| [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27886704/) | Well-planned vegan diets are appropriate at all stages of life | Answers the factual question about need |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: science doesn't say how much animals weigh

The most serious version grants the facts, but says science doesn't tell us how much animal suffering weighs against human interests, and that this weighing is subjective.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **The weighing doesn't need to be exact.** It's enough to see that the pleasure of a flavor weighs less than an animal's life and suffering.
2. **Moral weighing isn't arbitrary.** We use criteria such as severity of harm, necessity and alternatives, as in any other moral issue.
3. **The difficulty doesn't favor the status quo.** If we don't know the exact weight, that's a reason for caution, not for continuing to cause the harm.

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Makes moral judgments every day.** And doesn't consider them "just opinion".
2. **Accepts the science of sentience when it comes to dogs.** No one asks for "the study" to condemn mistreating a dog.
3. **Defends their own position as correct.** Which would be impossible if everything were just opinion.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "what isn't science is just opinion".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human rights | They'd be opinion, since they aren't scientific facts | No one accepts this |
| Condemning racism | It would be opinion | No one accepts this |
| The claim that eating meat is acceptable | It would also be just opinion | The argument turns against whoever uses it |

## 11. What the argument gets right

The distinction between facts and values is real and important. Science doesn't settle moral questions on its own, and it's healthy to distrust anyone who presents opinions as if they were data. It's also true that there are scientific uncertainties about the sentience of some animals.

But ethics isn't no man's land. It has methods, and what it asks here is little: to apply to animals that science recognizes as sentient a principle almost everyone already accepts.
