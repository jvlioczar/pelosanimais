# G8. "Children and pregnant women can't be vegan"

**Thesis:** the world's largest association of nutrition professionals, the US Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, considers well-planned vegan diets adequate at all stages of life, including pregnancy, lactation and childhood. Other bodies are more cautious: in 2024, the German Nutrition Society neither recommended nor advised against it for these groups, for lack of data. Across all positions, the common ground is careful planning and professional supervision, not a need for meat.

**Family:** G. Health and nutrition · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "That's child abuse. The baby won't develop. Pregnant women need meat."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Pregnant women, babies and children have greater and more delicate nutritional needs.
2. **P2.** Dietary mistakes at these stages can cause serious and sometimes irreversible harm.
3. **P3.** Vegan diets require planning and supplementation, and data on them in these groups is limited.
4. **C.** So children and pregnant women shouldn't follow vegan diets.

P1, P2 and P3 are true. The problem lies in the conclusion: what the premises justify is extra care and supervision, not prohibition.

The argument is **valid** as a warning. It fails as proof that the diet is impossible or dangerous in itself.

## 3. Answer for children

Children need lots of nutrients to grow. A vegan child can grow up strong and healthy if the family plans meals well and visits the doctor or dietitian regularly, taking the vitamins they recommend. The important thing is to eat well, with lots of different foods.

## 4. Short answer

The US Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics considers well-planned vegan diets adequate at all stages of life, including pregnancy and childhood. Other bodies, such as the German Nutrition Society, are more cautious and call for nutritional knowledge and professional supervision. The common ground is planning: B12, iodine, vitamin D, iron, omega-3 and enough energy. The serious cases reported in the news involve poorly planned restrictive diets, not well-supervised vegan diets.

**Question to ask back:** "Is a vegan diet planned with a dietitian the same as a poorly planned diet?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The main bodies don't all say the same thing, but they all converge on one point:

| Body | Position | Common ground |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics (US), 2016 | Adequate at all stages of life, if well planned | Planning |
| German Nutrition Society, 2024 | No recommendation for or against for pregnant women and children, for lack of data; calls for knowledge and supervision | Planning and supervision |
| Federal Council of Nutritionists (Brazil), 2022 | Possible at all life stages, if well planned; cites Brazil's dietary guidelines for children under 2, which accept a vegetarian diet in infancy with extra care | Planning |

None says meat is necessary; the disagreement is about the degree of caution.

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. The most cited nutrition body considers it adequate.** The Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics states that well-planned vegan diets are appropriate for pregnancy, lactation, infancy, childhood and adolescence.

**2. The cautious positions ask for care, not meat.** In 2024, the German Nutrition Society moved from "not recommended" to "no recommendation for or against" for these groups, and stresses that mistakes can have serious consequences, which requires knowledge and supervision.

**3. Serious cases involve poorly planned diets.** Reported cases of child malnutrition usually involve extremely restrictive diets, lack of B12 or energy, and no supervision. The same kind of neglect causes malnutrition on any diet.

**4. The critical nutrients are known.** B12, iodine, vitamin D, iron, zinc, calcium, omega-3 and enough energy. With supplementation and supervision, they can be secured.

**5. Diets with meat can also be inadequate.** Childhood obesity and anemia are common problems among children who eat meat.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |

| [German Nutrition Society (DGE), 2024 update](https://www.ernaehrungs-umschau.de/english-articles/24-02-2025-update-of-the-dge-position-on-vegan-diet) | For pregnant and breastfeeding women and children, no recommendation for or against for lack of data; requires nutritional knowledge and supervision | The more cautious position, softer than in 2016 |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: when in doubt, don't take risks with children

The most serious version says that, given uncertainty and the risk of irreversible harm, the precautionary principle says not to take risks with children, who can't choose.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Precaution calls for supervision, not meat.** The way to reduce the risk is to plan and monitor.
2. **Diets with meat carry risks too.** Precaution would apply to them as well.
3. **Children don't choose any diet.** Parents always decide; what matters is deciding responsibly.

### (f) Back to the moral question

This is the argument where caution is most justified, because it involves those who can't choose. But what it asks for is responsible feeding, not the death of animals. If a well-supervised vegan diet can nourish a child, the moral question still stands.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in G8 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Unwarranted generalization | From "requires care" concludes "is impossible" |
| Epistemological | Extreme cases as the rule | Uses cases of neglect to judge well-planned diets |
| Moral | Invented necessity | Treats a need for planning as a need for meat |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Doesn't question children's diets full of ultra-processed food.** Which also cause harm.
2. **Accepts other diets that require care.** Such as those of children with food allergies.
3. **Trusts nutrition bodies on other subjects.** But ignores their positions here.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains G8 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Availability bias | Reported cases of neglect seem representative | General literature on heuristics |
| Rationalization through the 4Ns | "Necessary" | [Piazza et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25865663/) |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a diet requires planning at a sensitive stage, it shouldn't be followed".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Children allergic to milk | They couldn't follow milk-free diets | They do, with supervision |
| Children with celiac disease | They couldn't follow gluten-free diets | They do, with supervision |
| Pregnant women in general | They shouldn't follow diets requiring extra folic acid and iron | Every pregnancy does |

## 11. What the argument gets right

This argument deserves more respect than many others. Pregnant women and children are vulnerable, mistakes can be serious, and there are serious bodies calling for caution for lack of data. Animal advocates shouldn't play this down.

But caution isn't prohibition. What all these bodies ask for is planning, knowledge and professional supervision, and with that a vegan diet can also nourish those just starting out in life.
