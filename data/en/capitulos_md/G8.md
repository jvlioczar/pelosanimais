# G8. "Plants have antinutrients"

**Thesis:** antinutrients exist, but in the usual amounts and with the usual preparation they matter little, and many of them bring benefits. Cooking, soaking, sprouting and fermenting reduce lectins, phytates and oxalates. Raw beans are harmful; cooked beans are a very nutritious food. And in large studies, eating legumes and whole grains is linked to lower mortality.

**Family:** G. Health and nutrition · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Beans have lectins. Spinach has oxalates. Plants have poisons to defend themselves."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Plants produce compounds that can reduce nutrient absorption or cause unwanted effects, such as lectins, phytates and oxalates.
2. **P2.** Plant-based diets contain more of these compounds.
3. **P3.** These compounds can harm health.
4. **C.** So plant-based diets are harmful, and animal foods are safer.

P1 is true. So is P2. The problem lies in P3: with the usual preparation, these compounds have small effects, and several of them are linked to benefits.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P3, which ignores dose and preparation.

## 3. Answer for children

Some plants have substances that can cause a bit of trouble, like raw beans. That's why we cook beans! Once cooked, they're tasty and very healthy. And scientists show that cooked beans are good for you.

## 4. Short answer

Antinutrients exist, but cooking, soaking, sprouting and fermenting reduce most of them. Raw beans are harmful; cooked beans are healthy, which is why no one eats raw beans. Many of these compounds, such as phytates and polyphenols, are even linked to benefits. And diets rich in legumes and whole grains are among those most linked to a long life.

**Question to ask back:** "Do you eat your beans raw or cooked?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument ignores dose, preparation and positive effects:

| Compound | Where it's found | What reduces it | Real effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lectins | Raw beans and grains | Cooking | Practically eliminated by cooking |
| Phytates | Grains, legumes, seeds | Soaking, sprouting, fermenting | Slightly reduce mineral absorption; linked to antioxidant effects |
| Oxalates | Spinach, beetroot | Cooking, variety | Relevant only for people prone to kidney stones |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Dose and preparation matter.** Lectins are practically eliminated by proper cooking. Phytates and oxalates are reduced by simple techniques.

**2. Many antinutrients bring benefits.** The Harvard School of Public Health's nutrition page summarizes studies indicating that phytates, polyphenols and other compounds may have protective effects, and that the foods containing them are linked to lower disease risk.

**3. Foods with antinutrients are healthy.** Legumes, whole grains and vegetables are among the foods most linked to good health and longevity in large studies.

**4. Animal foods also contain problematic compounds.** Processed meat is carcinogenic, and red meat, probably carcinogenic (G17).

**5. Plants aren't "poison".** That a plant produces defensive compounds doesn't mean it's toxic to those who prepare and eat it in normal amounts.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Harvard T. H. Chan School of Public Health, antinutrients](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/anti-nutrients/) | Antinutrients are reduced by preparation and many have beneficial effects; the foods that contain them are healthy | A reference page on nutrition |
| [Seidelmann et al., 2018, *Lancet Public Health*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30122560/) | Replacing carbohydrates with plant sources was linked to lower mortality | Also used in G16 |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: some people are sensitive

The most serious version grants that, for most people, antinutrients aren't a problem, but points out that some people have sensitivities, such as intolerances or a tendency to kidney stones.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Sensitivities call for adjustments, not meat.** People prone to stones can cut back on high-oxalate foods.
2. **These are specific cases.** They don't justify conclusions for everyone.
3. **Variety solves it.** A varied plant diet doesn't depend on any one food.

### (f) Back to the moral question

If antinutrients are controlled by preparation and the foods that contain them are healthy, the argument doesn't justify eating animals. The question returns: what justifies their suffering when there are safe, nutritious plant foods?

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in G8 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Ignoring the dose | Treats the presence of a compound as proof of harm |
| Epistemological | Not knowing food preparation | Ignores that cooking eliminates lectins |
| Epistemological | Cherry picking | Highlights the compounds and ignores the foods' benefits |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Eats cooked beans.** And feels no effect from the lectins.
2. **Consumes coffee, chocolate and wine.** Which also contain plant defense compounds.
3. **The animals they eat are fed on plants.** Grain and soy in their feed.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a food contains a potentially harmful compound, it should be avoided".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Processed meat | It should be avoided, because it's carcinogenic | Those who use the argument don't conclude this |
| Coffee | It should be avoided, because it has defense compounds | No one concludes this |
| Potatoes | They should be avoided, because they contain solanine | No one concludes this |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that plants produce defense compounds and that some of them can reduce mineral absorption or cause discomfort. Proper preparation matters, and people with specific conditions should take care.

But dose and preparation turn what would be a problem into a detail. Foods rich in antinutrients are among the healthiest there are.
