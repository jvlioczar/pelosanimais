# I4. "Sheep need to be shorn"

**Thesis:** wool sheep need shearing because they were bred to produce excess wool; their wild ancestors shed their coats naturally. The need was created by the industry itself. And wool production includes practices such as mulesing, cutting away skin from lambs without anesthesia, still legal in Australia, the largest producer of merino wool, and slaughtering sheep when production drops.

**Family:** I. Other uses: testing, clothing, entertainment · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "Without shearing, sheep die of heat. Wool is natural and sustainable."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Wool sheep don't shed their fleece on their own.
2. **P2.** Without shearing, they suffer from heat, dirt and infestations.
3. **P3.** Shearing relieves that suffering.
4. **C.** So using wool is making use of necessary care for sheep.

P1, P2 and P3 are true for today's wool sheep. The problem lies in the conclusion: the need was created by breeding for wool, and the industry includes practices that cause suffering.

The argument is **valid** for existing sheep. Its **soundness** fails because it leaves out where the need came from and what happens in production.

## 3. Answer for children

Long ago, sheep shed their coats on their own, like dogs in spring. People chose sheep with more and more wool, until they couldn't shed it anymore. Now they need to be shorn. That isn't a favor: it's a problem people created.

## 4. Short answer

Wool sheep need shearing because they were bred to produce excess wool; their ancestors shed naturally. It's the same pattern as dairy cows (C10). Besides, wool production includes practices such as mulesing, cutting skin from lambs' hindquarters, still legal and often done without anesthesia in Australia, and slaughtering sheep when they produce less.

**Question to ask back:** "If sheep were bred not to shed their wool, is shearing a favor or a fix for a problem we created?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument starts in the middle of the story:

| What the argument shows | What comes before |
| --- | --- |
| The sheep needs shearing | It was bred not to shed its wool |
| Shearing relieves the heat | Excess wool causes the heat |
| The wool is put to use | It's sold, and that's the point of raising them |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. The need was created.** Primitive and wild sheep shed their coats naturally. Continuously growing wool is the result of human breeding.

**2. Mulesing still exists.** Mulesing removes wrinkled skin from lambs' hindquarters to reduce flystrike. In Australia it's still legal, and pain relief is only mandatory in some states. The RSPCA in Australia itself calls for it to end.

**3. Commercial shearing can be violent.** Shearing is done at speed, often paid per animal, and investigations have recorded injuries and abuse.

**4. The sheep are slaughtered.** When wool production drops, sheep are sold for slaughter. Wool and meat are part of the same system.

**5. There are alternatives.** Cotton, linen, hemp, recycled fibers and other materials keep people warm and clothed without involving animals.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [RSPCA Australia, mulesing](https://www.rspca.org.au/key-issues/sheep-mulesing/) | Mulesing is still legal in Australia; the RSPCA advocates a transition to flystrike-resistant sheep and an end to the practice | An animal welfare organization |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: what about the sheep that already exist?

The most serious version grants that the need was created, but says existing sheep need shearing, and using their wool harms no one.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Caring for existing sheep doesn't require buying wool.** Sanctuaries shear sheep and don't sell the wool to fund new breeding.
2. **Buying wool funds new sheep.** Demand sustains breeding, selection and slaughter.
3. **Commercial wool comes from the industry.** Not from rescued sheep.

### (f) Back to the moral question

Shearing is necessary because we created sheep who depend on it. The moral question isn't whether we should care for the sheep that exist, but whether we should keep breeding and killing animals to clothe ourselves, when there are alternatives.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in I4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Omitting the cause | Presents the need without its origin |
| Logical | Appeal to nature | Calls "natural" an animal shaped by breeding |
| Moral | Exploitation presented as care | Shearing becomes a favor |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Knows the wool is sold.** Which shows the goal isn't only the sheep's good.
2. **Ignores mulesing and slaughter.** Which are part of production.
3. **Uses other fibers without a problem.** Cotton and synthetics.

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if we created a need, meeting it is a favor".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dairy cows | Milking would be a favor | This is the argument of C10 |
| Dog breeds with breathing problems | Surgeries would be favors, not consequences of breeding | Many vets criticize this breeding |
| Sheep | Shearing would be a favor | The need was created |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that today's wool sheep need shearing, and that not shearing them would cause suffering. Some farmers treat their sheep well, and careful shearing isn't cruel in itself.

But the need was created by breeding for profit, and the wool industry includes mulesing and slaughter. Caring for the sheep that exist is different from continuing to breed new ones to clothe ourselves.
