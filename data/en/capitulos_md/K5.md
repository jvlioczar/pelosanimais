# K5. "I'll wait for cultivated meat"

**Thesis:** the argument postpones the obligation until it's painless. Cultivated meat may one day help a great deal, but it still faces major challenges of cost and scale: a 2021 analysis concluded that, under the conditions studied, it would be hard to make it affordable as food, and other analyses are more optimistic. Meanwhile, billions of animals keep being killed. Waiting for a future solution doesn't justify causing harm in the present, when alternatives already exist.

**Family:** K. Effectiveness and individual responsibility · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "In ten years technology will fix it. When it's cheap, I'll switch."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Cultivated meat can offer meat without raising and killing animals.
2. **P2.** When it's available and cheap, many people will switch effortlessly.
3. **P3.** It's more effective to wait for a technological solution than to demand individual sacrifices.
4. **C.** So I can keep eating meat until then.

P1 is true in principle. P2 is plausible. The problem lies in the conclusion: the date is uncertain, and the harm continues while we wait.

The argument is **valid** if waiting had no cost. Its **soundness** fails because the cost is the lives of the animals killed in the meantime.

## 3. Answer for children

Some people are trying to make meat in a lab, without needing to kill animals. That could be really good in the future. But it will still take a while, and meanwhile many animals keep suffering. Today there are already lots of tasty foods that don't need animals.

## 4. Short answer

Cultivated meat could help a lot in the future, but it still faces major challenges of cost and scale, and no one knows when it will be widely available. Meanwhile, billions of animals keep being killed. Waiting for a future solution doesn't justify causing harm in the present, when alternatives exist today: beans, lentils, tofu and many other foods.

**Question to ask back:** "If cultivated meat takes 20 years, how many animals will have died while you waited?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument postpones change to an uncertain future:

| Moment | Situation |
| --- | --- |
| Today | Cheap plant alternatives are available |
| Uncertain future | Cultivated meat may become affordable |
| Meanwhile | Animals keep being raised and killed |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. The date is uncertain.** Cultivated meat was approved for sale in Singapore in 2020 and in the United States in 2023, but it's still produced on a small scale, and there's serious debate about when, or whether, it will be cheap.

**2. There are challenges of cost and scale.** A 2021 techno-economic analysis by David Humbird concluded that production costs, under the conditions studied, would hardly allow an affordable food. Other analyses, from the industry itself, are more optimistic.

**3. The harm continues while we wait.** Each year of waiting means billions of animals killed.

**4. Alternatives already exist.** No waiting is needed to stop eating animals.

**5. It postpones the obligation until it's painless.** If causing the harm is wrong, future convenience doesn't make it acceptable in the present.

**6. "It isn't natural" isn't a moral objection.** Being natural doesn't tell us whether something is good (C1). What matters is whether cultivated meat harms animals. The real problems, such as the use of fetal bovine serum in early processes, are technical and are being replaced by animal-free growth media. Whether cultivated meat is vegan is an open debate; see [The definition of veganism, debated](../alem/definicao-de-veganismo.html).

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: technology is what changes the world

The most serious version says mass change rarely comes from individual moral choices, but from technologies that make the alternative cheaper and more convenient.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **That may be true at the level of society.** But it doesn't excuse the individual who can already change.
2. **Demand for alternatives speeds up the technology.** Those who change today help create tomorrow's market.
3. **The duty doesn't wait.** The animal who dies today doesn't benefit from future technology.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The argument admits that killing animals is a problem, since it waits for a solution. If it's a problem, the question is why keep causing it while the alternatives already exist.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in K5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Indefinite postponement | The duty is moved to an uncertain future |
| Epistemological | Technological optimism | Assumes the solution will come soon and cheap |
| Moral | Convenience as a condition | Would only change when it's painless |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Admits killing animals is a problem.** By waiting for a solution.
2. **Already has alternatives.** And doesn't use them.
3. **Wouldn't wait for a technology to stop other harms.** If they were serious for them.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains K5 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present bias | The cost of changing today weighs more than the future benefit | General literature on temporal discounting |
| Optimism bias | The technological solution seems near and certain | General literature on optimism bias |
| Pro-innovation bias | Cultivated meat is treated as a guaranteed solution, without counting its limits of cost, scale and timing | [Senciência, 2024 (in Portuguese)](https://senciencia.org/2024/04/22/vieses-que-contribuem-para-a-negligencia-do-longo-prazo-e-dos-riscos-s/) |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "I can keep causing a harm until a technology makes the alternative painless".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pollution | I can pollute until technology fixes it | No one accepts this |
| Slave labor | I can buy its products until automation fixes it | No one accepts this |
| Meat | I can eat it until cultivated meat arrives | The same error |

## 11. What the argument gets right

Cultivated meat and other technologies could profoundly transform food production, and it's reasonable to bet on them. For many people, change will be easier when there are identical, cheap alternatives.

But future technology doesn't change what happens today. Until it arrives, plant alternatives already exist, and every animal spared now matters.
