# I22. "Racehorses and sporting dogs live better than many people"

**Thesis:** expensive care is not the same as a good life for the animal. Racehorses and greyhounds are well fed and looked after while they earn, because they are investments; when they get injured or stop winning, the care ends. In Australia, the Coalition for the Protection of Racehorses recorded 168 horses killed on racetracks in the 2022/23 season, one every two days. And comparing them with human poverty justifies nothing: poverty is an injustice to fix, not a yardstick for what may be done to animals.

**Family:** I. Other uses: testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companionship · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "A racehorse lives better than plenty of people: vets, good food, a clean stable. Nobody mistreats an animal worth millions."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Sporting horses and dogs get high-quality food, veterinary care and shelter.
2. **P2.** Many humans don't get that level of care.
3. **P3.** Whoever gets that care has a good life.
4. **C.** So using animals in sport does them no harm; they live better than many people.

P1 is true while the animal earns. So is P2. P3 fails: the care serves performance, and the conclusion ignores the risks, the injuries and what happens after the career.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P3, and the comparison in P2 is irrelevant to the conclusion.

## 3. Answer for children

A racehorse gets good food and a doctor while it runs fast. But it didn't choose to race, it can get badly hurt on the track, and when it stops winning there's often no one left to look after it. Being treated well only while you're useful isn't the same as being loved.

## 4. Short answer

The expensive care lasts while the animal earns: it's an investment. On the track, the risks are real: in Australia, a group that monitors the industry recorded 168 horses killed in races in a single season, one every two days. When they get injured or stop winning, many are discarded. And the comparison with human poverty justifies nothing: people in poverty suffer an injustice that should be corrected, not a measure of what may be done to animals.

**Question to ask back:** "If the care is out of love for the animal, what happens to it when it stops winning?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument only looks at the period when the animal is useful:

| Stage | What the animal lives through |
| --- | --- |
| Breeding | Selective breeding for speed; many animals are born and few get to race |
| Career | Intense training, transport, competition, risk of fractures and other injuries |
| Serious injury | Often euthanasia on the track itself |
| End of career | Some are adopted; others are sold, used for breeding or slaughtered |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Care is proportional to value.** A valuable animal is well cared for for the same reason a racing car is well maintained: it earns. The care doesn't come from recognizing the animal's interests, and it ends when the earnings end.

**2. Racing kills.** The Coalition for the Protection of Racehorses, which monitors Australian tracks in the absence of published official data, recorded 168 horse deaths in races in the 2022/23 season, the highest since the count began in 2014. The figures don't include deaths in training or the fate of retired horses.

**3. Governments recognize the problem.** New Zealand passed, with support from several parties, the end of greyhound racing, citing animal welfare; the last commercial race was held in July 2026.

**4. The comparison with humans is irrelevant.** That many people live in poverty is an injustice to be fought, not a standard for what may be done to others. "Others have it worse" has never justified a harm (J16).

**5. The animal didn't choose.** Even a well-treated animal was bred, trained and put at risk for other people's profit and entertainment.

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: the industry has welfare rules and adoption programs

The most serious version acknowledges the risks and points out that there are rules on whip use, veterinary checks and programs to rehabilitate and rehome retired animals, which improve every year.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Improving isn't making acceptable.** The rules reduce part of the harm, but the activity still requires animals to take risks for entertainment and betting.
2. **The programs don't reach all of them.** The number of animals bred to compete exceeds the capacity for adoption.
3. **The question remains.** Why breed animals to run that risk, if the goal is entertainment and betting?

### (f) Back to the moral question

The question isn't whether the animal eats well while it races, but whether it's fair to breed it, risk its life and discard it for entertainment and profit. Good care during use doesn't change the fact that it is being used.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in I22 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Irrelevant comparison | Human poverty is used as a yardstick for how animals are treated |
| Epistemological | Selective sample | It looks only at animals at their peak and ignores injuries and discarding |
| Moral | Instrumental care as respect | Treating it well while it earns is confused with taking the animal into account |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Says the animal is treated like royalty.** And accepts that it's put down on the track when injured.
2. **Uses human poverty as an argument.** Without proposing anything to fix it.
3. **Would reject the same with people.** A well-paid athlete discarded after an injury would be a victim of abuse.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains I22 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Survivorship bias | Only the animals at their peak are visible | General literature on selection bias |
| Motivated reasoning | Entertainment and betting make the harm invisible | General literature on motivated cognition |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if the animal gets expensive care while it's used, the use is acceptable".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cockfights with well-fed roosters | They would be acceptable | They are illegal in many countries |
| Well-fed circus animals | They would be acceptable | Many countries have banned wild animals in circuses |
| People discarded once no longer useful | It would be acceptable, if they were treated well before | No one accepts that |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It's true that many sporting horses and dogs get high-quality care, and that many people who work with them like the animals. It's also true that some people live in worse conditions, and that this is unacceptable.

But the two facts don't lead to the conclusion: the care depends on earnings, the risk is real, and human poverty calls for justice for people, not a license to use animals.
