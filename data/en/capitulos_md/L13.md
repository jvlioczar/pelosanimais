# L13. "Vegans shouldn't use artificial intelligence"

**Thesis:** the accusation bundles three things: that AI harms the environment, that it harms animals, and that it isn't natural. The first has some truth in it and deserves to be taken seriously: data centres, of which AI is one part, used around 1.5% of the world's electricity in 2024, and that use is expected to double by 2030. But veganism isn't a promise of zero impact: it is the refusal to exploit animals "as far as is possible and practicable". The second accusation is vague, and applies to any technology, including the critic's own phone. The third is the appeal to nature, which settles nothing. And there's an irony: of the choices each person makes, the one that weighs most on the environment and on animals is precisely what's on the plate.

**Family:** L. Attacks on veganism and vegans · **Part:** IND · **Note:** this chapter is about the charge of inconsistency. The idea that an AI's opinion settles the moral question is E8

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "A vegan using ChatGPT? AI uses an absurd amount of energy and wrecks the environment. And it isn't even natural. Where's the consistency?"

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Veganism presents itself as a way of reducing harm to the environment and to animals.
2. **P2.** Artificial intelligence consumes a lot of energy and water and depends on mining and infrastructure that harm ecosystems.
3. **P3.** Anyone who uses AI contributes to those harms.
4. **C.** So vegans who use AI are inconsistent, and veganism shouldn't be taken seriously.

P2 is partly true, and the scale is growing. P1 is imprecise: veganism is, above all, the refusal to exploit animals, not a promise of zero environmental impact. The leap to C is the central problem.

The argument is **valid** only in the version "whoever causes any harm is inconsistent". Its **soundness** fails at P1 and at the final leap: using something that has an impact doesn't make inconsistent someone who refuses something else, with a far larger impact and involving the death of animals.

## 3. Answer for children

Almost everything we use takes some energy: lights, the fridge, the phone, the computer. Nobody can live without leaving any mark on the world. Veganism doesn't promise that. It asks something simpler: not to hurt or kill animals when we can avoid it. And of everything each of us chooses every day, what weighs most on the planet and on animals is what goes on our plate.

## 4. Short answer

Veganism doesn't promise zero impact. It promises not to exploit animals when that can be avoided. AI does have an environmental cost that is growing and worth discussing: data centres used around 1.5% of the world's electricity in 2024. But livestock systems account for around 12% of all human-caused greenhouse gas emissions, according to the FAO, and kill billions of animals every year. If the criterion were environmental consistency, it would weigh far more on people who eat meat than on people who use AI. And "it isn't natural" doesn't say whether something is right: the critic's phone isn't natural either.

**Question back:** "If using AI takes away someone's right to defend animals, where does that leave someone who eats meat and uses AI?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The accusation carries three different criticisms, with different weights.

| Criticism | What's true in it | Why it doesn't lead to the conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI harms the environment | Data centres' electricity use is real and growing | Veganism doesn't promise zero impact, and the impact of diet is larger |
| AI harms animals | Mining and infrastructure affect ecosystems, as with any electronics | It applies to every industrial product, including the accuser's |
| AI isn't natural | Nothing | Natural isn't a synonym for good (C1) |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Veganism isn't a promise of zero impact.** The most-used definition speaks of excluding the exploitation of animals "as far as is possible and practicable". Nobody, vegan or not, can live without causing some impact. What veganism refuses is something specific: using and killing animals when there is an alternative.

**2. Scale matters, and it points the other way.** According to the International Energy Agency, data centres, of which AI is only a part, consumed around 1.5% of global electricity in 2024, and are expected to reach just under 3% in 2030. According to the FAO, livestock systems account for around 12% of human-caused greenhouse gas emissions. The two measures aren't identical — one is electricity, the other emissions — but the order of magnitude is clear: if the criterion is environmental consistency, it weighs far more on the plate than on the screen.

**3. The criticism applies to any technology.** Phones, computers, the internet and the power grid itself depend on mining and affect ecosystems. If using technology with an impact took away someone's right to defend animals, nobody could do it, including the critic (L11).

**4. "It isn't natural" settles nothing.** Vaccines, glasses, sanitation, writing and medicine aren't natural either. Whether something is natural doesn't say whether it's right; it's the same mistake as "it's natural to eat meat", applied in reverse (C1).

**5. AI can also help animals.** Researchers already use machine learning to identify and count wild animals in millions of camera-trap images, at a scale that would be impossible by hand. That doesn't erase the cost, but it shows the tool isn't against animals by nature.

**6. Demanding total consistency is a way of not discussing the main point.** Even if a vegan were inconsistent in using AI, that wouldn't show it's acceptable to exploit animals. The accusation shifts the subject from the victim to the advocate (L12).

### (d) The best reply: AI's cost is real and growing

The serious version isn't the charge of inconsistency. It is the observation that AI's energy and water use is growing fast, that much of the use is superfluous, and that people who care about the environment and about animals should use these tools with judgement.

### (e) Why the reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **It's reasonable, and it isn't an argument against veganism.** Using AI with judgement is a recommendation for everyone.
2. **It asks for proportion, and proportion favours veganism.** Anyone who wants to reduce impact has, in their diet, a choice with a far larger effect.
3. **It doesn't touch the moral question.** The central reason for veganism is not exploiting animals, and that doesn't change with whether or not one uses a tool.

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it shows up in L13 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Tu quoque | The vegan's supposed failing becomes an excuse not to discuss the animals |
| Logical | Appeal to nature | "It isn't natural" is treated as a moral argument |
| Logical | False dilemma | Either zero impact, or no right to criticise |
| Epistemic | Scope insensitivity | Treats impacts of different orders of magnitude as equivalent |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **It uses AI, phones and computers.** With the same impacts it points to in the vegan.
2. **It doesn't demand the same of meat eaters.** Although the environmental impact of meat is far larger.
3. **It accepts other "unnatural" technologies.** Medicine, vaccines, sanitation.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it props up L13 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tu quoque | The inconsistency attributed to the other eases one's own choice | Literature on the tu quoque fallacy |
| Appeal to nature | What is natural seems good in itself | Literature on the naturalistic fallacy |
| Scope insensitivity | Small, visible impacts weigh more than large, diffuse ones | [Senciência, 2024](https://senciencia.org/2024/04/22/vieses-que-contribuem-para-a-negligencia-dos-problemas-com-mais-vitimas/) |
| The meat paradox | Pointing out the other's failing reduces discomfort with one's own | [Loughnan, Haslam and Bastian, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20488214/) |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle under test is: "anyone who uses something with an environmental impact can't defend animals or the environment".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Environmentalists | Anyone who uses a computer couldn't defend forests | It would silence the whole environmental movement |
| Doctors | Anyone who uses hospital equipment couldn't defend public health | Confuses taking part in the world with endorsing every harm |
| The critic | Someone who eats meat and uses AI couldn't criticise anyone | The criterion silences its users too |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It gets something right that deserves attention: AI has a real and growing environmental cost, and much of its use is superfluous. People who care about the environment and about animals have reason to use these tools with judgement, and to support demands for efficiency and transparency about energy and water use.

But it doesn't follow that veganism is inconsistent. It never promised zero impact, only not to exploit animals when there's an alternative. And of the choices each person makes, the one that weighs most on the environment and on animals is still what goes on the plate.
