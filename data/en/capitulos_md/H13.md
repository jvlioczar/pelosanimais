# H13. "If meat is sustainable, there's no problem"

**Thesis:** the argument reduces a moral question about victims to an environmental one. Sustainability is about resources, emissions and ecosystems; it isn't about who is raised and killed. Carbon-neutral meat would still come from an animal who wanted to live. The environment is an important reason to eat less meat, but it isn't the main one: the main one is the animal.

**Family:** H. Environment, ecology and agriculture · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "My only issue is deforestation. With carbon-neutral meat, there's no reason to be vegan."

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** The main problems with meat are environmental: emissions, deforestation and water use.
2. **P2.** New technologies and practices can make meat sustainable.
3. **P3.** If meat causes no environmental harm, there's no reason to avoid it.
4. **C.** So with sustainable meat, veganism loses its point.

P2 may be partly true in the future. The problem lies in P1 and P3: they leave out meat's most direct harm, which is to the animal itself.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P1, which ignores the moral question.

## 3. Answer for children

Some people think the only problem with meat is that it pollutes the planet. But cows, pigs and chickens feel fear and pain, and they want to live. Even if meat didn't pollute at all, they'd still be killed. Caring for the planet is important, and so is caring for animals.

## 4. Short answer

Sustainability is about resources and emissions, not about who gets killed. Carbon-neutral meat would still come from an animal raised to die young. The environment is a good reason to eat less meat, but it isn't the only or the main one: the main one is the animal, who feels and wants to live. Solving the climate doesn't solve the moral question.

**Question to ask back:** "If meat were carbon neutral, would the animal suffer any less when it died?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The argument separates meat's harms and considers only some of them:

| Harm | Does sustainability solve it? |
| --- | --- |
| Greenhouse gas emissions | Partly, in the best case |
| Deforestation and land use | Partly |
| Water use and pollution | Partly |
| The animal's suffering and death | No |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. Sustainability doesn't include the animal.** The concept is about resources and ecosystems for future generations, not about the animal's interest in living and not suffering.

**2. The moral question comes first.** Even if meat had no environmental impact, the question would remain: is it fair to kill a sentient being when we don't need to? (A10, A16).

**3. Sustainable meat at scale doesn't exist.** The data show that even the lowest-impact meat emits far more than plant foods (H8).

**4. The argument swaps the main problem.** By reducing everything to the environment, it ignores that veganism began as a position about animals.

**5. Environment and animals often point the same way, but not always.** Eating less meat benefits both. But insect farming, hunting and "sustainable" leather show the two criteria can diverge, and then it's the animal that should carry the weight (H14).

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Our World in Data, less meat or sustainable meat](https://ourworldindata.org/less-meat-or-sustainable-meat) | Even the lowest-impact beef emits more than ten times as much as the highest-impact peas | Based on Poore and Nemecek, 2018 |
| [The Vegan Society, definition of veganism](https://www.vegansociety.com/go-vegan/definition-veganism) | Veganism seeks to exclude exploitation of and cruelty to animals | Shows the center is the animal, not the environment |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: cultivated meat

The most serious version points to meat cultivated in a lab, which doesn't require raising and killing animals, and says that in that case there'd be neither an environmental nor a moral problem.

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **Cultivated meat is something else.** If it doesn't involve raising and killing animals, it isn't what the argument was defending.
2. **The counter-reply agrees with the central point.** It recognizes that the problem is the animal.
3. **Until it exists at scale, the question remains.** The meat people buy today comes from killed animals.

### (f) Back to the moral question

This is the chapter where the return to the moral question is most direct. Sustainability is important, but it doesn't touch the heart of the problem: the animal who feels, wants to live and is killed. Even in a world of sustainable meat, that question would still be waiting for an answer.

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Wouldn't accept the logic for pets.** No one thinks killing a dog is acceptable if it's carbon neutral.
2. **Knows animals feel.** But leaves that out of the argument.
3. **Defends sustainable meat they don't buy.** Most meat consumed isn't sustainable.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains H13 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meat paradox | Focusing on the environment avoids thinking about the animal | [Loughnan, Haslam and Bastian, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20488214/) |
| Functional categorization | The animal is seen as a product, not as someone | [Bratanova, Loughnan and Bastian, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21569805/) |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a practice causes no environmental harm, there's nothing wrong with it".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Animal fighting | It would be acceptable if it didn't pollute | No one accepts this |
| Cruelty to dogs | It would be acceptable if it were carbon neutral | No one accepts this |
| Exploiting people | It would be acceptable if it were sustainable | No one accepts this |

## 11. What the argument gets right

Environmental concerns are legitimate and urgent, and they're among the strongest reasons to eat less meat. It's also true that technologies such as cultivated meat could change a lot.

But the environment isn't the only problem. Even perfectly sustainable meat would still come from someone who felt fear and pain, and didn't want to die.
