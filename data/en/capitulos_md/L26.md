# L26. "Vegans think they're the only ones who really defend animals"

**Thesis:** the complaint points to a real mistake some vegans make: looking down on people who rescue and care for dogs and cats. That work saves lives and deserves respect. But the phrase generalizes, and it mixes up two things: criticizing an inconsistency (protecting some animals and paying for the death of others) and despising people. The first is legitimate and can be done respectfully; the second is a mistake. People who rescue animals already accept the main premise, that animals matter. They are a starting point, not an opponent.

**Family:** L. Attacks on veganism and vegans · **Part:** IND

## 1. Formulation

**Everyday version:** "I've been rescuing dogs for twenty years, I spend money I don't have on food and vet bills, and a vegan comes along and says I don't love animals because I eat meat. So only vegans love animals?"

**Strongest version (steelman):**

1. **P1.** Rescuers and volunteers save, treat and shelter animals, often at great personal cost.
2. **P2.** Some vegans treat that work as worthless because the people doing it eat meat.
3. **P3.** A movement that despises those who help animals in practice is arrogant and doesn't deserve a hearing.
4. **C.** So the vegan critique can be ignored.

P1 is true. P2 is true of some vegans, and it's their mistake. The problem lies in P3 and the conclusion: the bad behavior of some says nothing about the animals who are still being killed, and the question about them still stands.

The argument is **valid**. Its **soundness** fails at P3, which generalizes from a few and swaps assessing the idea for assessing the people who defend it.

## 3. Answer for children

Someone who looks after an abandoned dog is doing a very good thing, and nobody should say otherwise. Vegans want to help animals too, but they also think about pigs, cows, chickens and fish. The two groups can help each other: someone who already loves one animal can learn to care about the others as well.

## 4. Short answer

People who rescue and care for animals do work that saves lives, and looking down on that is a mistake. If a vegan did that to you, they were wrong. The critique that makes sense is a different one, and it isn't against you: pigs, cows and chickens feel as dogs and cats do, and it's hard to protect some while paying for the death of others. You already believe animals matter. The conversation is only about which ones.

**Question to ask back:** "What do you feel when you rescue a mistreated animal? Would a pig or a chicken deserve less?"

## 5. Full answer

### (a) Reconstruction and diagnosis

The phrase bundles three different claims, and each deserves its own answer:

| Claim | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| The work of rescuers has value | Yes, a great deal |
| Some vegans look down on that work | Yes, and that's a mistake |
| So the vegan critique can be ignored | No: the question about the other animals remains |

### (b) Where the argument breaks

**1. It generalizes from a few.** Some vegans do look down on rescuers, and many vegans are rescuers, shelter volunteers and sanctuary carers. Judging all by some is the same mistake as judging all rescuers by one who mistreats animals.

**2. Criticizing an inconsistency isn't despising a person.** Saying "the animals you eat feel like the ones you rescue" is about an act, not about the worth of the person. The guide covers this difference in E13: good people also take part in unjust practices that custom accepts.

**3. The inconsistency is real, and psychology studies it.** Research on the so-called meat paradox shows that people attribute less mind and less moral importance to the animals they eat, and that simply classifying an animal as "food" is enough to make it seem to deserve less care (E11). A series of studies published in 2019 found that people give less moral value to individuals of certain species even after accounting for what they believe about those animals' intelligence and capacity to feel. The line between "pet" and "food animal" lies in custom, not in the animals.

**4. Rescuers already accept the main premise.** Someone who spends the night nursing a dog hit by a car doesn't need to be convinced that animals feel and that their lives matter. All that's left is extending this to other animals. That makes the rescuer the closest conversation partner veganism has.

**5. The scale asks for the conversation to go on.** Our World in Data records, for 2021, about 73.8 billion chickens, 1.4 billion pigs and 332 million cattle slaughtered worldwide. No rescue reaches those animals. That doesn't diminish rescue: it shows there are more animals to protect.

### (c) Data and sources

| Source | What it says | Note |

| [Minson and Monin, 2012](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/12/Minson---Monin,-2011.pdf) | Meat eaters rate vegetarians worse when they imagine being judged by them | Part of the sense of being despised may come from expecting judgment |

### (d) The strongest counter-reply: those who act are worth more than those who demand consistency

The most serious version says: the rescuer gets their hands dirty, spends time and money and saves actual animals. Many vegans only changed what's on their plate and spend their time demanding consistency online. So who does more for animals?

### (e) Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

1. **It's right to value action.** Changing your plate doesn't replace helping animals who need rescue now, and vegans who only make demands should hear that.
2. **The two things don't compete.** Rescuing is helping; no longer eating animals is ceasing to cause harm. One person can do both, and many do.
3. **Comparing merits doesn't answer the question.** Even if the rescuer does more than the vegan, the animals headed for slaughter are still there. The question was never who is the better person.

### (f) Back to the moral question

The dispute over who "really" defends animals is about people, and the animals are left out of it. For the rescued dog, what matters is having been rescued. For the pig, what matters is what happens to the pig. The moral question is about each of these individuals: if a dog's suffering moves us, what justifies ignoring that of an animal who feels in the same way?

## 7. Reasoning errors

| Level | Error | How it appears in L26 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical | Hasty generalization | Judges all vegans by those who look down on rescuers |
| Logical | Ad hominem | The arrogance of some becomes a reason to ignore the idea |
| Epistemological | Confusing criticism of an act with contempt for the person | Any mention of the inconsistency is heard as an insult |
| Moral | Changing the subject | The contest of merits takes the animals' place |

## 8. Contradictions

1. **Criticizes people who don't help animals.** But doesn't accept being questioned about the animals left outside their care.
2. **Knows that a dog feels fear and pain.** And knows the reasons for thinking the same of a pig or a chicken.
3. **Complains about being judged by some vegans.** And judges all vegans by those few.

## 9. Biases

| Bias | How it sustains L26 | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Functional categorization | "Pet" and "food" seem like different kinds of animal | [Bratanova, Loughnan and Bastian, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21569805/) |
| Cognitive dissonance | Loving animals and eating them creates a discomfort that needs an outlet | [Loughnan, Haslam and Bastian, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20488214/) |
| Do-gooder derogation | People who feel judged disparage whoever seems to be judging | [Minson and Monin, 2012](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/12/Minson---Monin,-2011.pdf) |
| Moral licensing | The good done for dogs and cats seems to make up for the rest | Linked to E10 |

## 10. Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if some defenders of a cause look down on those who help in a different way, the cause can be ignored".

| Applied to | What would follow | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food donors criticized by people calling for changes in the law | The fight against hunger could be ignored | No one concludes this |
| Hospital volunteers criticized by public health campaigners | Public health would stop mattering | Absurd |
| Rescuers and vegans | The animals who are slaughtered could be ignored | The same mistake |

## 11. What the argument gets right

It gets a lot right. People who rescue, treat and shelter animals do real, heavy and under-recognized work, and some vegans treat them with contempt, as if only total consistency had any value. That's unfair, and it's also bad strategy: it pushes away exactly the people who already care about animals. No one owns the defense of animals, and nobody needs to be vegan to have done an animal good.

But the value of rescue doesn't answer the question about the animals left outside it. Recognizing what the rescuer does and inviting them to look at the pig, the cow, the chicken and the fish both fit in the same conversation, as long as it's held with respect.
